The one-sentence version. Your ERP is a website (an application) that your team opens in a browser on the office PC or in the app on a phone on site. It stores everything in a secure database, and it turns one data entry — the customer and the quote — into the quote PDF, the project, the purchase orders, the invoice, the reminders and the review request, automatically.
Read the other guide first if the system is new. Filling in your company — identity, tax defaults, catalogue, people, bank, mailbox — is a separate, one-off job with its own document: 🏁 Guía de puesta en marcha / Initial Company Setup, in the same profile menu. This guide assumes that work is done.

How to use the workspace alongside this guide

Everything described here can be done today, with no installation, in your workspace:

  • El recorrido del cliente — a screen of the workspace (Proyectos → Recorrido), not a demo: enter your own project and follow all 13 phases over the same records every other screen uses.
  • Control Tower — the owner's dashboard (pipeline, money, alerts).
  • Horas — the site's day sheet, the office's review, and the month's reconciliation (section 7).
  • Master Data and Financial Data — every record the company runs on, and the live P&L / balance sheet / cash-flow / KPIs.
  • Quote builder — the catalogue-first presupuesto screen.
Section 1

The system at a glance

What you are putting into operation, in plain terms.

What it is

A web application

A private website only your team can log in to. Nothing to install on each computer — it opens in Chrome, Edge or Safari, on desktop and on the phone.

What it does

Runs the whole job

Lead → site visit → estimate → quote → acceptance → project → purchasing → execution → invoice → collection → supplier payment → close → review. One thread.

Where the data lives

A secure database

A PostgreSQL database with row-level security — each company's data is isolated. Backed up daily. You own it and can export it at any time.

What makes it different

A factory, not a fork

Adding a second company or changing tax rules is configuration (data), never re-programming. Legal rules are dated and the reason is saved on each document.

The promises the software keeps for you

  • Enter data once. The accepted quote becomes the project, the invoice and the emails — no re-typing.
  • Money is exact. All amounts are handled in whole cents; VAT is decided by the legal rule and the justification is stored on the invoice (e.g. the reduced 10% renovation rate, art. 91.Uno.2.10º).
  • Invoices are immutable and gap-free. Numbered in sequence and sealed in a chain (SHA-256); a correction is a rectificativa, never an edit.
  • Profit, not just turnover. Budget → committed → actual → revenue → margin → cash, per project.
  • Documents carry your identity. Quotes, invoices and emails all wear the Canei logo and colours.
Honest limits, stated once. The system never sends email and never files anything with anybody. It composes the message, attaches the document and puts it in your mailbox's Drafts folder; a person opens it and presses send. Verifactu/AEAT (legally required from 2027, not before), bank statement import and card payments are separate switch-ons and are listed in section 10.

How a change reaches you

Worth knowing, because it explains why nothing breaks quietly:

  1. A change is made and every automated check runs against it — tests, layer rules, the whole browser suite.
  2. If they pass, two container images are built and published.
  3. A smoke test then starts those exact images against a real database and runs the ERP end to end inside them. Only if that passes is the version marked good.
  4. The server checks for a good version every minute and pulls it. Nothing is pushed to the server, so there is no key to the machine sitting in a build system.
Plain-English takeaway. You are renting one small machine, not a rack. The system updates itself only with versions that have already been proved to start, connect and invoice — and the previous version is one command away if it is ever needed.
Section 3

How it's built (and why that matters to you)

You don't need this to operate the system — but it explains why changes are cheap and safe. The software is built in layers. Knowledge about Spain and about renovations lives in the upper, swappable layers; the core at the bottom knows nothing about any country or trade.

What this buys you

  • A second company = a new configuration file, ready in minutes.
  • A tax-rate change = a dated rule update; old invoices keep their old rule.
  • Your branding & wording are data — no code change to restyle.

How quality is protected

  • Automated checks run on every change (tests, boundaries, linters).
  • A rule forbids Spanish/tax knowledge from leaking into the core.
  • Every change is versioned in GitHub and can be rolled back.

The two costs that decide the margin arrive from different places. Materials and subcontractors come in as documents you book (steps 7–8). Labour arrives as hours typed on a phone, by the person who worked them, on the day they worked — which is why it has a section of its own and a review step before it counts.

Section 6

Go-live: who does what

Three parts, and only the middle one is yours. Part A is done once by whoever installs the system; Part B is the company setup, which has its own guide; Part C is switched on when it starts to pay off.

Part A — Stand up the system (one-time, technical) IT partner

1
Rent the machine and point a name at it
One small Linux server in the EU, and a DNS record. The full procedure is written down in the project's own operations handover — your IT partner works from that, not from this page.
2
Bring up the containers
The application, PostgreSQL 17 and Caddy come up together from one compose file. The database tables are created by a migration container that runs before the app, so there is no manual setup step and no order to remember.
3
Set the public address
The one setting worth naming here: the server has to be told the address the outside world reaches it on. Activation links and invitation emails are built from it — get it wrong and the links point at the inside of the container, which nobody can open.
4
Enable automatic updates and backups
A timer pulls each proven version within a minute of it being published; a nightly job takes an encrypted, off-site copy of the database. Both are switched on once and then left alone.
5
Create the first administrator
One account, for the owner. Everybody else is invited from inside the system afterwards — see the setup guide.

Part B — Make it yours (configuration, no coding) owner / office

This part has its own document. Company identity, tax defaults, the company mailbox, bank accounts, the catalogue of chapters and sub-chapters, your workers and their hourly cost, and who can log in — all of it, in order, with a printable checklist, is in 🏁 Guía de puesta en marcha / Initial Company Setup, in the profile menu next to this guide. Budget about an hour.

Do not issue anything real until its checklist is finished. The system will refuse to issue an invoice while the legally required company fields are empty, which is the behaviour you want — but the catalogue and the workers' costs are not enforced, and a job quoted without them reports a margin that means nothing.

Part C — Optional connections (turn on when ready) later

6
Bank statement import
The cheapest win in cash visibility: upload the statement and match movements against invoices and bills. Nothing to contract — it reads the file your bank already gives you.
7
Card payments
Add Stripe for pay-online links on invoices. Pay-per-use, and not needed to start operating.
8
Verifactu / AEAT
Legally required from 2027. Plan the certification with your gestoría in good time; the invoices the system produces are already sealed and gap-free in the way the regime expects.
Section 7

Hours: from the site to the payroll

Labour is usually the largest cost on a renovation and the one nobody has a document for. So the ERP collects it where it happens — on a phone, by the person who worked it — and puts a review step between the crew's typing and the company's numbers.

Three screens, in the order the work happens

Parte diario — the crew types Revisión — the office corrects & approves Resumen — the owner reads
ScreenWho uses itWhat it is for
Parte diarioForeman, on siteOne day, one tile per worker, showing what has been booked: job, chapter, sub-chapter, kind of hour, hours. It displays; it does not edit. Adding is «Añadir línea»; changing anything is done in Revisión
RevisiónOffice / ownerThe week's entries as a list, filtered by still open / approved / all. Here an entry can be corrected — hours, job, chapter, sub-chapter, date — or deleted, and the week approved
ResumenOwnerDay, week, month or year, for all projects or one: who worked, what it cost, overtime, and the month's reconciliation including wages paid in cash
Mis horasSite workerThe same booking screen, and only their own hours. No amounts, no colleagues, no rest of the ERP
Why the day sheet cannot be edited. A screen that both shows the day and changes it makes an accidental edit indistinguishable from a booking — and the person holding the phone on a scaffold is not the person accountable for the numbers. Booking is one deliberate action («Añadir línea»); correction is somebody else's screen, and it leaves the entry marked as reviewed.

What an entry carries

Four things, and each earns its place:

  • Obra — which job the hours belong to. Without it the hour has no cost centre.
  • Partida and Subpartida — which chapter and, optionally, which line. This is what makes «the tiling ran 40 % over» a sentence you can say.
  • Tipo de hora — normal, overtime, and so on. Overtime is valued at the worker's overtime rate, not the normal one.
  • Horas — in hours and minutes.

Section 7 · continued

The week, and what the numbers are worth

The weekly rhythm

1
Every day, on site
Each worker — or the foreman for the crew — books the day's hours against the job and the chapter. Two minutes. The tile shows what is already there, so a repeat entry is visible rather than duplicated.
2
Once a week, in the office
Open Revisión, filter to still open, and work down. Fix what is wrong — the wrong job, a chapter nobody chose, eleven hours that were nine — then approve. The yellow «Revisar y aprobar N h» button on the summary is the count of what is still waiting.
3
Once a month, with the payroll
The reconciliation block compares what the hours say against what left the company, including wages paid in cash. A difference here is either an unbooked day or a payment nobody recorded — both worth finding on the last day of the month rather than in March.

Reading the summary

Two controls sit above everything and filter everything below them:

  • The project — all projects by default, which is the company view. Choose one and every figure underneath narrows to it. The ★ beside it shows only your favourite jobs in the list.
  • The period — day, week, month or year. Click the date itself to choose which one.

Underneath: hours and cost for the period, who worked (hours, overtime and cost, per person), where the hours went by job and chapter, and the month's reconciliation.

Take it to Excel. The Excel button produces a full report, not a dump: your identity and logo, the period and the scope it was taken under, a block of headline figures, then the hours by job, by job and chapter, by worker, the month's reconciliation, and every individual entry behind them. It is the document to send to the gestoría or to keep with the payroll.
Approved is not the same as paid. Approval says the hours are correct. Paying them is payroll, and cash paid on site is recorded where the cash is — the reconciliation is what brings the two together.

Section 8

Daily operation runbook

The rhythm once you are live.

Every day

  • Capture new leads and book visits.
  • On site: photos + notes on the phone.
  • On site: book the day's hours before leaving.
  • Turn accepted quotes into projects (one click).
  • Book supplier bills as they arrive.
  • Send drafted invoices & reminders after a glance.

Every week

  • Review and approve the week's hours (section 7).
  • Review the Control Tower: pipeline, margins, overdue.
  • Chase overdue invoices (reminders are pre-drafted).
  • Pay supplier bills due this week.
  • Send review requests for finished jobs.

Every month

  • Reconcile hours against the payroll and the cash paid on site.
  • Export the ledger and the hours report for your gestoría.
  • Check margin-by-line-item for lessons on pricing.
  • Confirm the backup ran (section 9).

If something looks wrong

  • Nothing is deleted — invoices are immutable; use a rectificativa.
  • The app can be re-deployed to the previous version in minutes.
  • Contact your IT partner; the data is safe in the database.
Golden rule. Enter each fact once, at the step it first appears. The system carries it forward. If you find yourself re-typing, there is probably a button that already has it.
Section 9

Backups, security & data protection

Backups

  • A nightly job takes an encrypted copy of the database and stores it off the machine.
  • Once a month, take your own copy too — ⤓ Exportar datos in the profile menu — and keep it somewhere the company controls.
  • Twice a year, do a restore drill: confirm a backup actually opens. A backup nobody has restored is a hope, not a backup.

Access & security

  • Each person has their own login; the four permission levels limit what they can do.
  • A site worker's limits are enforced by the server, not by hiding buttons.
  • The database has row-level security — data is isolated per company.
  • Never share the database address or passwords; they belong in the server's own settings, never in email.
  • Turn on 2-factor login on GitHub and on the server provider.

Data protection (GDPR / LOPD)

  • You hold customer names, addresses, photos — treat them as personal data.
  • Only collect what you need; keep a simple record of processing.
  • Be ready to export or delete a customer's data on request.
  • Your hosts (EU regions available) act as processors — keep their DPAs on file.

Legal / fiscal

  • Invoices are gap-free and sealed; VAT rule & justification are stored on each one.
  • Documents render in Spanish (es-ES); a Catalan label set can be switched on.
  • Verifactu is mandatory from 2027 — plan the certification with your gestor in good time.

What has closed since version 1 of this guide

Version 1 carried an audit of what each step still needed. Those inputs are in the screens now, which is why this guide no longer lists them as gaps:

  • Itemised quotes — quantity × unit price per line, with measurements, from the catalogue.
  • Progress valuation and deposits — partial invoicing against the contract, with withholding where it applies.
  • Projects with dates — planned start and end, and the alerts that fire when they slip.
  • Purchasing and the supplier ledger — orders, bills with their number and date, duplicate detection, and cost committed against each chapter.
  • Hours — booked on site, reviewed in the office, valued at each worker's cost and charged to the chapter (section 7).
  • Payments — date and method, cash included, reconciled against the bank.
  • Documents in three languages — the customer's own, whatever language the office works in.
What remains is the table above: connections to other people's systems — the tax authority, the bank, the card processor — each of which needs a credential or a certificate from outside the company, not new software.
Adding a second company is configuration, not a rebuild: a new company file plus its catalogue, ready in well under an hour. That is the whole point of the “factory”.
Reference

Glossary

Application (app)

The website your team logs into to run the business — in a browser, or in the phone app.

Container

A packaged copy of the app, identical everywhere it runs. What gets tested is what gets deployed.

Database / PostgreSQL

Where all data is stored, safely and with backups.

Deploy

Publishing a new version — automatic, and only after it has passed every check. Reversible.

Migration

Creating or updating the database tables. Runs by itself before each new version starts.

Draft

A finished email waiting in your mailbox for a person to press send. The system never sends.

Tenant / configuration

Your company set up as data (name, logo, tax profile) — no coding.

Baseline

The frozen budget copied from the accepted quote; everything is measured against it.

Partida / subpartida

Chapter and line of work. Costs — materials, subcontractors and hours — are charged to them.

Committed vs actual

Committed = ordered (POs); actual = billed by suppliers.

Rectificativa

A correcting invoice — invoices are never edited or deleted.

Verifactu

Spain's anti-fraud invoicing regime, mandatory from 2027.

RLS (row-level security)

Database rule keeping each company's rows isolated.
