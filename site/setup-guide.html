The one-sentence version. Your ERP is a website (an application) that your team opens in a browser on the office PC or a phone on site. It stores everything in a secure database, and it turns one data entry — the customer and the quote — into the quote PDF, the project, the purchase orders, the invoice, the reminders and the review request, automatically.

  • Control Tower — the owner's dashboard (pipeline, money, alerts).
  • Quote demo and Backend — the quoting screen and the API responses.

Section 1

The system at a glance

What you are putting into operation, in plain terms.

What it is

A web application

A private website only your team can log in to. Nothing to install on each computer — it opens in Chrome, Edge or Safari, on desktop and on the phone.

What it does

Runs the whole job

Lead → site visit → estimate → quote → acceptance → project → purchasing → execution → invoice → collection → supplier payment → close → review. One thread.

Where the data lives

A secure database

A PostgreSQL database with row-level security — each company's data is isolated. Backed up daily. You own it and can export it at any time.

What makes it different

A factory, not a fork

Adding a second company or changing tax rules is configuration (data), never re-programming. Legal rules are dated and the reason is saved on each document.

The promises the software keeps for you

  • Enter data once. The accepted quote becomes the project, the invoice and the emails — no re-typing.
  • Money is exact. All amounts are handled in whole cents; VAT is decided by the legal rule and the justification is stored on the invoice (e.g. the reduced 10% renovation rate, art. 91.Uno.2.10º).
  • Invoices are immutable and gap-free. Numbered in sequence and sealed in a chain (SHA-256); a correction is a rectificativa, never an edit.
  • Profit, not just turnover. Budget → committed → actual → revenue → margin → cash, per project.
  • Documents carry your identity. Quotes, invoices and emails all wear the Canei logo and colours.
Honest limits on day one. A few connections need your credentials before they are switched on: real email sending, Verifactu/AEAT (legally required from 2027, not before), bank statement import, and card payments. Until then the software produces the document or draft and logs it — it never sends or files anything on its own. These are listed in section 9.

Section 3

How it's built (and why that matters to you)

You don't need this to operate the system — but it explains why changes are cheap and safe. The software is built in layers. Knowledge about Spain and about renovations lives in the upper, swappable layers; the core at the bottom knows nothing about any country or trade.

What this buys you

  • A second company = a new configuration file, ready in minutes.
  • A tax-rate change = a dated rule update; old invoices keep their old rule.
  • Your branding & wording are data — no code change to restyle.

How quality is protected

  • Automated checks run on every change (tests, boundaries, linters).
  • A rule forbids Spanish/tax knowledge from leaking into the core.
  • Every change is versioned in GitHub and can be rolled back.

Section 6 · the checklist

Go-live: step-by-step setup

Follow these in order. Each step says what to do and why. Where you must copy a setting, it looks like THIS. You do not need to understand the code — you are creating accounts and pasting values. Budget about half a day. If a step feels technical, your IT partner can do Part A once in ~1 hour.

Part A — Stand up the system (one-time, technical)

1
Create the accounts
Go to each site and sign up (email + password): GitHub (github.com — you already have the code here), Vercel (vercel.com, “Continue with GitHub”), and a database at Neon (neon.tech) or Supabase (supabase.com). All have free tiers.
2
Create the database & copy its address
In Neon/Supabase click New project. When it finishes, copy the connection string (starts with postgres://…). Keep it private — it is like a key to your data.
3
Connect the app to GitHub in Vercel
In Vercel: Add New → Project, choose the OpenProject2 repository, framework “Next.js”. Don't deploy yet — first add the settings in step 4.
4
Paste the settings (environment variables)
In Vercel → Project → Settings → Environment Variables, add: DATABASE_URL = the address from step 2; UI_LANG = es (or ca for Catalan, en for English). These tell the app where the data is and which language to show.
5
Create the tables (migrate)
Once, run the database setup: pnpm install then pnpm db:migrate (your IT partner runs this from the code, with the same DATABASE_URL). This builds the empty tables. Nothing to decide — just run it.
6
Deploy
Press Deploy in Vercel. In ~2 minutes you get a web address like canei-erp.vercel.app. Open it — the app is live. (Optional: point erp.caneisubirats.com to it under Vercel → Domains.)
7
Prove it works
From the code, run make demo. It builds a sample quote and two invoices and checks the seals — if it prints green, the engine is healthy. This is the “smoke test”.

Part B — Make it yours (configuration, no coding)

8
Confirm the company details
Check the company file tenants/diorka/tenant.yaml: legal name, CIF, address, logo colours, invoice numbering, IVA/IRPF profile. These are already set to Canei Subirats — verify the CIF and banking once your gestor confirms them.
9
Load your catalogue & suppliers
Enter your usual chapters/items and prices, and your suppliers with their prices. This is what makes quoting “pick, don't type”. Start with your 20 most common items; add more over time.
10
Create the user accounts & roles
Add each team member and give them a role: Owner, Administration or Operations (permissions are already defined). The owner can do everything; operations can capture visits and purchasing but not, say, change tax settings.

Part C — Optional connections (turn on when ready) later

11
Email & Outlook drafts
Provide your Microsoft 365 / SMTP details so emails can be sent and saved to the Outlook Drafts folder. Until then the app still produces the branded email and a downloadable Outlook draft.
12
Payments, bank & tax
Add Stripe for pay-online links; connect the bank for statement import; and, ahead of 2027, complete the Verifactu/AEAT certification with your gestor. Each is a self-contained switch-on and is not needed to start operating.
Section 7

Daily operation runbook

The rhythm once you are live.

Every day

  • Capture new leads and book visits.
  • On site: photos + notes on the phone.
  • Turn accepted quotes into projects (one click).
  • Book supplier bills as they arrive.
  • Send drafted invoices & reminders after a glance.

Every week

  • Review the Control Tower: pipeline, margins, overdue.
  • Chase overdue invoices (reminders are pre-drafted).
  • Pay supplier bills due this week.
  • Send review requests for finished jobs.

Every month

  • Export the ledger for your gestor (CSV).
  • Check margin-by-chapter for lessons on pricing.
  • Confirm the backup ran (section 8).

If something looks wrong

  • Nothing is deleted — invoices are immutable; use a rectificativa.
  • The app can be re-deployed to the previous version in minutes.
  • Contact your IT partner; the data is safe in the database.
Golden rule. Enter each fact once, at the step it first appears. The system carries it forward. If you find yourself re-typing, there is probably a button that already has it.
Section 8

Backups, security & data protection

Backups

  • Managed Postgres (Neon/Supabase) takes automatic daily backups; keep 7–30 days.
  • Once a month, export a full copy (the app supports JSON/CSV export) and store it off-site (e.g. company OneDrive/Drive).
  • Twice a year, do a restore drill: confirm a backup actually opens.

Access & security

  • Each person has their own login; roles limit what they can do.
  • The database has row-level security — data is isolated per company.
  • Never share the DATABASE_URL or passwords; store them in the host's secret settings, not in email.
  • Turn on 2-factor login on GitHub, Vercel and the database.

Data protection (GDPR / LOPD)

  • You hold customer names, addresses, photos — treat them as personal data.
  • Only collect what you need; keep a simple record of processing.
  • Be ready to export or delete a customer's data on request.
  • Your hosts (EU regions available) act as processors — keep their DPAs on file.

Legal / fiscal

  • Invoices are gap-free and sealed; VAT rule & justification are stored on each one.
  • Documents render in Spanish (es-ES); a Catalan label set can be switched on.
  • Verifactu is mandatory from 2027 — plan the certification with your gestor in good time.

Reference

Glossary

Application (app)

The website your team logs into to run the business.

Host / Vercel

The service that runs the app on the internet.

Database / PostgreSQL

Where all data is stored, safely and with backups.

Environment variable

A private setting (like DATABASE_URL) pasted into the host, not into the code.

Deploy

Publishing a new version of the app — one button; reversible.

Migration

Creating/updating the database tables. Run once at setup.

Tenant / configuration

Your company set up as data (name, logo, tax profile) — no coding.

Baseline

The frozen budget copied from the accepted quote; everything is measured against it.

Committed vs actual

Committed = ordered (POs); actual = billed by suppliers.

Rectificativa

A correcting invoice — invoices are never edited or deleted.

Verifactu

Spain's anti-fraud invoicing regime, mandatory from 2027.

RLS (row-level security)

Database rule keeping each company's rows isolated.
