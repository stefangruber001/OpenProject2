# 06 — VALUE PROPOSITION AND QUANTIFIED ROI

This document is Phase 6 of the Barcelona market roadmap (`docs/market/barcelona/ROADMAP.md`), written on **2026-09-13**, and it does one thing: it states, for each of the three segments that `05-TARGET-LIST.md`'s Tier A and Tier B actually fall into, what continuing as they are costs a firm in euros per year, which named capability removes which part of that cost and by how much, how long the money takes to come back, what has to be true for it not to come back inside twenty-four months, the strongest case for refusing to buy at all, and one recommended price. It is written in the tradition of IESE's Pablo Fernández on value creation, which means it refuses to count a feature as a benefit: every figure below is a cash-flow effect on the buyer's own profit and loss, built from named inputs with the arithmetic shown, and every input that has no source behind it is marked as an assumption in bold with a range so that a reader who disagrees can substitute their own number and recompute. Two honesty constraints shape the whole document and are stated here rather than buried. First, the incumbent is not a competing product — it is a quotation pad, a spreadsheet and an external gestoría, whose cash cost does **not** go away when the product is bought, so the product is additive spend justified on value alone, not substitution spend justified on a fee it replaces. Second, the capture rates applied below are deliberately well under 100%, because the reference case's own record (`00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` A.5, and `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.2.7) says plainly that a firm which absorbs extras informally to keep a client happy is a firm whose margin problem this product diagnoses and whose behaviour it does not change.

## 0. Method, and the inputs every segment shares

### 0.1 How a euro figure is built here

Each operational failure below is costed in three steps, always in the same order, so that any line can be attacked at the step where it is weakest rather than in aggregate. **Step one, the physical quantity**: hours, jobs, quotations, extras, invoices, euros of third-party spend — each traced to a sourced figure, to `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md`'s evidence of how the reference customer actually worked, or to a bold assumption with a range. **Step two, the price of that quantity**: a loaded hourly cost, a margin point, a penalty amount with its instrument, or a cost of money. **Step three, the capture rate**: what share of that annual cost a named capability actually removes in the first year, stated as a percentage with a reason. The product of the three is the figure carried into the payback model. Gross annual cost of the failure is reported as well as captured value, because the gap between them is the part of the pitch that is not true yet, and a reader who only sees the captured number cannot audit the claim.

A note on what is deliberately **not** counted. No revenue growth is modelled anywhere in this document — not a higher win rate from faster quotations, not a larger average job from a better-looking proposal, not a price premium from trilingual documents. Those effects are real in the literature and unmeasurable here, and including them would let the payback model be carried by the one term nobody can check. Every euro below is a cost avoided or a cash already earned but not collected. No discount rate is applied either, for the reason `05-TARGET-LIST.md` flagged when it omitted one: the payback periods modelled below all land inside twelve months at central case, and discounting a twelve-month cash flow at any plausible hurdle rate changes the month of payback by zero.

One consequence of the counts above deserves stating before any euro appears: S2 is half the near-term pipeline by firm count and carries the highest revenue per firm, so if this document's recommendations conflict between S1 and S2, S2 wins on weight.

## 1. SEGMENT 1 — FINISHING AND COMPLETION TRADES

**Segment box.** CNAE 43.3x/43.9x, S.L. form, layer L0. Twelve of thirty-seven Tier A+B firms, including four of the seven Tier A design partners (Gonzvilarsa S.L., A Barcelona Reformas SL, Reformas Y Construcciones Montcada SL, Instalaciones Y Reformas De Calidad SL). `01-MARKET-MAP.md` §5 ranks interior fit-out in this family at **95 of 100** on the adjacency score, L0, "everything survives" — the highest score in the whole adjacency map, because this is the reference customer's own trade family. Sizing used below: **assumption, 8–15 employees and €1.0m–€2.5m of revenue, modelled at 11 employees and €1.6m**, the lower-middle of Phase 1's sweet spot; **assumption, 27 jobs a year at an average of €60,000 over 2.5 months**, giving about 5.6 concurrent sites, inside `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.2.3's four-to-twenty band. **Assumption, 55% of revenue passes through subcontractors and material suppliers** (= €880,000), against the ICP's "at least a third of cost" floor. Revenue and job-count ranges: €1.0m–€2.5m, and 18–40 jobs.

### 1.1 Failure one — the margin is decided at purchase time, by hand, and nobody can see what was compared: €16,800 a year

**Method.** Two components, costed separately because they behave differently.

_The labour of comparing._ `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` B.1 documents the reference customer's actual instrument: one spreadsheet of roughly **245 rows and 18 columns**, comparing **four named cost alternatives** against an initial study, with absolute and percentage variance at chapter level and exceptions marked by cell highlighting. **Assumption: a comparable pass costs five hours per job in this segment (range two to twelve hours), split evenly between the administrative owner and the operations owner**, because the prices are gathered by one and judged by the other. Arithmetic: 27 jobs × 5 hours = 135 hours; 67.5 hours × €18 = €1,215 plus 67.5 hours × €35 = €2,363, giving **€3,578, rounded to €3,600 a year**.

_The money lost because the comparison is incomplete._ The same BRD appendix insists that "missing supplier prices must be visible rather than silently treated as a valid zero" and that "the selected cost basis must be explicit, rather than inferred from spreadsheet colour or position" — which is evidence that in the incumbent arrangement both failures occur. **Assumption: 1.5% of third-party spend is overpaid because a comparison was incomplete, a discount was stale, or a price that had expired was used anyway (range 0.5% to 3.0%).** This is the least evidenced number in the segment and it is also the largest, which is stated plainly rather than hidden in a total. Arithmetic: €880,000 × 1.5% = **€13,200 a year**.

**Annual cost of failure one: €3,600 + €13,200 = €16,800. ESTIMATE.** At the bottom of both ranges it is €1,400 + €4,400 = €5,800; at the top, €8,600 + €26,400 = €35,000.

### 1.2 Failure two — what the customer accepted cannot be proved, and extras are absorbed: €20,900 a year

**Method.** BRD pain point **P4**, "inability to prove which scope, price and options the customer accepted", is the sourced failure; `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` A.5 records the reference customer's own insistence that a variation must be a real budget joined to the original by acceptance, and that the screen for it belongs under the commercial area because "an extra is a change to what was sold, negotiated and approved before anyone builds it". A firm with no such document absorbs a share of its extras.

_Extras never invoiced._ **Assumption: 1.5 extras per job, averaging €1,200 each — about 2% of a €60,000 job — of which 35% are never invoiced because there is no accepted variation document to invoice against (ranges: 0.5–3 extras, €600–€2,500, 15%–55% unbilled).** Arithmetic: 27 × 1.5 × €1,200 × 35% = **€17,010, rounded to €17,000 a year**.

_Disputes over scope._ **Assumption: one dispute a year settled by concession at €3,500, plus twelve hours of owner time (range: 0–3 disputes, €1,000–€8,000 each).** Arithmetic: €3,500 + (12 × €35) = **€3,920 a year**.

**Annual cost of failure two: €17,000 + €3,920 = €20,920, rounded to €20,900. ESTIMATE.** Low end €4,900; high end €55,000.

### 1.3 Failure three — the administrative quarter, and the compliance exposure sitting behind it: €9,700 a year

**Method.** Four labour components and two expected-loss components.

_Re-typing._ `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` B.1 states that **quotations were prepared on paper by the operations owner and the back office then re-typed them** — that is BRD pain point P1, data captured more than once, sourced from the customer's own requirements document. At a 1-in-3 win rate, 27 won jobs means 81 quotations. **Assumption: 45 minutes per quotation to re-type, and 1.6 versions per quotation with each revision costing a further 30 minutes.** Arithmetic: 81 × 0.75h = 61h; plus 0.6 × 81 × 0.5h = 24h; 85 hours × €18 = €1,530, taken at **€1,400** after allowing that some quotations are trivial.

_The quarterly reconstruction._ `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md`'s second-ranked urgency states it exactly: "Between the 1st and the 20th of next month somebody in your office will reconstruct a quarter of purchases and sales from paper to get the VAT return out." Modelo 303 is filed from the 1st to the 20th of the month following the quarter (fourth quarter 1–30 January) and modelo 111 within the first twenty calendar days of the same months — both verified against the tax authority's own deadline pages per `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` §5. **Assumption: three days of administrative time and half a day of owner time per quarter (range: one to six days).** Arithmetic: 4 × 24h × €18 = €1,728, plus 4 × 4h × €35 = €560, giving **€2,288, rounded to €2,300**.

_The annual counterparty sort._ Modelo 347, the declaration of operations with third parties, is filed during February for the previous calendar year (approved by Orden EHA/3012/2008, BOE-A-2008-16973; the €3,005.06 counterparty threshold conventionally applied is **UNVERIFIED** per `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` §5). `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` describes counterparty totals by tax identifier across the calendar year as "exactly what a spreadsheet-and-paper firm cannot produce without a week of sorting". **Assumption: 40 hours.** Arithmetic: 40 × €18 = **€720**.

_Gestoría re-work._ **Assumption: €150 per quarter of additional fee for incomplete or late records (range €0–€400).** Arithmetic: 4 × €150 = **€600**.

_Verifactu expected loss._ `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` §1 is the best-cited section in the roadmap: Ley 11/2021 (BOE-A-2021-11473) inserted art. 29.2.j) and art. 201 bis into the Ley General Tributaria; RD 1007/2023 (BOE-A-2023-24840) fills it in; Orden HAC/1177/2024 (BOE-A-2024-22138) develops it; the date moved from 1 July 2025 to 1 January 2026 by RD 254/2025 (BOE-A-2025-6600) and then to **1 January 2027** for corporate income tax payers by art. 3 of RD-ley 15/2025 (BOE-A-2025-24446), ratified by the Congress of Deputies (BOE-A-2025-25695). The **user's** exposure under art. 201 bis LGT is a fixed fine of **€50,000 per financial year** for holding systems that are not duly certified when certification is required. Every firm in S1 is an S.L. and therefore inside art. 3.1.a), so the date is 1 January 2027 — **110 days from today**. **Assumption: a 4% annual probability of the exposure crystallising for a firm still issuing invoices from a spreadsheet after that date (range 1%–15%). The penalty amount is sourced; the probability is not.** Arithmetic: €50,000 × 4% = **€2,000 a year**.

_Reduced-rate re-rating._ `LEGAL_REVIEW.md` §2, via `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` A.7, records that the dwelling-renovation reduced rate turns on three cumulative conditions and that one failed condition re-rates the whole invoice; `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` §5 calls this "the expensive error" for this sector. **Assumption: 30% of revenue is reduced-rate dwelling renovation, 5% of it is wrongly rated or — which costs the same on inspection — correctly rated with no recorded justification (range 1%–12%).** Arithmetic: €1,600,000 × 30% × 5% × 11 points = **€2,640 a year**.

**Annual cost of failure three: €1,400 + €2,300 + €720 + €600 + €2,000 + €2,640 = €9,660, rounded to €9,700. ESTIMATE.**

**Segment 1, gross annual cost of the three failures: €16,800 + €20,900 + €9,700 = €47,400.**

### 1.5 The payback model, and what has to be true for payback to exceed 24 months

**Cost to the buyer, year one.** Subscription €300 a month (see §1.7) = €3,600. One-time onboarding €1,200, charged once — **assumption**, to be replaced by Phase 7's measured tailoring cost at L0; Phase 7's mandate is an engineering-hours and calendar estimate per layer, and this figure is a placeholder priced at what the market will read as credible rather than at what it costs to deliver. Internal adoption effort: **assumption, 45 administrative hours and 12 owner hours across the first quarter** = €810 + €420 = **€1,230**, counted as a real cost because it is the buyer's own time and leaving it out is how payback models flatter themselves.

**Central case.** Cumulative cost crosses cumulative captured value in **month 6** (cumulative cost €4,230 against cumulative value €4,512). Under the slow adoption ramp, **month 10**. First-year net effect at central case: €23,550 × the ramp (about 61% of a full year) = €14,400 of value against €6,030 of cost, a net **+€8,370** in year one and **+€19,950** in year two.

**What has to be true for payback to exceed 24 months in Segment 1: captured value must fall to about 24% of central, or roughly €5,650 a year.** That is not a vague pessimism; it is one specific and nameable world, and all three of these have to hold at once: (a) **extras are already billed** — the owner loses nothing today by absorbing them because he does not absorb them, which removes €8,500, the largest single line; (b) **supplier choice is not actually contestable** — one preferred merchant on an annual discount, so the comparison asset is worth close to nothing, removing a further €4,620; and (c) **the gestoría absorbs the quarter and the 2027 billing regime** — the adviser provides a conforming billing tool at no incremental cost, removing most of €6,705. A firm for which all three hold is a firm this segment should not be sold to, and each one is a qualifying question that can be asked on a first call. The practical reading is that payback in this segment is robust to any one of the three failing and fragile to all three failing together.

### 1.6 The strongest argument against buying

The strongest case against is not a competitor. It is this: **roughly 43% of the modelled value (€10,068 of €23,553) depends on the owner changing a commercial habit, and nothing in the product compels that change.** The variation screen makes an extra easy to raise; it does not make the owner raise it in front of a customer he will want again next year. `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.2.7 says so in its own words, and `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` A.5 records that the reference customer already had the reflex before buying. A buyer entitled to be sceptical would say: you have priced my self-discipline and called it your product.

Five further arguments, each with its own weight:

1. **Keep using Excel and a gestoría — costed in §4 below — is cash-neutral.** The gestoría fee does not fall. The incumbent's cash cost is mostly unavoidable either way, so this is €4,830 of genuinely new spend in year one against value that is entirely internal and therefore entirely arguable.
2. **The 2027 trigger is not yet discharged by the product.** `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` §1 sets out exactly what RD 1007/2023 and Orden HAC/1177/2024 oblige — billing and cancellation records, hash chaining, electronic signature in the non-verifiable mode, the QR code and legend, the event log, the producer's declaración responsable — and `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` A.7 confirms the repository holds immutability, gapless numbering, a rectification path and the hash chain, and **not** the official record layout, the QR content, the event log or the submission channel. Until it does, the honest answer to "will this make me compliant on 1 January 2027" is "not today", and a gestoría saying "we will give you a billing tool" is making a cheaper promise.
3. **One reference customer, and every legal rule stamped unverified.** `LEGAL_REVIEW.md` carries `legally_verified: false` on every rule pending a human adviser. A prudent buyer discounts the compliance half of the pitch until that stamp changes.
4. **The Catalan asset is machine-authored.** `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` C.3 records that the Catalan dictionary awaits a native reviewer, and that this is "a disclosure to make before a Catalan-speaking prospect reads a contract, not after". For a firm whose binding customer documents must be in Catalan, that is a reason to wait.
5. **Nineteen of twenty-seven areas are still in the hand-written engine.** `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` A.16 says so. A buyer is being sold a factory output whose factory is half-built.

**Verdict on the segment: beaten, not conceded.** At central case payback is six months and the breach threshold is a 76% shortfall in captured value — a wide margin. But the rebuttal has to be specific rather than rhetorical: the pitch should lead with the extras and the scope evidence, qualify hard on the three breach conditions in §1.5, and price the compliance half at zero until the Verifactu build lands.

### 1.7 Pricing hypothesis — Segment 1

**Recommended model: flat, per legal entity, banded by headcount. €300 a month, €3,600 a year, all named users and all projects included. Planning ACV for Phase 7: €3,600.**

Justification against how this segment already buys. It buys its gestoría as a **flat monthly retainer** — **assumption, €250 to €450 a month for an S.L. with payroll and quarterly filings; no source, and it should be asked on the first call** — and it buys its prevention service, its insurance and its telephony the same way: one number, once a month, no counting. A model that asks this buyer to count something introduces a negotiation every month where the flat model has none. Per-seat is rejected for a specific reason rather than a general one: the office is two or three people, so a per-seat price high enough to reach €3,600 would be €100 a seat, which then prices the crew-facing surface out of use — and that surface is the one with **no money figures in it at all because they are removed server-side** (`00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` A.10), so charging per crew member taxes the adoption the product most needs. Per-project is rejected because it gives the buyer a standing reason to keep small jobs out of the system, which destroys the single most valuable property in the asset, "one record of truth, no second copy anywhere" (`00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` C.2). Per-site is rejected here because at 5.6 concurrent sites a per-site price tracking this value would land at about €336 a month — within a rounding error of the flat figure, for more complexity.

The €3,600 figure is anchored, not invented. `01-MARKET-MAP.md` §3.6 and `05-TARGET-LIST.md` Table 1 both use €3,600 for the 3–9 band as 60% of the **Kit Digital Segment II ceiling of €6,000**, which `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` §6 describes as "the one public number stating what a Spanish SME of each size has been given permission to spend on software" — while also recording that **there is no open Kit Digital call today**, that the subsidy is therefore an anchor and not a trigger, and that the per-segment amounts themselves are **UNVERIFIED** against the bases' annexes. Against the modelled €23,550 of captured value, €3,600 is a 6.5:1 value-to-price ratio, which leaves room to discount for a design partner without inverting the economics. Two price mechanics to carry into Phase 8: the onboarding fee should be quoted separately, because `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` §6 notes the open Catalan ACCIÓ vouchers fund **advisory services** rather than licences, and a separately quoted configuration engagement is the half a voucher can pay for; and a second sibling entity under the same owners should be priced at 50%, because `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.1 observes that two S.L.s under the same two owners is "a two-tenant sale at nearly one tenant's delivery cost".

## 2. SEGMENT 2 — RENOVATION-LED GENERAL BUILDING CONTRACTORS

**Segment box.** CNAE division 41 (41.20 construction of buildings, plus the malformed 41.00/41.01/41.02/41 rows `05-TARGET-LIST.md` reads as division 41 on the ground that the real subclass is 41.20), S.L. form, layer L0. Nineteen of thirty-seven Tier A+B firms — **the largest segment, 51% of the near-term pipeline** — including three of seven Tier A design partners (Edificaciones Y Reformas Mollet 2000 SL, Delgado & Dilla Construccions I Reformes Integrals SL, Reformas Lluca Les Corts SL). `01-MARKET-MAP.md` §2 scores 41.2 "strong, with a caveat": new-build housing contractors fit the baseline and valuation spine exactly, and the 18-chapter taxonomy is renovation-shaped, which is pack data rather than code; §5 ranks restoration and rehabilitación at **88 of 100**, L0–L1. Ranges: €2.0m–€8.0m of revenue, 14–32 jobs, 6–16 concurrent sites.

### 2.1 Failure one — margin decided blind on €2.4m of third-party spend: €40,900 a year

**Method.** Identical in shape to §1.1, larger in every quantity, and the overpayment term now dominates completely.

_The labour of comparing._ Eighteen chapters rather than four, and more subcontract packages per job. **Assumption: nine hours per job (range four to twenty), split 60% administrative and 40% owner.** Arithmetic: 22 × 9 = 198 hours; 119h × €18 = €2,142 plus 79h × €35 = €2,765 = **€4,907, rounded to €4,900 a year**.

_The money lost because the comparison is incomplete._ **Assumption: the same 1.5% of third-party spend (range 0.5%–3.0%).** Arithmetic: €2,400,000 × 1.5% = **€36,000 a year**. The range is €12,000 to €72,000, and the width of it is the honest statement about this line: it is simultaneously the biggest number in the document and the least evidenced, and the first thing a Tier A design partner should be asked to measure is its own chapter-level price variance over one completed job.

**Annual cost of failure one: €4,900 + €36,000 = €40,900. ESTIMATE.**

### 2.2 Failure two — extras absorbed and executed work left un-valued: €61,500 a year

**Method.** Two components. The first is §1.2's extras at this segment's job size; the second is new to this segment and does not exist in S1 or S3 at any material size.

_Extras never invoiced._ **Assumption: 2.5 extras per job averaging €3,000 — about 1.7% of a €180,000 job, a lower percentage than S1 because a general builder's extras are chapter-scale rather than item-scale — of which 30% are never invoiced (ranges: 1–5 extras, €1,500–€6,000, 15%–45%).** Arithmetic: 22 × 2.5 × €3,000 × 30% = **€49,500 a year**.

_Executed work never valued._ This is the progress-valuation failure, and it only appears once jobs run for months. `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` A.6 states the computation: the engine proposes a valuation as **executed minus already billed**. Where no such computation exists, work executed in month four of a five-month job is billed in month five if someone remembers the measurement, and partly not at all if nobody does. **Assumption: 1.5% of annual revenue sits executed-but-unvalued at any moment, and 20% of that is never recovered at all (ranges: 0.5%–4% and 10%–40%).** Arithmetic: €4,000,000 × 1.5% × 20% = **€12,000 a year**. The timing cost of the other 80% is counted in §2.3 as working capital, not here, so it is not double-counted.

**Annual cost of failure two: €49,500 + €12,000 = €61,500. ESTIMATE.** This is the largest single failure in the largest segment, and it is the one the product addresses most directly.

### 2.3 Failure three — the administrative quarter, the subcontracting paperwork and the receivables ledger nobody owns: €21,400 a year

**Method.** Nine components: four of labour, two of working capital, three of expected regulatory loss.

_Re-typing._ At a 1-in-2.5 win rate, 22 won jobs means 55 quotations, each larger. **Assumption: 1.2 hours per quotation plus 0.8 further versions at 0.8 hours.** Arithmetic: (55 × 1.2) + (0.8 × 55 × 0.8) = 101 hours × €18 = **€1,818, rounded to €1,800**.

_The quarterly reconstruction._ **Assumption: five days of administrative time and one day of owner time per quarter (range: two to ten days).** Arithmetic: 4 × 40h × €18 = €2,880, plus 4 × 8h × €35 = €1,120 = **€4,000 a year**.

_The annual counterparty sort._ **Assumption: 60 hours for modelo 347 at this transaction volume.** Arithmetic: 60 × €18 = **€1,080**.

_Gestoría re-work._ **Assumption: €300 a quarter.** Arithmetic: **€1,200 a year**.

_Avoidable days sales outstanding._ `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md`'s non-regulatory trigger table names working-capital stress directly: "retentions plus long customer terms against shorter supplier terms, with nobody owning the receivables ledger". **Assumption: 15 days of avoidable delay on invoices issued late or chased late, at a 6% cost of money (range 5–30 days).** Arithmetic: €4,000,000 × (15 ÷ 365) × 6% = **€986, rounded to €1,000 a year**. This is deliberately small: financing fifteen days of a €4m turnover is cheap, and claiming otherwise is the standard way receivables benefits get inflated.

_Avoidable bad debt._ **Assumption: one avoidable write-off of €9,000 every three years, avoidable in the sense that earlier visibility of an overdue balance against an unsigned variation would have stopped work sooner (range: €0 to €30,000 every three years).** Arithmetic: €9,000 ÷ 3 = **€3,000 a year**.

_Verifactu expected loss._ As §1.3 — art. 201 bis LGT, **€50,000 per financial year** for the user, 1 January 2027 for corporate income tax payers under disp. final cuarta of RD 1007/2023 as amended by art. 3 of RD-ley 15/2025. **Assumption: 5% annual probability, slightly above S1 because a larger firm with more invoices is a more visible one (range 1%–15%).** Arithmetic: €50,000 × 5% = **€2,500 a year**.

_Reduced-rate re-rating._ **Assumption: 25% of revenue reduced-rate, 5% of it wrongly rated or unjustified.** Arithmetic: €4,000,000 × 25% × 5% × 11 points = **€5,500 a year**.

_Subcontracting compliance._ `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` §4 is the cluster where the product is furthest from the statute and the prospect closest to the pain. Ley 32/2006 (BOE-A-2006-18205) art. 8 and chapter IV of RD 1109/2007 (BOE-A-2007-15766) require a **Libro de Subcontratación that must remain on the site at all times**, recording each subcontracting in chronological order with its subcontracting level, the contracting undertaking, the object of the contract and the identity of the person exercising organisational and management powers — authorised by the labour authority. Breaches are sanctioned under RDLeg 5/2000 (LISOS), and `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` flags the **exact current euro brackets of art. 40.2 LISOS as UNVERIFIED**: the consolidated text returned roughly **€2,046–€40,985 for serious** and **€40,986–€819,780 for very serious** prevention infringements, while other sources report materially higher post-uprating figures and the conflict could not be settled against the primary table. Taking the midpoint of the serious bracket, €21,500, and carrying the UNVERIFIED label forward: **assumption, 6% annual probability for a firm passing more than a third of its cost through subcontractors whose paperwork lives in a folder (range 2%–15%).** Arithmetic: €21,500 × 6% = **€1,290, rounded to €1,300 a year**. Two non-monetary consequences are firmer than the bracket and matter more to an owner: prevention infringements prescribe at one, three and five years, and **very serious prevention sanctions are published** under RD 597/2007 (BOE-A-2007-9190) — a reputational penalty, and, as `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` notes, an externally detectable signal.

**Annual cost of failure three: €1,800 + €4,000 + €1,080 + €1,200 + €1,000 + €3,000 + €2,500 + €5,500 + €1,300 = €21,380, rounded to €21,400. ESTIMATE.**

**Segment 2, gross annual cost of the three failures: €40,900 + €61,500 + €21,400 = €123,800.**

### 2.6 The strongest argument against buying

The strongest case against in this segment is not behavioural — it is that **the firm may be in the wrong regime by the time the project goes live, and the product's dated trigger then evaporates**. `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` §3 records the knife-edge: the immediate information supply system is mandatory for large undertakings, meaning turnover above **€6,010,121.04** in the preceding calendar year, requiring billing records supplied through the tax authority's electronic office within four calendar days; and taxpayers keeping their record books that way under art. 62.6 of the Reglamento del IVA are **excepted from RD 1007/2023 altogether**, "pues se considera que sus sistemas informáticos y procedimientos ofrecen la necesaria garantía". A firm modelled here at €4.0m with a growth trajectory is a firm whose 2027 Verifactu argument may simply not apply to it, and whose replacement obligation — sustaining a four-day channel with acceptance, rejection and error states as durable invoice state — is, in `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md`'s own words, "a channel to be sustained, not a document to be produced", which it classes as a Tier C engineering commitment rather than a configuration item. A buyer at the top of this band is therefore entitled to say: your compliance story is for the firm I was two years ago.

Four further arguments:

1. **The statutory registers a general contractor actually asks for do not exist in the product.** This is the sharpest version of the against in this segment, because it is the pain `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` §4 says is closest: the Libro de Subcontratación that must be on site, REA registration with its three-year expiry, the coordination-of-business-activity documents under RD 171/2004, the trade card. The product holds dated documents with expiry alerts and a communication template saying that without the paperwork site access is not possible — useful, but not the authorised book. A firm whose last three pre-qualification packs all demanded those four objects by name is buying a system that does not answer its loudest question.
2. **An incumbent ledger, and integration rather than replacement.** `01-MARKET-MAP.md` §4.2 names this as what breaks above the band: a controller, a payroll bureau, often an accounting package already in place. At 18 employees and €4m some firms in this segment are already there, and `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` treats an incumbent ledger as a hard gate. The product's strongest claim — one record, no second copy — is the claim hardest to honour next to an existing ledger, and the honest posture with such a firm is to decline rather than to integrate.
3. **Keep using Excel and a gestoría.** Costed in §4. At this size the incumbent consumes more hours but also employs a real administrator to consume them, so the hours are already paid for and the €6,480 is new cash. The counter-argument is the progress-valuation line, which is not labour at all.
4. **The buying centre is starting to fragment.** `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` B.3 records a purchase made by two owners jointly with no evidence of anyone else in the room; at the top of this segment there is a third voice, and `01-MARKET-MAP.md` §4.2 warns the cycle lengthens past what the unit economics tolerate before there are references.

**Verdict on the segment: beaten, and it is the segment to lead with.** The against is real but it is a qualification problem rather than a value problem: ask revenue against the €6,010,121.04 threshold, ask for the last pre-qualification pack, and ask whether an accounting package is in place. A firm that passes all three has a five-month payback with a breach threshold at 16% of modelled value.

### 2.7 Pricing hypothesis — Segment 2

**Recommended model: per-site. €60 per active site per month, floored at €400 a month and capped at €900 a month, per legal entity, all named users and all projects included. At the modelled nine concurrent sites that is €540 a month, €6,480 a year. Planning ACV for Phase 7: €6,480.**

This is the one segment where a variable model beats flat, and the reason is that the value and the buyer's own mental model of cost both live at the site. The statute puts them there: Ley 32/2006 art. 8 requires the subcontracting book **on the site**; RD 171/2004 coordination duties attach to the works; REA and trade-card checks are demanded per site by the principal undertaking; and `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` §4 records that the enforcement moment "arrives with every new site rather than on a calendar". The firm already buys per site and is used to it — skip hire, scaffolding, site insurance riders, prevention coordination, temporary supply — so a line reading "nine sites this month" is read as an operating cost of working, which is the category the buyer accepts most easily, rather than as a software licence, which is the category it defends hardest.

Three mechanics make the model safe, and each answers an objection a sceptical buyer will raise. **Billing is on sites opened in the period, not sites open at month end**, which removes the incentive to close a site in the system before the paperwork is finished — the exact behaviour the product exists to prevent, and the reason a naive per-site price would be self-defeating. **The €400 floor** keeps a quiet quarter from falling below the cost of serving the tenant and keeps the contract legible as a retainer. **The €900 cap** means a good year is never punished, which removes the standard objection to usage pricing and keeps the price inside the **Kit Digital Segment I ceiling of €12,000** for the 10–49 band (`03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` §6, and note those amounts are **UNVERIFIED** against the bases' annexes and no call is currently open) — at the cap the annual figure is €10,800, still under the ceiling.

Why not the alternatives. **Flat** would be simpler and is the fallback if a design partner refuses to count sites, at €540 a month; it is rejected only because it forgoes the one place where price genuinely tracks value in this segment, and because it prices a six-site firm and a fifteen-site firm identically when the second has 2.5 times the captured value. **Per-seat** is rejected more firmly than in S1: at 18 employees, most of whom are crew, a per-seat price either excludes the crews — in which case it is flat with extra arithmetic — or includes them, in which case it taxes exactly the money-redacted crew surface that `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` A.10 describes as the thing "a competitor ships late because nothing goes red when it is missing". **Per-project** is rejected for the reason given once in §1.7 and applying everywhere: it rewards keeping jobs out of the system, and a second set of books kept outside the product is the failure mode `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` C.2 says cost about 5,400 deleted lines to remove from the product's own architecture.

Against the modelled €63,000 of captured value, €6,480 is a 9.7:1 ratio — higher than S1's, which is the correct direction, since this segment's value is more verifiable and its payback shorter.

### 3.2 Failure two — extras absorbed almost entirely, because the customer is a household: €8,700 a year

### 3.3 Failure three — the quarter, done in the evening by the owner: €6,400 a year

**Method.** All labour in this segment is costed at the **blended €30/hour** rate, and one point has to be made before the arithmetic rather than after it: **this labour is not a cash cost**. It is the owner's evening. No invoice is raised for it, no salary is paid for it, and it does not appear in the profit and loss. Counting it as a cost is analytically correct and commercially nearly useless, and that asymmetry is the entire reason this segment is hard to sell to. It is stated here, not in the objections section, because it changes how every number below should be read.

_Re-typing._ At a 1-in-2.5 win rate, 55 quotations at **assumption 0.6 hours each**. Arithmetic: 33h × €30 = **€990, rounded to €1,000**.

_The quarterly reconstruction._ **Assumption: 12 hours per quarter (range 6–24).** Arithmetic: 4 × 12h × €30 = **€1,440 a year**.

_The annual counterparty sort._ **Assumption: 16 hours for modelo 347.** Arithmetic: 16 × €30 = **€480**.

_Gestoría premium._ **Assumption: €100 a quarter.** Arithmetic: **€400 a year**.

_Verifactu expected loss._ €50,000 per financial year under art. 201 bis LGT (sourced), from **1 July 2027** for this legal form. **Assumption: 3% annual probability, the lowest of the three segments because a firm this size is the least visible (range 0.5%–10%).** Arithmetic: €50,000 × 3% = **€1,500 a year**.

_Reduced-rate re-rating._ **Assumption: 40% of revenue reduced-rate dwelling renovation, the highest share of the three segments because the customer base is almost entirely households, and 5% wrongly rated or unjustified.** Arithmetic: €700,000 × 40% × 5% × 11 points = **€1,540 a year**.

**Annual cost of failure three: €1,000 + €1,440 + €480 + €400 + €1,500 + €1,540 = €6,360, rounded to €6,400. ESTIMATE.**

**Segment 3, gross annual cost of the three failures: €8,000 + €8,700 + €6,400 = €23,100.**

**Cost to the buyer, year one.** Subscription €180 a month (see §3.7) = €2,160. ### 3.6 The strongest argument against buying

**In this segment the argument against wins often enough that it should be stated as the default and rebutted only on evidence.** `01-MARKET-MAP.md` §4.1 already wrote it, and it is quoted rather than paraphrased because the concession is the point: _"Excel plus a gestoría remains the rational choice. This must be conceded rather than argued around. A five-person firm running three jobs at a time can hold the margin question in one head. The product's value begins when the number of simultaneous jobs exceeds what one person can track, which is the real variable the employee count is standing in for."_ The modelled firm here runs 2.8 concurrent jobs. By Phase 1's own test it is below the line.

Four specific arguments, beyond that:

1. **The incumbent's cash cost is very close to zero and the product's is not.** §4 below puts the incumbent's incremental cash cost at **€632 a year** (spreadsheet licences and paper) against an unchanged gestoría retainer, versus €3,060 of new cash in year one for the product. The owner is being asked to multiply his software spend by five to recover value that is three-quarters his own time and his own risk tolerance.
2. **The permission model, the crew surface and the two-role split — three of the product's distinctive assets — have no audience.** `01-MARKET-MAP.md` §4.1 names all three: the operations-versus-administration distinction is inert, the firm is more often _the_ subcontractor than the one collecting others' documents, which inverts the document-expiry flow the product was built for, and redacting money figures before data leaves the server "matters when crews are people other than the owners".
3. **The legal form is a real obstacle and not a formality.** `05-TARGET-LIST.md` states that the product's permission model and billing configuration were built around a single-administrator S.L., so for an S.C.P. or a cooperative that fit "has to be argued, not assumed". A civil partnership has a different liability envelope, a different banking relationship and in the cooperative's case no single administrative owner at all — and `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.1 places the comunidad-de-bienes family, which an S.C.P. resembles, in the "Out" column. The onboarding is still L0, but the first conversation costs more than the S.L. cohort's and converts less often.
4. **The only dated urgency arrives six months later than the pitch claims.** Because an S.C.P. is not a corporate income tax payer, its Verifactu date is 1 July 2027. Leading with "110 days" to a firm that actually has 291 is the kind of error that loses a deal permanently when the gestoría corrects it.

**Verdict on the segment: conceded in part, on stated conditions.** It is not declined outright — `05-TARGET-LIST.md` placed these six in Tier B on evidence, and four of them carry a NIF, which makes them cheap to verify. But the case for buying does not survive its own sensitivity table without two qualifying facts established first: four or more simultaneous jobs, and a named job whose margin could not be explained. Absent both, the correct recommendation to the prospect is to keep using Excel and a gestoría, and the correct recommendation to the factory is to spend the contact on Segment 2 instead.

### 3.7 Pricing hypothesis — Segment 3

**Recommended model: flat, per legal entity. €180 a month, €2,160 a year, all named users and all projects included, with a hard onboarding cap of eight hours. Planning ACV for Phase 7: €2,160.**

Justification against how this segment already buys. It buys almost nothing: a gestoría retainer (**assumption, €150 to €280 a month at this size; no source**), a mobile contract, a vehicle lease, a spreadsheet licence. Every one of those is flat, monthly and small, and the relevant psychological comparison is not to other software but to the gestoría's own fee — which is why the recommended figure sits **below** it. A price above the adviser's monthly fee invites the question "so why am I still paying the gestoría?", and the honest answer is that the product does not replace the adviser: `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` A.11 records that `accounting-export@1` is unbound pending the accountant's preferred format, and `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md`'s non-regulatory trigger table calls a change of gestoría "the highest-leverage referral channel in the whole map". Designing the price so that the buyer even asks the question is a strategic error as well as a commercial one.

€2,160 is also the figure `01-MARKET-MAP.md` §3.6 assigns to the whole 1–9 band (€2,400 there; €2,160 here, 10% below it, because this segment sits at the bottom of that band and below the sweet-spot floor), and it is 72% of the **Kit Digital Segment III ceiling of €3,000** for firms under three employees and 36% of Segment II's €6,000 — both **UNVERIFIED** amounts per `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` §6, and with no call currently open. Per-seat, per-project and per-site are all rejected for one shared reason specific to this segment rather than the reasons given above: there is one buyer, one user and one evening a week available for administration, and any model that produces a different number each month produces a monthly conversation that this customer does not have time for and will cancel rather than discuss. The eight-hour onboarding cap is part of the price, not a service note: against €2,160 of annual revenue and a 4.7:1 value ratio — the thinnest of the three — an onboarding that overruns destroys the tenant's contribution for its first two years, which is precisely the measurement Phase 7 is being asked to make.

### 4.2 Where the incumbent wins, stated as a decline list

Six conditions under which "keep using Excel and a gestoría" is the correct recommendation to the prospect, each traceable to a source rather than invented:

1. **Three or fewer simultaneous jobs.** `01-MARKET-MAP.md` §4.1 and `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.2.3: below four jobs one person holds the state and the product's central promise has no audience. This is the decisive condition and it applies most often in Segment 3.
2. **Jobs below roughly €25,000 with no subcontracting.** `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.2.5: "A firm with direct labour and no subcontractors is buying four features it will never use." Four of the seven weighted adjacency components in `01-MARKET-MAP.md` §5 never activate.
3. **One preferred supplier on an annual discount.** The sourcing and supplier capabilities — worth €15,540 of the captured value in Segment 2 and €4,620 in Segment 1 — are worth close to nothing, and the comparison sheet that justified building them was the reference customer's own instrument, not a universal one.
4. **Invoicing once on completion rather than in instalments or against progress.** `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.2.6: such a firm "uses perhaps half the billing asset", and in Segment 2 it removes the €8,400 progress-valuation line, which is the most robust number in the document.
5. **Spanish-only customer documents.** `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.2.10 is explicit that where only Spanish is needed the trilingual asset "is worth nothing and should not be priced". Five of the six Tier A+B firms whose registered names are Spanish rather than Catalan are candidates for this test — noting that `05-TARGET-LIST.md` found exactly one Catalan-named row in 128.
6. **Already inside the immediate information supply system, or inside a gestoría that will supply a conforming billing tool before 2027.** The first removes the Verifactu argument by operation of law (art. 62.6 Reglamento del IVA, exception recited in RD 1007/2023 art. 3); the second removes it commercially, and it is the competitive threat this document takes most seriously, because the gestoría's offer costs the firm nothing incremental and arrives through a relationship that already exists.

A seventh condition is not a property of the prospect but of the product, and it is the honest close to this section: **until the Verifactu record layout, the QR content, the event log and the submission channel are built, the incumbent wins on compliance by default in every segment**, because the product cannot discharge the one obligation with a date on it. `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` §1 calls that build "a bounded, fully specified build whose specification is public, and the single most dateable piece of work in the roadmap". It is 110 days to 1 January 2027.

**Closing note on the register.** Fifty assumptions is not a confession of weakness; it is the price of showing arithmetic instead of asserting benefit. Three of them carry the whole document and should be attacked first by anyone who wants to break it: **A20** (the overpayment rate on third-party spend, which is 57% of Segment 2's gross failure cost), **A23** (the share of extras never invoiced, which is 40% of Segment 2's and 36% of Segment 1's captured value), and **A46** (the gestoría retainer, which decides whether this product is additive or substitutive spend). Each is measurable on a single Tier A design partner's completed job file within one week, and none requires a paid data source. That is the cheapest possible falsification test for this phase, and Phase 11 should carry it as such.
