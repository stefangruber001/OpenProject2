# 11 — RED TEAM AND PRE-MORTEM

This review was written on **2026-09-13** by the faculty supervisor of this market-entry plan, in the role an IESE programme assigns to the person who did not write the work and owes its authors nothing: to read eleven artefacts as an adversary rather than as a reader, to find the joints where one document's caveat is dropped by the next document that cites it, and to say which of them would survive contact with a real buyer. I have read `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` through `10-PILOT-SPEC.md` in order and skimmed all 128 rows of `04-PROSPECTS.jsonl`. The plan is unusually honest about its own weaknesses — more honest than most corporate strategy documents I see, and the governance convention of labelling ESTIMATE, UNVERIFIED and **assumption** in bold is genuinely good practice. That honesty is also the plan's most dangerous feature, because a caveat written in Phase 1 and repeated in Phase 3 and then silently promoted to a planning input in Phase 5, a price in Phase 6 and a sentence in a customer-facing email in Phase 9 has not been managed; it has been laundered. Everything below is an attack. Where I verified an external fact myself I say so and cite it. Where the plan is right, I have not said so, because that is not what this phase is for.

## Pre-mortem

It is **March 2028**. Eighteen months have passed and not one profitable tenant exists beyond Canei Subirats. Here are the five most probable histories, most likely first, each with the artefact that made it possible and the reasoning that artefact exposes.

### 1. The list was never a pipeline, and fifteen months went into discovering that

The most probable cause of failure is the most boring one: there were never enough qualified conversations to learn anything. `04-EXTRACTION-QA.md` states the position with a candour that should have stopped the roadmap dead: of twenty-two schema fields, **exactly four** — `legal_name`, `trade_name`, `nif`, `cnae` — "leave this audit in a state Phase 5 can build on, and two of those four rest on fewer than five verified data points." `municipality` failed at **30.8% error across four independent failure mechanisms**; `directors[]` is empty in **128 of 128** rows; `public_contracts_count`, `permits_last_24m`, `permit_value_total_eur`, `review_avg`, `review_recency_months` and `known_software[]` are empty in **128 of 128**; `nif` and `cnae` are null in **64.1%**; one row (67, Coperfi) is a real company in **Gondomar, Pontevedra**; two more are real-estate businesses; one row's email field contains its own phone number.

`05-TARGET-LIST.md` then does the only honest thing available and scores Cultural, Economic and Geographic distance at **5/10 flat for all 127 scorable firms**, producing a fixed 22.0-point floor and a ceiling of **63.6 out of 100** — the "Qualified" band, when "Ideal" starts at 85. Its own sentence: "no firm in this extraction can score above the Qualified band." Tier A is therefore not seven firms selected on fit; it is seven firms selected out of a 31-way tie on **whether a NIF and a phone number were recovered**. Read the seven write-ups: Montcada has "no phone, email or website recovered for this row at all"; Delgado & Dilla, "no contact channel of any kind was recovered"; Lluca, "no contact channel was recovered"; Mollet 2000's email field is its phone number. Four of the seven design partners cannot be contacted with the data the plan possesses.

The arithmetic of failure is then trivial. Thirty-seven Tier A+B names. Suppose two in three can be reached at all — generous, given the above — and of those, half pass the hard-gate screen that `08-GTM-PLAN.md` §3 requires (revenue mix, incumbent ledger, public work, bank, legal form, two buyers identified), and of those, one in four converts. That is **three customers**, and the plan's own throughput ceiling (cause 4) will not deliver three. At 18 months the post-mortem reads: we could not tell whether the product, the price or the message was wrong, because n was too small to carry a signal. `05-TARGET-LIST.md` names the cheapest fix — re-extract one field, CNAE, and 64 of the 78 Tier D rows probably become Tier B — and nobody did it, because re-extraction is not a deliverable and outreach is.

### 2. The product that was sold is not the product that ships

`07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §2 is the most valuable page in the entire roadmap and it is the page the three downstream documents treat as a footnote. Its bespoke list, item 2: "**The live invoice path is not the factory's invoice path.** `site/erp-ownership.json` records `invoices-collections` and `org-entity-numbering` as engine-owned, and `site/erp-engine.js:327` carries its own `vatRates: [2100, 1000, 500, 0]`… The effective-dated rule engine, the persisted legal basis, the rule identifier and the pack version — everything `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` §C.2 names as the asset a competitor cannot copy quickly — are exercised by `pnpm factory demo` and by tests, and are **not what issues the invoices the customer sends**."

Now read `09-OUTREACH-KIT.md`'s leave-behind, in the words that go on a site table: "_El motivo del tipo reducido queda grabado en la factura el día que se emite_." And the referral note to the adviser: "_con el motivo de cada tipo reducido grabado en la factura_." Claim C9 in the kit's own register traces this to `06-VALUE-CASE.md` line 209 and marks it "**Product fact** — may be asserted." It is a product fact about a command-line demonstration. The kit was written the same day as the measurement that contradicts it, cites Phase 6 rather than Phase 7, and so carries forward a capability claim that the shipping ERP does not perform. This is the cleanest example of the joint failure the review was asked to look for: the caveat exists, in the repository, in writing, and the customer-facing asset does not know it.

The same document's §4 then lists nine things that must happen before a firm can sign in, of which two are structural: **a tenant is baked into the container image** (`Dockerfile:98`), so a new customer rides a CI build and a release; and **the session token carries no tenant claim**, environment accounts are merged into every tenant's membership, and no test asserts that a cross-tenant request is refused. Seven to fourteen weeks. The probable 2028 history: the first pilot began, week three surfaced that the invoice the partner would actually send was not the invoice in the pitch, the team stopped selling and started migrating, the pilot expired on kill criterion 3 (no metric movement by day 75), and the reference was never manufactured.

### 3. The only dated trigger was discharged by somebody else, on time, and it was not us

I verified the deadline independently: RD-ley 15/2025 of 2 December 2025 postponed the billing-systems regime to **1 January 2027** for corporate income tax payers and 1 July 2027 for the rest, and the Congress convalidated it on 11 December 2025 by 179 votes to 168 ([AEAT nota informativa](https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/iva/sistemas-informaticos-facturacion-verifactu/nota-informativa-ampliacion-plazo-adaptacion-facturacion.html); [Noticias Jurídicas](https://noticias.juridicas.com/actualidad/noticias/20735-nueva-prorroga:-verifactu-no-sera-obligatorio-hasta-2027-para-sociedades-y-otros-contribuyentes/)). `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` ranks this first on urgency and is right to. It is the only forcing function in the map with a date, a penalty and a requirement that the firm's **own** software must change.

And `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §4.2 closes with the honest sentence the rest of the plan then walks past: "**until the Verifactu record layout, the QR content, the event log and the submission channel are built, the incumbent wins on compliance by default in every segment.**" Phase 6 instructs Phase 7 to "cost it as a dated jurisdiction-pack line with 1 January 2027 as its deadline, not as a backlog item" (§5.1, input 3). **Phase 7 does not cost it.** It is absent from the investment table that prices the N43 adapter, the plugin loader, the bridge module and three packs. The single most dateable, most commercially decisive piece of engineering in the roadmap has no hours, no owner and no slot, 111 days before the deadline it exists to meet.

By March 2028 the deadline is fourteen months gone. Every S.L. in the target list bought something in late 2026 — from its gestoría, from A3, Sage, Holded, or one of the "dozens" of Spanish construction packages `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` §C.1 concedes exist and Phase 6 never names. The urgency was spent on a competitor, and the plan's own objection 2 predicted it: "if it is real and dated before January 2027, that is a legitimate reason to decline our compliance pitch."

### 4. Throughput, not demand, was the binding constraint — and nobody priced the throughput

`10-PILOT-SPEC.md` commits to "**at most two pilots run at the same time, each on its own dedicated stack**", each 90 days, each consuming 16 onboarding hours plus 36 year-one support hours plus a hand-measured baseline session of 4–8 hours of the partner's time and a matching load on ours. `08-GTM-PLAN.md` §4 sets the cycle at 4–8 weeks before that. Two slots × four quarters (minus the 7–14 weeks of isolation work that must be done by the same pair of hands) is **six to eight pilot slots in eighteen months**. At an optimistic half conversion that is three or four paying tenants, at a blended €4,988 — **under €20,000 of annual recurring revenue** after eighteen months.

Compare `01-MARKET-MAP.md` §3.9, which models 40 / 75 / 120 tenants at month 36 and €192k–€576k of ARR, and says the binding constraint is supply rather than demand. It was right, and then nobody reconciled that sentence with the measured supply. The 2028 history: the plan behaved as if finding firms was the problem. Finding firms was never the problem. The province holds roughly 7,000 of them and 128 were enumerated by name in an afternoon. The problem was that the factory could host two.

### 5. The value case was never measured on a customer, so the price could not be defended when it was challenged

`06-VALUE-CASE.md` closes by naming the three assumptions that carry the document — A20 (overpayment on third-party spend), A23 (extras never invoiced), A46 (the gestoría retainer) — and states that "each is measurable on a single Tier A design partner's completed job file within one week, and none requires a paid data source. That is the cheapest possible falsification test for this phase, and Phase 11 should carry it as such." It is carried below as experiment 2. The probable history is that it was never run: the measurement has no owner in Phase 8, no slot in Phase 10's ninety days before onboarding, and no line in anybody's week. So the seller kept quoting €63,000 of captured value against €6,480 of price to owners who had been sold to every week for twenty years, the first two prospects' own job files produced numbers an order of magnitude smaller, the price was cut to close, and the discount ate a year-one cost to serve that `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §5 already measures at 44% of revenue in the weakest segment.

## Attack on the market sizing

**The load-bearing number is €134,000 of turnover per employed person** (`01-MARKET-MAP.md` §4: national construction turnover of €205,204m over approximately 1.53m people employed). Not the TAM, not the SAM, not the 76% apportionment. That quotient is what converts the employee band into a revenue band — "eight employees implies about €1.07 million and forty implies about €5.4 million", widened at the top to €8m — and the revenue band is the spine of everything after it: the ICP box in `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` §A.0, the Economic anchors in the rubric, the segment sizing in `06-VALUE-CASE.md` (S1 modelled at 11 employees and €1.6m; S2 at 18 and €4.0m), the third-party spend those revenues imply (€880,000 and €2,400,000), and therefore **every euro of failure cost, every capture rate, every payback month, every price and every CLV figure in Phases 6 and 7**, plus the three pilot metrics in Phase 10, which are measured against those same quantities.

It is the wrong shape of number, and the plan contains the evidence of that in its own sourced figures. `01-MARKET-MAP.md` §2 reports, from INE's structural statistics: **95% of construction enterprises have fewer than ten employees, and those firms employ 44.9% of sector personnel; 5% of enterprises account for 62% of turnover.** Take those two facts together. The 95% that are the target population earn 38% of turnover with 44.9% of the people, so their turnover per employed person is 0.38/0.449 = **0.85 of the sector mean: about €113,000, not €134,000.** A mean inflated by the top 5% has been used to size the bottom 95%. That is a first-year error, and §6 item 7 of the same document admits the correct figure was never found: "Average revenue per construction firm **by employee band** — not found."

**The conclusion at half its value.** Take €67,000 per employed person and hold every other assumption in Phase 6 exactly as written.

- The sweet spot band becomes roughly **€0.5m–€4.0m**, not €1.0m–€8.0m.
- The S2 reference firm — 18 employees — is a **€1.21m** business, not a €4.0m one. Third-party spend at 60% is **€726,000**, not €2.4m.
- Failure 1's overpayment line, A20 at 1.5%, falls from €36,000 to **€10,890**; captured at 35%, from €12,600 to **€3,812**.
- Extras: 22 jobs of €55,000 rather than €180,000, 2.5 extras each at 1.7% of job value, 30% unbilled — captured at 50%, from €24,750 to **€7,565**.
- The progress-valuation line, the plan's proudest number, falls from €12,000 gross and €8,400 captured to €3,630 and **€2,541**.
- Receivables from €2,000 to **€600**; the reduced-rate line from €5,500 to €1,664, so the tax lines captured fall from €7,905 to **€3,539**. Administrative labour is the only line that survives intact, because quotation counts do not scale with revenue.
- **Captured annual value: about €22,500, against €63,039 as written — 36% of central.**

Phase 6's own sensitivity table says 36% of central is a payback of roughly **ten months central and thirteen on the slow ramp**, which is not the 24-month breach. So the plan survives the halving on its own terms, and I want to be precise about what that does and does not mean. What it destroys is not payback; it is the **segmentation and the price**. Value-to-price falls from 9.7:1 to **3.5:1**, below the 4.7:1 that Phase 6 itself calls "the thinnest of the three" and uses to concede Segment 3 in part. A €540-a-month subscription on a €1.21m firm earning, say, a 4% net margin is **13% of that firm's annual profit**. And the 8-to-40-employee band now maps onto revenues that Phase 6 assigned to Segment 3 — the segment it concedes, the segment whose economics `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` shows are 31% of an S2 tenant's with a year-one cost to serve at 44% of revenue. **At half the productivity number, the entire ICP relocates into the one segment the plan tells itself to sell last.** That is the conclusion, and it follows from halving one quotient that the plan's own sources already say is 15% too high for the target population.

Three further hits on the sizing, each short.

**The provincial numbers rest on an apportionment the document itself calls its weakest, while the answer sat in its own bibliography.** §3.3 item 6 apportions Catalonia to Barcelona province across a 73–80% range because "no provincial construction count was retrieved", and §6 item 1 says every provincial count is an ESTIMATE until INE table 301 is read. Meanwhile §"Regional and territorial context" cites **XODEL's _Estructura empresarial de la província de Barcelona_, Edició 2025**, published 20 November 2025 and still live ([XODEL](https://xodel.diba.cat/news/2025/11/20/estructura-empresarial-de-provincia-de-barcelona-edicio-2025)). A provincial enterprise-structure report was in the source list, unread, because an egress policy blocked the fetch. The honest statement is not "not found"; it is "not fetched from this container", and the remedy is ten minutes on any unblocked machine.

**Halving the count changes nothing, which is the real indictment.** Halve SAM from 7,240 firms and €30.5m to 3,620 and €15.2m: SOM is unaffected, because §3.9 sets SOM by delivery capacity. Halve the apportionment: same answer. The entire Phase 1 apparatus — four sourced anchors, two cross-checks, a method shown, a range given — **does not bind any decision the plan makes**, because the binding constraint is two concurrent pilots and 7–14 weeks of platform work. Ninety pages of sizing were spent establishing that a market the plan cannot serve at scale is somewhat larger or somewhat smaller than a market the plan cannot serve at scale.

**And the segment that is half the pipeline is defined by CNAE codes that do not exist.** `05-TARGET-LIST.md` concedes that rows carry "4100, 4101, 4102, 4121, and the two-digit 41" and that none "correspond to any class the Spanish CNAE-2009 nomenclature actually defines at that precision (the real subclass is 41.20)". `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §0.3 then builds **Segment 2 — nineteen firms, 51% of the near-term pipeline, three of seven design partners** — on "CNAE division 41 (41.20 and the malformed 41.00/41.01/41.02/41 rows read as division 41)". The cut between S1 and S2 is defended as "the right cut because it is the only one the data supports at 0% audited error." The 0% error figure comes from **three or four audited observations in a thirteen-row sample**, and the codes in question are malformed. Phase 1 warned, in §0, that CNAE-2025 has re-cut these codes and "the crosswalk has to be held explicitly or the segments will silently change shape." They changed shape silently.

## Attack on the layer model

The layer model is the best thing in the plan and it is mis-specified in a way that costs real money. Three attacks, in descending order of expected cost.

**First: "L0" is most likely wrong about the bank, and the plan knows it and sells anyway.** `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` §C.1 lists "a second bank's statement layout" as the first item under "what looks L0 and is not"; §C.5 names the statement-import port as "the highest-value L4 on the board, because unlike every other candidate it is forced by the core segment rather than by an adjacency"; §A.5 soft gate 9 caps such a firm at 75. `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §5 then estimates, with no source, that the one supported bank "holds at most one in five primary banking relationships", so "**roughly thirty of the thirty-seven Tier A+B firms are blocked on this single axis today**."

Cost when it is wrong, in Phase 7's own hours: L4 is L0 + 110 hours. For an S2 tenant that is 16 + 110 = **126 hours, €8,820 at €70**, against an annual contract value of €6,480 and a year-one L0 gross margin of €5,132. Phase 7's own margin table gives the answer: **−€2,168, −24.4%.** Charge the bank to the first tenant that needs it and the tenant is unprofitable in year one by a quarter of its revenue; do it four-fifths of the time, as the estimate implies, and the business is a loss-making bespoke shop with a very good architecture document. The fix is named, costed at 50 hours and €3,500, and pays back at two tenants — and `08-GTM-PLAN.md` §2 nonetheless sequences it **after** the first Segment 2 reference converts, which is precisely backwards: the reference cannot be converted profitably until the adapter exists.

**Second: the model's taxonomy cannot express its own binding constraint.** L0 to L5 classify _domain knowledge_ — config, plugin, vertical, jurisdiction, capability, kernel. The two items that actually stop a second customer are neither: a tenant is a build artefact (2–4 weeks) and a session has no tenant claim (3–6 weeks). At the same €70 and the same single-engineer productivity that is roughly **280 to 560 hours, €19,600 to €39,200**, and it appears in no layer row, no margin table and no payback calculation anywhere in Phases 5 to 7. A cost model that prices six layers of domain distance and omits the platform work that gates every sale is not conservative; it is looking in the wrong place. `10-PILOT-SPEC.md` deserves credit here — it carries the constraint faithfully, writes "two customers cannot yet safely share one stack" into a partner-facing document, and caps concurrency at two. `08-GTM-PLAN.md` §4 carries it too, as a throughput limit. Neither converts it into euros, and Phase 7's compounding section does not list it among the investments.

**Third: every hour figure in the model is quoted at a productivity that cannot be hired, and the one "good news" finding depends on it.** §3 states the rate honestly — one engineer with heavy automated assistance, and "a conventional human-only team should multiply every hour figure by three to five and every calendar figure by two to three" — and then says the multiplier "does not change any margin conclusion." It changes the only margin conclusion that is cited downstream. Apply 3× to the measured S2 L0 of 16 hours: **48 hours, €3,360**, against the €2,400 onboarding fee that Phase 7 celebrates as covering cost with "53% of headroom". At 4× it is €4,480. The fee-positive finding, which `08-GTM-PLAN.md` §4 uses to declare a demo on the prospect's own data "close to free", holds only while the factory is operated by the one person who built it at the rate they built it. That is a single-point-of-failure assumption wearing the costume of a measurement.

Two smaller blades. The accountant export: `accounting-export@1` is unbound, and `09-OUTREACH-KIT.md`'s referral cover tells the adviser "_dime qué formato quieres y lo preparamos así_" — an unpriced promise to build a per-gestoría adapter, which with no plugin loader is L1-priced-as-L2 at **€2,800, or 43% of an S2 annual contract value**, given away in a cover note, once per adviser. And the L0 boundary "validates shape, not fitness": §1 records that a tenant resolves and validates with the placeholder tax identifier `B00000000` (which is check-digit valid and sits in three tenant files today), with `NOT-A-CIF`, with no tax identifier at all, with no fiscal address, with an unsubstituted legal-name token and with two series sharing an id — and then issues immutable, gapless, hash-chained invoices correctable only by rectification. The cheapest catastrophic L0 failure in the plan is a successful onboarding.

## Attack on the ICP

The ICP has an adverse-selection problem that the rubric encodes deliberately and the value case then prices as if it did not exist.

`02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md`'s Economic anchor at score 4 reads: "a margin discipline already in place with weekly variance reporting, **which removes the product's central promise**." The instrument therefore scores a well-run firm _down_. Its own note admits why: "Phase 11 is already scheduled to attack the ICP on precisely this point." Scheduling the attack is not answering it. The structure of the problem is this: the product's value is the value of knowing, and the firms that score highest are by construction the firms that have chosen for six to thirty years not to know. **A firm that has survived that long without knowing has revealed its own estimate of what knowing is worth, and the estimate is lower than the price.** Willingness to pay does not track the modelled value of information; it tracks the owner's felt pain, and felt pain is precisely what a coping firm does not have.

Now the harder version, which is the statistical error. `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §1.5 says payback exceeds 24 months in Segment 1 only if **three** things hold at once — extras already billed, supplier choice not contestable, the gestoría absorbing the quarter — and concludes that payback "is robust to any one of the three failing and fragile to all three failing together." §2.5 requires **four** simultaneous conditions for Segment 2 and calls that world "harder to construct". Both arguments treat the conditions as independent. **They are not independent; they are four symptoms of one cause.** A firm that bills its extras does so because somebody owns the paperwork; the same person lets subcontract packages to three known firms at negotiated annual rates, closes the quarter in two days, and has already asked the adviser about 2027. Well-run-ness is the latent variable. Conditional on it, all four conditions co-occur, and the breach that Phase 6 computes as the product of four improbabilities is in fact the modal state of exactly the firms that score best on the rubric's Cultural dimension ("keeps a job-cost workbook today and will show it", "volunteers margin numbers", score 10).

So the ICP splits into two halves, and both are unsellable for opposite reasons. The coping half scores high, has no pain, and closes at the price of a long education. The struggling half has the pain, and it is the half with a `@yahoo.es` address, a mobile-shaped sole phone number, 2.8 concurrent jobs against a four-job floor, an S.C.P. or no legal-form suffix at all, and a Verifactu date six months later than the one the outreach quotes. `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §3.6 concedes it in writing: "_Excel plus a gestoría remains the rational choice… This must be conceded rather than argued around_."

What remains in the middle is a conjunction, and conjunctions are thin. The firm must have 12–40 employees, no weekly variance reporting, no incumbent ledger, no material public work, no maintenance-majority revenue, the one supported bank, a cooperative gestoría with a conventional export format, a second administrative person, four or more live jobs, and — for a Catalan-first prospect — a Catalan-speaking seller available. Assign each gate a generous independent pass rate and multiply: 0.6 bank × 0.8 no ledger × 0.8 no public work × 0.7 not maintenance-led × 0.7 headcount band actually in range ≈ **19%**. Nineteen per cent of thirty-seven is **seven firms** — numerically the Tier A cohort, arrived at by coincidence rather than by selection, and none of the seven has had a single gate confirmed, which `05-TARGET-LIST.md` states plainly: "None of the six hard gates can be evaluated from this extraction, so none is claimed clear here."

One more, because it is the root: the rubric's calibration example is the reference tenant itself, scored 92.0, and the indicators of a perfect Cultural score are a recitation of what Canei Subirats did — commissioned a requirements document, sent real bank exports, reviewed screens on a phone, mandated its own vocabulary. **An ICP derived from one customer and validated against that same customer is a portrait with weights.** `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` §B.2 is admirably clear that nobody knows why that customer bought — "I do not know what triggered the purchase, and the repository does not record it" — and questions 1 to 5 of its own list remain unanswered. The plan has therefore built a targeting instrument whose origin is a purchase whose motive is unknown, and the instrument cannot score any prospect above 63.6 on the data it is fed. An ICP that cannot be tested is a hypothesis in the costume of a filter.

## Attack on the value case

**The softest euro figure is A20: the 1.5% of third-party spend assumed to be overpaid because a comparison was incomplete — €36,000 a year in Segment 2 and €13,200 in Segment 1.** The document volunteers that it is the weakest, which earns it no protection whatsoever, because it is also the largest and because three separate things are wrong with it.

_It is unfalsifiable after the fact._ To prove you overpaid by 1.5% you need the price you did not obtain. There is no line in any ledger, no invoice, no bank movement and no variance report that records a counterfactual. Every other line in Phase 6 can in principle be audited from the firm's own files — an extra either was invoiced or was not, a quarter either took five days or three — and this one cannot be audited even by the buyer, which is why it is the line a sceptical owner will reject from experience and the line no pilot metric can settle. `10-PILOT-SPEC.md` notices this and quietly retreats: Metric 1 measures "the percentage of decisions with no recorded comparison… **rather than as a euro figure, because the euro figure depends on the overpayment-rate assumption §2.1 itself flags as its least evidenced number**." So the pilot will measure a proxy and the pitch will keep the euro.

_Its stated range is a sixfold spread on the biggest number in the document._ 0.5% to 3.0% on €2.4m is €12,000 to €72,000. A line with a 6× range carrying 29% of a segment's gross failure cost is not an estimate; it is a placeholder with a decimal point.

_And the assumptions register mis-states its own weight by a factor of nearly three._ The closing note of `06-VALUE-CASE.md` says A20 "is 57% of Segment 2's gross failure cost". €36,000 of €123,800 is **29%**. The 57% figure is €36,000 ÷ €63,039 — a **gross** failure cost divided by **captured** value, which is exactly the conflation §0.1 built a three-step method to prevent ("the gap between them is the part of the pitch that is not true yet"). Only €12,600 of that €36,000 is claimed as captured, i.e. **20%** of captured value. A document whose governing discipline is that gross and captured must never be mixed mixes them in the one sentence that tells the reader which assumption to attack first.

Two runners-up, because softness is not a single axis.

**A46, the gestoría retainer (€300 / €450 / €200 a month, "no source")** is the number the author correctly calls "the most important unsourced number in the document", because it decides whether the product is additive or substitutive spend and sets the psychological price ceiling. It is also a number obtainable in one phone call to any Catalan gestoría, and nobody made the call. The entire §4 conclusion — "86% to 91% of the incumbent's cash cost survives the purchase" — is derived from it.

**The Verifactu expected-loss lines (€2,000 in S1, €2,500 in S2)** multiply a genuinely sourced penalty (€50,000 per financial year, art. 201 bis LGT) by an invented 4–5% probability with a stated range of 0.5% to 15% — a thirtyfold spread. Worse than the spread is the direction: the product **does not discharge the obligation**. Phase 6 claims 85% capture on the tax lines while its own §4.2 says the incumbent wins on compliance by default until the submission channel exists. Revenue is being booked today against a penalty the product will not remove on 1 January 2027, and `09-OUTREACH-KIT.md` puts the €50,000 in a follow-up email. The honest capture rate on that line, today, is zero, exactly as the subcontracting-register line is honestly carried at zero.

And one correction to the plan's own self-assessment. Phase 6 calls the €8,400 progress-valuation line its most robust number "because it is arithmetic the system performs rather than a habit the owner must form", and Phase 8 sequences the whole go-to-market on that claim. The arithmetic is _executed minus already billed_. **"Executed" is a human input**, entered by a site manager who today does not measure it — which is the entire premise of the failure being costed. The most robust number in the document rests on the same site-discipline assumption every other line was discounted to 50% for. Rename it honestly: it is a habit with a calculator attached, and it deserves a capture rate nearer 50% than 70%, which takes €8,400 to €6,000 and removes a fifth of the reason Segment 2 leads.

## What the plan has not considered

1. **Liability, insurance and what happens when the system is wrong about money.** Every rule in `LEGAL_REVIEW.md` carries `legally_verified: false`; a tenant can go live with a placeholder tax identifier; invoices are immutable and hash-chained. A single mis-rated quarter on a €4m Segment 2 firm at 11 points of tax is six figures of exposure for the buyer. `10-PILOT-SPEC.md`'s term sheet — fourteen rows, art. 28 clauses, sub-processors, breach windows — contains **no limitation of liability, no indemnity, no professional-indemnity cover and no errors-and-omissions position**. The pilot fee is €900. Separately, `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` §1 records a **producer-side** exposure of €150,000 per financial year per type of system for marketing non-conforming billing software, plus a _declaración responsable_ the producer must sign. That is the factory's own liability, it is named once, and it never appears in a cost model, a risk register or a price.
2. **Who does the work.** There is no headcount, no hiring plan, no cost of sales and no bus factor anywhere in Phases 5 to 10. One person is implicitly the engineer at €70/hour, the Catalan-speaking seller `08-GTM-PLAN.md` §5 requires, the support function at 36 hours per tenant per year, the signatory of art. 28 agreements, and the on-call responder who must decide kill criterion 1 within 24 hours of a security incident. The only cost-of-sales figure in the entire plan is a hypothetical 15% gestoría revenue share.
3. **The profit and loss, the funding requirement and the runway.** Eighteen months of one-to-two people, plus 7–14 weeks of platform work, plus N43, plus the loader, plus the Verifactu build, plus a pack, against €20,000–€60,000 of ARR. Nobody has written the cash number. Phase 12 is asked for "the ask"; nothing in Phases 0 to 10 computes it.
4. **Named competitors and real market prices.** `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` §C.1 says "there are dozens for Spanish construction, and Phase 6 will have to argue against them by name." Phase 6 argues against a spreadsheet. No Presto, no Arquímedes, no Gestproject, no Sage, A3, Holded or Odoo; no competitor price list; no switching-cost analysis; no feature comparison. An evening's work decides whether €540 a month is a bargain or triple the market, and it was not done — while the plan simultaneously identifies the gestoría channel as both its best referral route and its sharpest competitor without ever asking what software those gestorías already resell.
5. **Churn, insolvency and the 12.5-year lifetime.** `05-TARGET-LIST.md` assumes 92% gross retention, yielding a 12.5-year expected life, for a product that has never renewed a single customer, in a cyclical sector, with no model of customer insolvency — which in Spanish construction is not a rounding error. Halve the lifetime and S2 net CLV falls from €70,646 to roughly €35,000, which changes every sequencing and discounting argument built on it. No discount rate is applied anywhere, by explicit choice, twice.
6. **The art. 28 security representation the plan is about to sign while a known defect is open.** `10-PILOT-SPEC.md` commits to naming art. 32 measures "specifically rather than generically — per-tenant schema isolation, dedicated infrastructure…", signed before the baseline session. `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §4 item 9 records that environment accounts are merged into every tenant's membership, the session token carries no tenant claim, the shared-password route has no account behind it, and **no test asserts that a cross-tenant request is refused**. Dedicated stacks mitigate the risk; they do not make the representation true of the software. Signing that clause before the test exists is a misrepresentation to a controller, and it is the one failure in this plan that ends in a regulator rather than a lost sale.
7. **Tenant #1.** Nineteen of twenty-seven domain areas are still engine-owned, Canei's identity is compiled into the shipped product, and the production compose project is named after them. Every hour on tenant #2 is an hour not spent finishing tenant #1, and nothing in the plan asks whether the reference survives the distraction, what the existing contract permits, or whether consent to be named has been sought — question 30 of `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md`'s own list, still open.
8. **The per-site price meters data the customer enters.** Billing on "sites opened in the period" makes the invoice a function of the buyer's own bookkeeping, which is the identical perverse incentive Phase 6 rejects per-project pricing to avoid ("it rewards keeping jobs out of the system"). The mitigation offered is a billing rule; the exposure is a dispute in month four of every contract.
9. **The subsidy was declared dead on search evidence, and the evidence is contested.** `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` §6 states there is "no open Kit Digital call" and flags the per-segment amounts as UNVERIFIED; Phases 1, 2, 5 and 6 then use those unverified ceilings as the anchor for every price in the plan. I checked: secondary reporting on **Orden TDF/39/2026** describes a live route via remaining funds — "permite solicitar el bono digital hasta agotar presupuesto" — with the Segment I ceiling of €12,000 maintained ([Red.es convocatorias](https://www.kitdigital.red.es/convocatorias); [Incrementa](https://incrementamarketing.com/blog/kit-digital-2026-fondos-remanentes/)). I do not assert this is correct; I assert that the plan's only funding lever is simultaneously load-bearing as a price anchor and unverified as a fact, in both directions, and that one hour on the Red.es site settles it.

## Three experiments

Three falsifying experiments, each executable inside 30 days, cheapest first, each with a pre-committed numeric threshold that counts as failure. The word is chosen deliberately: these are not validation exercises, and an experiment whose failure condition is written afterwards is a sales call.

### Experiment 1 — Can the list be reached at all? (cost ≈ €1,400; 5 working days)

Telephone all thirty-seven Tier A and Tier B firms. No pitch. Four questions: are you trading and do you do renovation contracting; how many employees; how many sites are open today; which bank does the company use. Record outcomes. Cost: 20 hours of one person at €70 = **€1,400**, no cash outlay, no data purchase.

**Pre-committed failure thresholds.** (a) If **fewer than 12 of 37** firms can be reached and confirmed as trading renovation contractors, the extraction is not a pipeline: all outreach stops and the CNAE-and-contact re-extraction `05-TARGET-LIST.md` names as its cheapest action runs first. (b) If **fewer than 8 of 37** report four or more live sites, the four-job floor disqualifies the list and the ICP band is wrong, not the prospects. (c) If **more than 26 of the firms reached** name a bank other than the supported one, the N43 adapter moves ahead of every sales activity, including the first pilot. This experiment also tests, for €1,400, the one assumption `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §5 concedes has no source and on which thirty of thirty-seven tenants' layer classification depends.

### Experiment 2 — Are A20, A23 and A46 real? (cost ≈ €2,100; 3 weeks)

Pay two firms €500 each for four hours of access to two completed job files and one quarter's gestoría invoice. Measure three things on their numbers: for the three largest purchase decisions per job, whether more than one priced alternative existed and what the spread between the cheapest alternative and the committed price actually was; the count and euro value of extras raised versus extras invoiced; and the adviser's actual monthly retainer. Cost: €1,000 of incentive plus 16 hours at €70 = **€2,120**.

**Pre-committed failure thresholds.** (a) If the measured price spread on committed purchases is **below 0.5% of third-party spend on both firms**, A20 is falsified at the bottom of its own range and the €36,000 line is struck from Segment 2 before another email is sent. (b) If the unbilled-extras share is **below 15%**, A23 is falsified at the bottom of its range and, with (a), Segment 2's captured value falls below **€25,000**, a 3.9:1 value-to-price ratio — thinner than the ratio on which Phase 6 concedes Segment 3 — and the €540-a-month per-site price is withdrawn pending a rebuild. (c) If the measured gestoría retainer is **below €250 a month** for a Segment 2 firm, the incumbent's cash cost and therefore the price ceiling are overstated and Phase 6 §4 is rewritten before it is quoted again. Phase 6 identified this experiment itself and called it "the cheapest possible falsification test for this phase". It has no owner. Give it one.

### Experiment 3 — Can one real tenant actually be delivered, on the shipping product, on its own bank? (cost ≈ €5,800; 30 days)

Take one Tier A Segment 2 firm and onboard it end to end, with a stopwatch, on the product a customer would actually use — not `pnpm factory demo`. Its branding, its series, a sample of its catalogue, its own bank export imported, and one invoice issued through the path the firm would really send from. Budget 80 delivered hours at €70 plus €200 of infrastructure and provisioning = **€5,800**.

**Pre-committed failure thresholds.** (a) If total delivered hours exceed **40** — 2.5× the measured central of 16 — then the L0 cost model, the €2,400 onboarding fee, the "fee-positive" finding and the 46–58% year-one margins are all false, and no second pilot starts until they are re-measured. (b) If the firm's bank export **cannot be imported without writing code**, the layer classification of that firm was wrong and the adapter is on the critical path, confirming or refuting experiment 1(c) on a real file rather than a phone answer. (c) If the invoice the firm would actually issue **does not carry the persisted rule identifier, legal basis and pack version**, then claim C9 is deleted from `09-OUTREACH-KIT.md` within 24 hours, the compliance half of the value case is priced at zero in all three segments, and the Verifactu build is scheduled with hours and an owner before any further outreach. Any one of the three is a stop, not a learning.

## Verdict

**Proceed with conditions.** The asset is real: a printable configuration boundary, a proven separation of jurisdiction from capability, and a segment small enough to enumerate by name. The plan around it is not yet a plan — it prices a product that does not ship the thing it sells, against a list that cannot be phoned, on a number its own sources say is fifteen per cent too high.

Conditions, all of them gating: run the three experiments before any further outreach, and honour the thresholds. Strike claim C9 from the outreach kit and price compliance at zero until the submission channel exists. Build the N43 adapter before the first pilot, not after the first reference. Close the tenant-identity and session-isolation defects before real customer data arrives, and do not sign an art. 28 security representation until a cross-tenant refusal test passes. Re-derive the revenue band from banded data. Re-extract CNAE and contact channels. Write the profit and loss and the funding ask.

Fail experiment 1 and stop.
