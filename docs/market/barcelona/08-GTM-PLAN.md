# 08 — GO-TO-MARKET MOTION

This document is Phase 8 of the Barcelona market roadmap (`docs/market/barcelona/ROADMAP.md`), written on **2026-09-13**, and it designs the route to market for the thirty-seven firms `05-TARGET-LIST.md` placed in Tier A and Tier B, using the segment economics of `06-VALUE-CASE.md`, the qualification instrument of `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md`, the trigger openers of `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md`, and the measured tailoring cost of `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md`. It answers the roadmap's six questions in order — channel against direct, sequencing, qualification, sales cycle, language, and the objections a seller will actually hear — and it inherits the discipline the phases before it were built on: a claim traced to a file or a cited source is stated as fact, a claim resting on judgement is marked **assumption** in bold with a reason, and the honest answer is given even where it argues against the sale. Three external facts ground the channel assessment below and are cited where used: the membership and service structure of the Gremi de Constructors d'Obres de Barcelona i Comarques and of the Cambra de Contractistes d'Obres de Catalunya (CCOC), the services model of CAATEEB, and the current, narrower state of the Kit Digital digitalisation-voucher programme as it stood in early 2026.

## 1 · Channel versus direct

The roadmap names three channel hypotheses specific to Catalonia. None of the three is free of a cost this document has to state plainly: a channel partner earns nothing until the first referral closes, and two of the three sit closer to the buyer than we do, which is leverage that can point either way. The frame used below for all three is the one `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §4 already established for the incumbent — a channel is worth building only if it is honestly compared against the cash-and-time cost of going direct, not assumed to be free because it is not a salary.

### a. Gestorías and asesorías

**Incentive structure.** A gestoría already holds the books of hundreds of firms in our ICP and files their quarterly VAT and payroll returns, which means it feels the exact pain `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md`'s second-ranked trigger names — "somebody in your office will reconstruct a quarter of purchases and sales from paper" — from the other side of the desk. The natural incentive structure, and the one the market's own software vendors already run, is a **partner/"colaborador" programme**: certification as an official partner, a listing the adviser can put in front of its own client base, and a recurring revenue share rather than a one-off finder's fee. Holded's own accounting-firm partner programme is exactly this shape — certified-partner status, a marketplace listing, and negotiated terms per firm — and it is the model to imitate rather than invent, because a Catalan gestoría has likely already seen an offer shaped this way from a general-ledger vendor and will recognise the structure instantly. **Assumption, no source: a 15% recurring share of the annual contract value for the life of the referred tenant, capped so the vendor's own steady-state gross margin (measured at 82–89% per `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §5) is never compressed below 70%.** A flat one-off fee is worth naming as the fallback, because it is simpler to explain to an adviser who bills by the hour and does not think in annuities.

**Conflict risk — the highest of the three, and it must be named before anything else about this channel.** `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §4.2's sixth decline condition and its §1.6/§2.6 objections are explicit: the single most credible competitive threat to this product in every segment is not another ERP vendor, it is **the gestoría's own conforming billing tool**, offered at no incremental cost to a relationship the client already trusts and already pays for. A gestoría approached as a channel partner has, by the same conversation, been told exactly what a construction SME's compliance pain is worth — which is an invitation to solve it themselves rather than refer it onward. This is not a hypothetical risk to be managed later; it is the same fact `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §4.1 states about the incumbent's cash cost surviving the purchase (86–91% of it, unmoved), read from the adviser's side of the ledger instead of the client's.

**Time to first referral.** A gestoría has zero standing incentive to refer today: no relationship with us exists, no comp plan exists, and referring a client to a third party is itself a small trust transaction the adviser has no reason to spend until it has been asked, trained and paid once. **Assumption: two to four months** from a signed partner agreement to a first live referral, because the natural moment to raise the subject with a client is the same quarterly filing cycle that causes the pain — so referrals cluster around quarter-end rather than arriving evenly, and the first cluster is the one the partner agreement has to be in place before.

**What has to be true for this to beat direct.** The channel only outperforms direct sales in a narrow, nameable world: the gestoría's own Verifactu-tool ambitions have to be absent or slower than ours (a fact worth asking directly on the first call, per objection 2 in §6 below), the revenue share has to survive the segment's own margin math (viable in Segment 2 at 88.6% steady-state margin, tight in Segment 3 at 82.0%), and the adviser has to see more upside in an ongoing share of a client's software spend than in selling that spend itself. Until a gestoría's own product roadmap has been asked about directly, this channel should be treated as **defensive intelligence-gathering on the six named gestoría relationships already inferred to exist among Tier A and B firms**, not as a lead source to build a quota against.

### b. CAATEEB aparelladors

**Incentive structure.** `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.4.6 already names the mechanism: an aparellador or architect sits between owner and contractor on most reforms, is trusted by both sides, and their presence correlates with measured schedules and chaptered quotes — exactly the shape of document the product produces. CAATEEB (Col·legi d'Aparelladors, Arquitectes Tècnics i Enginyers d'Edificació de Barcelona) describes its own role publicly as extending beyond its roughly 8,000 members into "a services entity… for other professionals and companies in the building sector," running technical sessions at which companies present products to its membership — a real, existing mechanism, not one that has to be invented. It also has precedent for a formal software convention: a published agreement between CAATEEB and the accounting-and-management platform GESTCAT shows the college does sign institutional conventions with software providers, not only host informal talks. Two structures follow from this: an individual aparellador can be incentivised the way most Spanish professional colleges permit — reciprocal value rather than a cash commission, since deontological codes for regulated professions typically restrict members from taking referral fees on client business they supervise — and an institutional convention (a CAATEEB-branded technical session, a joint checklist for document-expiry compliance on site, or co-produced content) can be pursued in parallel, at the college level rather than the individual level, once a reference exists to present.

**Conflict risk — lower than the gestoría channel, but real in a different way.** The aparellador supervises the works, not the accounts, so there is no product-substitution risk of the kind a gestoría poses. The risk here is reputational and precise: an aparellador's professional standing depends on independence from any single contractor's back office, so a pitch that reads as "get your contractor to sign up and we will look after you" reads as compromising that independence and will be declined by any aparellador worth having as a channel. The safe framing is the one CAATEEB's own model implies — reciprocal professional value (the graphic annex and progress-valuation documents the aparellador themselves reviews, made easier to check) rather than a referral transaction.

**Time to first referral. Assumption: four to six months.** An aparellador works across many simultaneous job sites for many different owners and the natural moment to mention a tool is at the start of a new client relationship, which is intermittent rather than scheduled — slower than the gestoría's quarterly cadence, but the professional's trust, once given, carries further because it is disinterested.

**What has to be true for this to beat direct.** The product has to visibly reduce the aparellador's own friction — chiefly the progress-valuation and graphic-annex documents `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §2.4 already names as the highest-capture, most arithmetic (not behavioural) part of the value case — because an aparellador refers on professional judgement about whether the paperwork on a site improves, not on commission. This channel is realistically a Sequencing-phase amplifier (§2 below), not a first-tenant source: nothing in `04a-SOURCE-PLAN.md`'s own list of professional-college referral channels changes the fact that a college endorses outcomes it has seen, not promises.

### c. Trade gremis as endorsement and event access

**Incentive structure.** The Gremi de Constructors d'Obres de Barcelona i Comarques reports roughly 1,300–1,332 affiliated companies — the figure `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.4.5 already cites — and its own published member-services page states it already runs **discounted, endorsed third-party business services for its affiliates**, including labour-and-accounting management services aimed specifically at construction and rehabilitation SMEs. That is a structural opening rather than a hypothesis: becoming one of the gremi's endorsed-supplier discounts is a known slot to apply for, not a mechanism to invent, and the natural incentive is a **sponsorship or exhibitor arrangement plus a standing member discount**, not a per-referral commission — gremis are membership bodies funded by dues and sponsorship, and behave commercially like a trade-show organiser rather than an affiliate network.

**Conflict risk.** Two distinct risks, both worth naming rather than one glossed. First, the gremi's own labour-and-accounting service for members is itself adjacent to the compliance pain this product addresses, so an endorsement conversation should establish early whether that in-house service already covers billing software or stops at payroll and bookkeeping. Second, the Cambra de Contractistes d'Obres de Catalunya (CCOC) — the other trade body the roadmap names — is a materially different population: CCOC's own account of itself states its members perform on average 80% of the volume of public works in Catalonia and its roster includes national contractors such as Acciona Construcción and FCC Construcción. That is precisely the population `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.5's third hard gate excludes — public-sector work as a material share of revenue — so CCOC should be treated as a mismatched channel for this ICP rather than a second gremi to court, and the roadmap's instruction to assess trade gremis "as endorsement and event access" is best read, on the evidence, as an instruction about the Gremi de Constructors specifically.

**Time to first referral. Assumption: six to twelve months.** Endorsement decisions at a membership body run through a board or committee on its own calendar, event slots are typically quarterly at best, and — as with CAATEEB — a gremi endorses what it has already seen work, which means this channel is structurally a lagging amplifier rather than a leading one.

**What has to be true for this to beat direct.** A named, citable reference has to exist before the gremi will put a name behind the product at all; a first speaking slot or newsletter mention is achievable earlier, but a discount-programme listing — the concrete commercial asset — realistically requires the same reference `06-VALUE-CASE.md` calls citable. This is the clearest of the three channels to sequence: it comes **after** the first tenant, not instead of it.

**The channel-versus-direct verdict.** All three hypotheses share one property that decides this section: none can deliver a referral inside the sales-cycle window (§4 below) without a reference already existing, and the one channel that could move faster — the gestoría — is also the one with the sharpest conflict risk. The recommendation is therefore **direct sales for Tier A, with channel development run in parallel as intelligence-gathering rather than as a lead-generation programme**, and the three channels graduate to active amplification only once Sequencing's first reference (§2) exists to show them. This is not a rejection of the channel thesis; it is a statement of order.

## 2 · Sequencing

**Segment 2 — renovation-led general building contractors — goes first.** `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §2.6 states its own verdict without hedging: "beaten, and it is the segment to lead with." The reasons compound rather than stand alone. Segment 2 is the largest slice of the near-term pipeline at nineteen of thirty-seven Tier A and B firms (51%), the payback is the shortest of the three segments at five months central case, and — the fact that matters most for a first reference — its single largest captured-value line, the €8,400 progress-valuation correction, is **arithmetic the system performs rather than a habit the owner must form**, carrying the highest capture rate in the whole value case at 70%. A reference built on a capability the product executes rather than one that depends on the owner changing how he behaves is a reference that survives scrutiny, which is exactly what a citable outcome requires. Segment 2 also happens to be where `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §6 finds the two highest-leverage infrastructure investments pay back fastest: the Spain × construction subcontracting-and-registers bridge pays back at a single converted Segment 2 tenant, and the N43 bank-statement adapter — which unblocks an estimated thirty of the thirty-seven Tier A+B firms currently on an unsupported bank — pays back at two tenants of any segment. Leading with Segment 2 therefore does not just produce the best first reference; it is the fastest path to funding the two fixes that unlock the rest of the pipeline.

**Segment 1 — finishing and completion trades — earns the right to go second.** It is the reference customer's own trade family (`01-MARKET-MAP.md` §5 scores it 95 of 100, the highest adjacency score in the whole map), it shares the jurisdiction pack and almost all of Segment 2's tooling, and a Segment 2 reference is directly transferable to it: both segments sell the same eighteen-chapter quotation spine, both face the same 1 January 2027 Verifactu deadline as corporate income tax payers, and both are sold to by the same kind of owner-manager. Once a Segment 2 firm can be named as a working customer, the pitch to a Segment 1 prospect is "the same product, the same compliance deadline, a smaller job size" rather than a cold claim.

**Segment 3 — owner-partner micro-contractors — is sequenced last, deliberately, and largely as a referral and reference source rather than a revenue base.** `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §3.6 is explicit that the argument against buying wins often enough in this segment to be stated as the default: firms here run 2.8 concurrent jobs against Phase 1's own four-job floor, and the segment's own captured value collapses under two individually plausible assumptions (the owner's evening hours priced at zero, and his own inspection risk judged near zero). `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §6 confirms the same conclusion from the cost side: Segment 3's net CLV is 31% of a Segment 2 tenant's, its year-one cost to serve runs to 44% of revenue, and the priced eight-hour onboarding cap equals the measured central estimate exactly — meaning half of all Segment 3 onboardings overrun the cap by construction unless the cap or the fee is revised. Selling here first would put the weakest economics and the tightest onboarding margin in front of the newest sales motion; selling here last, once the motion is proven on Segments 2 and 1, is the safer order.

**Tier C's installer pack follows the same logic and not before.** `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §6 states it directly: build the recurring-contract-and-work-order pack for the eight named Segment-adjacent installers **after the first Segment 2 reference converts, not before** — selling an adjacency before the core segment has a named reference turns a reusable asset into a one-off project, which is precisely the failure mode the layer model exists to prevent.

**The reference being manufactured.** Per the roadmap's own instruction, this is named by firm type, not by a fabricated quote or testimonial — no customer statement is invented anywhere in this document, and any real quote is gated behind the `[REQUIRES CUSTOMER CONSENT]` placeholder convention `docs/market/barcelona/ROADMAP.md`'s own Phase 9 mandates. The reference to manufacture is **a Tier-A renovation-led general building contractor (Segment 2)**: concretely, one of the three Segment 2 firms `05-TARGET-LIST.md` places in Tier A — Edificaciones Y Reformas Mollet 2000 SL, Delgado & Dilla Construccions I Reformes Integrals SL, or Reformas Lluca Les Corts SL. Each is a real, sourced row in the target list rather than an invented example, and the choice among the three is a qualifying-call outcome (reachability, revenue-mix confirmation, and — for Delgado & Dilla specifically, whose registered name is the one Catalan-language entry in the whole 128-row extraction — the Catalan-language question §5 below addresses directly), not a decision this document makes in advance of that call.

## 3 · Qualification

The framework the roadmap names is MEDDPICC (Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Paper Process, Identify Pain, Champion, Competition), and it was built for a buying centre where those eight roles are distributed across different people with different incentives — a finance sponsor, a technical evaluator, a procurement officer, an end user. `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` B.3 records what the reference purchase actually looked like: two owners deciding jointly, with no evidence of anyone else in the room, and `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.4.7 names the absence of a controller, a finance director or an IT employee as itself a positive signal for this ICP, not a gap to fill. In an owner-managed SME of eight to forty people, the Economic Buyer, the Champion and the end user are frequently the same one or two people, and MEDDPICC's central assumption — that a Champion sponsors the deal to a separate Economic Buyer, taking on internal risk to do so — has no one on the other side of that sponsorship to persuade. The adaptation below keeps every letter of the framework and restates what each one has to mean here.

**Metrics.** Not a generic ROI slide; the segment's own value-case arithmetic from `06-VALUE-CASE.md`, asked as a question the prospect answers rather than told as a number they receive. The single most diagnostic question, stated directly in `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §3.5's own conclusion for the hardest segment and applicable to all three, is whether the owner **can name one completed job whose margin he could not explain afterwards.** A firm that cannot name one has just told the seller its own Metrics; a firm that can name several has told the seller the deal is close to qualified before a single number has been quoted.

**Economic Buyer.** Usually a duo, not a hierarchy. Where two owners split operations and administration — the pattern `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` records and the product's own permission model is built around — both must be identified and both must agree, because a sale that wins one and loses the other stalls indefinitely rather than closing slowly. Where the firm is a true sole operator (Segment 3's modal case), the Economic Buyer is one person, and the qualification question shifts from "who else needs to agree" to "does this one person have forty owner-hours a year to give the product" (`06-VALUE-CASE.md` §3.5's own adoption-cost line).

**Decision Criteria.** Not a feature checklist scored against named competitors — there is usually no competing product under evaluation, because the incumbent is Excel and a gestoría, not a rival ERP (`02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.3, "the incumbent is not a competitor product"). The real decision criterion, as `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §3.7 states in pricing Segment 3 deliberately below the adviser's own monthly fee, is a **psychological ceiling anchored on the gestoría's retainer**, not a value-for-money comparison against alternative software.

**Decision Process.** Informal and short by construction, precisely because the committee, procurement function and written specification that lengthen a normal enterprise Decision Process are the same organisational features `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.6 type 3 names as reasons to decline a firm (a controller, a payroll bureau, a comparison against named commercial products). The process for this buyer is a phone call, a demonstration, and a decision inside days rather than committee cycles — but exactly because there is no formal process, a stalled deal has no internal mechanism pushing it forward either, so the seller has to supply the cadence a committee would otherwise provide.

**Paper Process.** There is usually no legal department and no procurement portal. The paper that actually moves — and the piece of Paper Process this ICP takes seriously in a way a generic SME might not — is the GDPR/LOPDGDD processor agreement `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.5's sixth hard gate names directly: real financial and employee data requires a signed processor agreement and a stated retention position before a pilot's baseline can even be measured, and a firm unwilling to sign either is disqualified from being a design partner regardless of how well it scores everywhere else.

**Identify Pain.** Use `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md`'s own openers verbatim as the diagnostic instrument, segment-adjusted: the Verifactu opener for the dated, universal pain every S.L. and S.A. shares, and the quarterly-reconstruction opener for the pain that is felt every ninety days regardless of any legal deadline. Both are written to be falsifiable in the room — the prospect either recognises the described Tuesday or does not — which is a better qualification signal than any question asked directly.

**Champion.** This is the letter that most visibly breaks, and the adaptation has to be stated rather than assumed away. `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.4.2 calls a **named administrative person who is not the operations owner** the single most reliable organisational qualifier, precisely because MEDDPICC's Champion function — someone inside the account who carries the case internally — needs a second person to carry it to. Where that second person exists (most of Segment 1 and Segment 2), they are the Champion in the framework's own sense, and their engagement, not the owner's, is the leading indicator. Where no second person exists — Segment 3's modal firm, and `01-MARKET-MAP.md` §4.1's own finding that the operations-versus-administration distinction is "inert" below roughly eight people — there is no one to carry the case internally, because the Economic Buyer, the user and the only candidate Champion are one exhausted person at the end of a working day. The honest adaptation is that Champion, in that case, is not a role a second person fills; it is a test of whether the owner will advocate the tool to **his own future evening self**, and the tell is the same one used for Metrics above — a named job whose margin he could not explain.

**Competition.** Not another ERP vendor in the overwhelming majority of deals — `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.3 and `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §4 both name the real incumbent as a spreadsheet and a gestoría — but the qualification question has to go one step further than that concession, because the sharpest live competitive threat identified anywhere in this roadmap is the **gestoría's own emerging compliant billing tool**, not a hypothetical rival. Scoring Competition honestly for this buyer means asking, directly, what the prospect's own adviser has told them about 2027 — because if the answer is "my gestoría is already handling it," that answer should be believed and tested, not argued past.

## 4 · Sales cycle

**Length. Assumption, no source: four to eight weeks from qualified first contact to a signed contract for Segment 1 and Segment 2 Tier A firms carrying a live trigger (the Verifactu deadline, a recent quarterly-close pain point, or a named margin loss); six to twelve weeks where no such trigger is freshly felt.** The reasoning behind the range, not just the range itself: the absence of a procurement committee (§3 above) argues for the shorter end, but two owners who must agree jointly and a craft culture that — per the outreach persona `docs/market/barcelona/ROADMAP.md`'s own Phase 9 brief states plainly — "is sold to constantly, and can smell a template," argues against compressing it further. A sales cycle this short by enterprise standards is still long against the segment's own rhythm: Segment 1's central-case payback is six months and Segment 2's is five, so a cycle above roughly two months starts to eat a visible fraction of year-one payback before the tenant has even signed.

**Stages, exit criteria, and the artefact that moves each one.**

1. **Trigger identification and first contact.** Exit criterion: the prospect recognises the specific pain named in one of `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md`'s openers as their own Tuesday, not a generic software pitch. Moving artefact: the opener itself, delivered as the first-touch message `docs/market/barcelona/ROADMAP.md`'s Phase 9 will draft, addressed to a named, verifiable fact about the firm rather than a template.
2. **Qualifying call.** Exit criterion: the hard-gate checklist of `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.5 is answered — revenue mix (project versus recurring maintenance), incumbent ledger status, public-work exposure, bank, legal form, and both Economic Buyers identified where two exist. Moving artefact: a completed, dated qualification record against that six-item hard-gate list, because a deal that skips this stage is a deal that discovers a hard gate after money has been spent rather than before.
3. **A demonstration on the prospect's own data — the pivot stage, addressed honestly below.** Exit criterion: both Economic Buyers have seen a document — a quotation, a contract, or an invoice — bearing their own firm's name, tax identifier and at least a sample of their own catalogue, not a generic demo tenant's. Moving artefact: the demo tenant itself, and the PDF documents it produces.
4. **Data-protection sign-off.** Exit criterion: the processor agreement is signed and a retention position is stated, satisfying `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.5's sixth hard gate before any real data moves. Moving artefact: the signed agreement, which for this buyer is very often the entire "Paper Process" the deal will ever produce.
5. **Onboarding.** Exit criterion: the smoke test named in `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §1 passes — a quotation, a contract and an invoice, each issued and downloaded — and a handover note is delivered. Moving artefact: the handover note itself, and the first real (not demo) document the firm has issued from the product.
6. **Conversion to paid, and the first quarter closed inside the product.** Exit criterion: the firm's first quarterly filing cycle is assembled from the product rather than reconstructed from paper — the moment `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md`'s second-ranked trigger stops being a monthly threat. Moving artefact: the export package for that first quarter, which doubles as the artefact a reference conversation can be built around.

**The honest assessment of a demo on their own data, given Phase 7's answer.** `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §3 measured, rather than assumed, what a signed-in tenant costs today: **8 hours central for Segment 3, 11 for Segment 1, 16 for Segment 2**, of which 6.5 hours are fixed for every tenant regardless of size — provisioning a stack, creating the tenant row, issuing accounts, entering identity and clause blocks, clearing the shipped demo seed, running the money-chain smoke test, and writing the handover note — at a fully loaded €70 an hour, that is **€560 to €1,120** per tenant, and calendar time of **three to ten working days**, dominated not by engineering effort but by waiting on the customer's own tax identifier, bank export and catalogue. Read narrowly, this is genuinely cheap: a demo on a prospect's real data costs roughly what a single hour of the owner's own time is worth in the value case it is trying to prove, and it is well inside the range an onboarding fee already priced at €900–€2,400 comfortably covers, with 36–53% of headroom at the central estimate per `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §5. **On that reading, a demo on their data is not just affordable, it is close to free relative to the deal it is meant to close, and it should be the default pivot artefact rather than an expensive concession reserved for the largest prospects.**

But the honest answer does not stop at the per-unit cost, and the roadmap is explicit that it should not: `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §4 records, as items 8 and 9 of its unsoftened verdict, that **a tenant today is baked into the deployed container image rather than read as data at runtime**, so bringing a new tenant live rides behind a CI build and a release rather than existing independently, and that **the authorisation layer above the database assumes one customer per deployment** — there is no tenant claim on a session token, and a cross-tenant request is not yet a case any test asserts must fail. Put plainly: the cost of building one demo tenant is cheap and measured; the cost of running several at once, safely, on the infrastructure that exists today, has not been paid down, and until it is, **two customers cannot yet safely share one stack**. The practical consequence for this sales cycle is a throughput constraint, not a cost constraint: **assumption, reasoned directly from `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §4's items 8 and 9 — at most two to three concurrent demo-or-pilot tenants should be run at any one time, each on its own dedicated stack (at a measured infrastructure cost of about €9 a month per stack, per `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §5, which is not the binding constraint), until the runtime-tenant and session-isolation fixes land, which `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §4 prices at two to four weeks and three to six weeks of engineering respectively.** This is exactly why Sequencing (§2) matters commercially and not only strategically: running the entire Tier A cohort through the demo-on-their-data pivot simultaneously is not a sales-execution question, it is presently an infrastructure limit, and the correct response is to stagger Tier A outreach — Segment 2 first, in small batches — rather than to promise every prospect a parallel demo before the isolation work is done.

## 5 · Language

**Catalan is required, not merely appreciated, in one specific and identifiable place: legally binding customer documents at a firm whose own working language of business is Catalan.** `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.5's tenth soft gate states this directly — a prospect requiring Catalan for such documents, with no native reviewer available for the machine-authored Catalan asset, caps the fit score at 80 and, more importantly, **must be disclosed before a contract is signed, not after.** `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` C.3 records the underlying fact this gate exists to manage: the Catalan dictionary in the product today was machine-authored and is awaiting a native reviewer, which `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §1.6's fourth argument against buying calls "a disclosure to make before a Catalan-speaking prospect reads a contract, not after." Selling Catalan documents as a settled differentiator to exactly the firm for whom Catalan is legally load-bearing would be the single most avoidable error this plan can name.

Two facts from the extraction itself sharpen where this actually bites. First, `05-TARGET-LIST.md`'s Tier A write-up for Delgado & Dilla Construccions I Reformes Integrals SL notes that its registered legal name is written in Catalan — "the only one of 128 rows where this is true" — a genuinely observed fact rather than an inference, and exactly the kind of firm where the qualifying call must ask, directly, whether contracts and accepted quotations are issued in Catalan today. Second, and in the opposite direction, `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §4.2's fifth decline condition notes that **registered trade-name language is a weak proxy for working language**: the overwhelming majority of Tier A and B firms carry Spanish-language trading names even though Catalan is very often the spoken language on site and in the owner's own head, which means the honest qualifying question is about the documents the firm actually signs and files, not about the language stamped on its registry entry.

Beyond binding documents, Catalan is **appreciated rather than required** in most of the rest of the sale: the internal English-language reporting `CLAUDE.md` itself mandates for the operator is a wholly separate concern from the language the customer is sold in, and `docs/market/barcelona/ROADMAP.md`'s own Phase 9 brief already scopes outreach assets in Spanish and Catalan with English kept for internal review only — this document does not revise that split, it inherits it. Where the implication does reach beyond documents is **who does the selling.** `docs/market/barcelona/ROADMAP.md`'s Phase 9 brief describes the buyer as someone "on site, reads on a phone, is sold to constantly, and can smell a template," and Phase 9's own rule that Catalan "must be written, not translated" applies with equal force to a live sales conversation: a qualifying call or a demonstration run for a Catalan-first owner in a visibly translated register reads as exactly the corporate outsider `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` Part B's Cultural-distance dimension is built to detect, and undoes in one phone call what a well-written opener achieved in an email. The practical conclusion is that **whoever runs the qualifying call and the demonstration for a Catalan-registered or Catalan-first prospect — Delgado & Dilla is the concrete near-term case — should be a genuine Catalan speaker**, not a Spanish-speaking seller working from a translated script, and that this staffing constraint, not a marketing preference, is the real content of "language" in this go-to-market plan.

## 6 · Objection register

Twelve objections a seller will actually hear, in the order they tend to surface across a qualifying call, with the honest answer to each. Objections 3, 4, 5 and 6 are cases where the honest answer is that the firm is not the right fit, drawn directly from `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md`'s hard and soft gates and from the segments `06-VALUE-CASE.md` concedes in whole or in part; objection 12 is a fifth, partial case of the same kind.

| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | "Why would I pay for this when I already have a spreadsheet and a gestoría?"                              | Concede the premise before arguing past it: `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §4.1 shows the incumbent's cash cost (€3,000–€6,500 a year across the three segments) does not fall when you buy this — 86–91% of it survives — so this is additive spend, not a substitution. The case for it is a cash-flow effect on your own margin, not a fee replaced, and the central-case payback is five to eight months depending on your segment.                                                                                                                                                                            |
| 2   | "My gestoría says they'll give me a compliant billing tool for the 2027 rule anyway — why do I need you?" | Take this seriously rather than argue past it — `06-VALUE-CASE.md` names it directly as the sharpest competitive threat in the whole roadmap, because it costs you nothing incremental. Ask to see what your adviser is actually planning and when; if it is real and dated before January 2027, that is a legitimate reason to decline our compliance pitch specifically, even if the rest of the case still stands on the margin-visibility side.                                                                                                                                                    |
| 3   | "We only run two or three jobs at a time."                                                                | **Not the right fit today.** `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md`'s soft gate on job count caps the score here, and `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §3.6 concedes plainly that Excel and a gestoría remain the rational choice below four simultaneous jobs — the product's central value has no one to serve at this scale. | 4   | "It's just me and one guy — my brother-in-law does the rest."                                             | **Not the right fit.** `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.6 names this shape directly and recommends decline: there is no second administrative person, so the operations-versus-administration permission split the product is built around has no one to hold the second half of it.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| 5   | "Most of our work is public — schools, town-hall jobs."                                                   | **Not the right fit within the current planning horizon.** Public-sector work is a hard gate in `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.5: it requires tender-file handling, classification registers and public-sector e-invoicing the jurisdiction pack does not hold today. | 6   | "We already run an accounting package / ERP."                                                             | **Not the right fit, and the honest reason is structural, not competitive.** The product's central claim — one record of truth, no second copy anywhere — is unhonourable next to an incumbent ledger; `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` treats this as a hard gate rather than an integration opportunity, because two systems recording the same facts agree until the day a customer notices they do not.                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| 7   | "Two-thirds of our revenue is maintenance contracts, not project work."                                   | **Not yet the right fit.** There is no recurring-contract object or service-level clock in the product today (`02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.6, "the service-led climate installer with a maintenance book"); a firm earning the majority of its revenue this way is a decline now and a named Tier C candidate once the recurring-contract pack — costed and quantified in `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §6 — is built.                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| 8   | "Can you give me contracts and quotations in Catalan today?"                                              | Partially, and this must be said before a contract, not after. | 9   | "Will this make me fully compliant with the new invoicing law on day one?"                                | Not yet, and this is stated without softening because `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §4.2 states it the same way: the official Verifactu record layout, QR content, event log and submission channel are not built yet. The immutable, gapless, hash-chained invoicing that regime will require is in place; the last mile that connects it to the tax authority's own channel is a dated, bounded, still-open piece of work with the same 1 January 2027 deadline you have.                                                                                                                                       |
| 10  | "How do I know these savings numbers are real for my business, not just a model?"                         | They are not measured on your business — they are a modelled estimate built from named assumptions with explicit ranges (`06-VALUE-CASE.md`'s own method), offered so they can be challenged rather than believed. The cheapest way to test them costs you nothing: pull one completed job file and check what share of third-party spend was actually compared before it was committed, and what share of extras on that job were ever invoiced — those two assumptions carry more of the case than any other single number in the model.                                                             |

The onboarding is still config-only, but the first conversation costs more and converts less often — a fair trade for a firm willing to have that conversation, not a reason to lead with this segment. |
