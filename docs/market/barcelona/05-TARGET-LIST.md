# 05 — TARGET LIST: SCORING AND PRIORITISATION

This document is Phase 5 of the Barcelona market roadmap (`docs/market/barcelona/ROADMAP.md`), written on **2026-09-13**, and it applies the weighted rubric of `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` to every one of the 128 firms in `04-PROSPECTS.jsonl`, adds the trigger detectability of `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md`, and sorts the result into four tiers. Read it as a ranking on sector code, legal form and registry presence, and nothing more, until an enrichment pass confirms the rest: `04-EXTRACTION-QA.md`'s verdict for this phase excludes `municipality` and `province` from scoring outright — both exceeded the 5% sample error threshold the phase brief set, one of them (`municipality`, at 30.8%) for four separate, independent reasons — and finds that every other field except `legal_name`, `trade_name`, `nif` and `cnae` is either unaudited or effectively unpopulated across the whole file. That leaves the four CAGE distance dimensions the Phase 2 rubric asks for resting on evidence for only one and a half of them: Administrative distance can be partly read from a firm's legal form, and Layer distance can be partly read from its CNAE code, but Cultural, Economic and Geographic distance have no reliable field to read from at all. The section immediately below states exactly how that gap was handled, mechanically and the same way for every row, rather than papered over with case-by-case judgement calls that two different analysts would not reproduce. The consequence, stated here so it is not a surprise forty pages in: no firm in this extraction can score above the "Qualified" band on the Phase 2 rubric's own 0–100 scale, because three of five weighted dimensions are honestly unknown for all 128 rows and the rubric's own unknown-scoring rule caps what an unknown can contribute. Tiering therefore does not rank firms against an absolute bar of fit; it ranks them against each other, on the two dimensions the data actually supports, and treats everything else as a named, priced task for the next research pass.

## How the scoring was applied given the QA verdict

**What is load-bearing and what is not.** Per `04-EXTRACTION-QA.md`'s closing verdict, only four of the schema's twenty-two fields survive into this scoring pass as anything more than unweighted colour: `legal_name` and `trade_name` (0% sample error, used here only to parse legal form and sector keywords, never as a scored input in themselves), and `nif` and `cnae` (0% sample error each, but audited on only five and three-to-four observations respectively, so they are used but flagged, never treated as proven). `municipality` and `province` are excluded outright — the QA report found four independent failure mechanisms behind the municipality error rate and recommends `Construcciones y Reformas Coperfi SL` (row 67) be dropped from the pool entirely, because the registry match search turned up is a real company in Gondomar, Pontevedra, on the opposite side of Spain, not a Barcelona prospect at all. This document follows that recommendation literally: row 67 is carried in the master table for completeness and audit trail, marked EXCLUDED, and is not scored or tiered as a live prospect. `founded_year`, `employees_band`, `website`, `email`, `phone` and `review_count` are used below only as unweighted context in individual write-ups — never as a scored input — because the QA sample audited zero to two values for each of them, which is an absence of evidence, not evidence of reliability, and the rubric's own procedural rule (`02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` §0) is explicit that no dimension may be scored above an 8 without a named artefact, let alone scored at all from a field with no audited reliability behind it.

**Cultural, Economic and Geographic distance: the rubric's own unknown rule, applied literally.** `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` §0 states the instrument's second procedural rule in so many words: "an unknown scores 5 and carries a flag, never a guess." Cultural distance is read from a job-cost workbook, a margin answer, a working language and a phone-first habit — none of which this extraction captures for any firm. Economic distance is read from revenue band, project-size distribution and payment terms — all either null or audited too thinly to use. Geographic distance is read from travel time and site count, both of which route through the excluded municipality field. All three dimensions are therefore scored **5 out of 10 for every one of the 127 scorable firms, and flagged PROVISIONAL**, exactly as the rubric instructs for a genuine unknown, rather than estimated from a name, a phone area code, or an inference this document is not entitled to make. Weighted, a flat 5 on Cultural (weight 16), Economic (weight 16) and Geographic (weight 12) contributes a fixed 22.0 points to every firm's total, out of the 100-point scale, before Administrative and Layer distance are considered at all. This is disclosed once here rather than repeated eighty times in the table below, and it is the reason the arithmetic in the next paragraph works out the way it does.

**Administrative distance: legal form, read mechanically from the registered name and cross-checked against the NIF prefix.** `legal_name` is 0% error and fully populated; a firm's corporate suffix is therefore one of the few facts in this file that can be read off the page rather than inferred. A closing "S.L." or "S.L.U." scores 6 — the reference tenant's own form, but capped well below the rubric's 8-or-10 anchors (which require confirmed filings, gestoría relationship, subcontracting-register status and public-work exposure, none of which is observable here) rather than assumed to carry the rest of those indicators for free. "S.A." scores 5, following the rubric's own "acceptable if small" qualifier, which size is exactly what this extraction cannot confirm. A cooperative suffix ("S.C.C.L.") scores 4, which is not a downgrade invented for this exercise — it is the rubric's own anchor-4 language, "a cooperative governance structure with no single administrative owner," applied to a form this extraction can identify with certainty but whose actual governance it cannot see. A civil-partnership suffix ("S.C.P.") scores 3: real, taxable, common in this sector at its smallest end, but a materially different administrative and liability envelope from the reference tenant's S.L., and one the rubric's legal-form table places in "Out" territory for the comunidad-de-bienes family it resembles. A name carrying no legal-form suffix at all — a bare trading name such as "MAYORCASA" or "Reformas Pastoret" — scores 2: not a proven decline, but the presumptive-autónomo condition the ICP's own firmographics table (`02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.1) lists as "Out," and one this extraction gives no way to disconfirm. Where a NIF is present, its first character corroborates the parsed form (B for S.L./S.L.U., A for S.A., J for a civil partnership) in every case checked, which is a small, mechanical confirmation this document treats as raising confidence from Low to Medium, never as raising the score itself.

**Layer distance: CNAE code, read against the ICP's own subclass table, with the coding noise named rather than smoothed over.** Where a four-digit CNAE code is present and maps cleanly onto the ICP's target subclasses — the 43.3x/43.9x finishing family or a renovation-led 41.2x general-construction code — layer distance scores 8, the rubric's own "L0 with one open question that is very likely config" anchor, because sector fit is confirmed but nothing in this extraction confirms the specific configuration questions (accountant export format, bank layout, public-work exposure) that separate a clean L0 from an L0-with-a-question. Where the CNAE code falls in the 43.2x installer family, layer distance scores 6 (the rubric's L1 anchor), because — per Worked Example 3 in `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` §R.7 — an installer's project-work chapters are L0 but its maintenance-revenue share, which this extraction cannot measure, is exactly the fact that turns a strong-looking installer into additive-but-bespoke work under today's unbuilt plugin loader. Two further, smaller groups scored a flat 4: firms whose CNAE reads as 42.x civil engineering, which the ICP firmographics table places explicitly "Out," and one whose CNAE reads as 47.x retail trade, which is not a construction contractor at all — both are named individually below rather than silently dropped, because in at least one case (CNAE 42.99 against a name that reads as masonry) the mismatch looks more like extraction noise than a real sector miss, and that is exactly the kind of fact a paid registry check resolves cheaply. Four rows carry a CNAE code — 4100, 4101, 4102, 4121, and the two-digit "41" — that does not correspond to any class the Spanish CNAE-2009 nomenclature actually defines at that precision (the real subclass is 41.20); rather than discard them, they are read as division-41 general building construction, flagged Medium confidence, because the extraction pipeline's own audit already found unrelated formatting noise in several other fields and a truncated or malformed subclass code is the most parsimonious explanation. Where no CNAE was recovered at all — 82 of 128 rows, the large majority — layer distance is estimated from a keyword in the trading name itself ("Instal·lacions"/"Instalaciones" → installer-inferred, scored 5; "Reformas"/"Reforma"/"Rehabilitació" or "Construcciones"/"Obra" → renovation-or-general-construction-inferred, scored 6; no usable keyword → the same default of 6, on the ground that every row in this file was sourced specifically as a Barcelona-area reformas/construction firm, per `04a-SOURCE-PLAN.md`) and flagged **Low** confidence throughout, because a name is not a registry fact.

**What this means for the total score, honestly stated before the table rather than after it.** Combining the fixed 22.0-point CAGE floor with the best achievable Administrative score (6, for a confirmed S.L.) and the best achievable Layer score (8, for a confirmed target CNAE) yields a ceiling of **63.6 out of 100** — the "Qualified" band (55–69) on the Phase 2 rubric's own scale, not "Strong" (70–84) and nowhere near "Ideal" (85–100). No firm in this list can cross into Strong or Ideal on today's evidence, and that is not a finding about these firms' fit; it is a mechanical consequence of scoring three of five dimensions as unknown, which is what an honest application of the rubric to this extraction produces. **Confidence** is reported per firm as High / Medium / Low, and High is never used: it would require an observed value on Cultural, Economic or Geographic distance, and none exists for any of the 127 scorable rows. Medium is reported where both the CNAE-derived layer estimate and the name-derived legal form are directly observed (not inferred from a keyword); Low where either rests on inference. **Hard gates and soft caps** from `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` §R.2 — public-work exposure, an incumbent ledger, recurring-maintenance majority revenue, a UTE contracting vehicle, refusal of the data posture, a second-bank layout, a single decision-maker — cannot be evaluated from this extraction at all, because every one of them requires either a qualifying conversation or a field this file does not carry reliably. None is therefore applied here as a scored deduction; each is instead the specific, named fact a first call or a paid registry pull must establish before a firm currently scored Tier A or B is treated as gate-clear. **No firm in this extraction shows L5-triggering evidence** — a demand to override a tax decision, skip invoice numbers, or add per-tenant custom fields — because L5 is a fact established in a sales conversation that has not yet happened with any of these 128 names, not a fact a registry search can surface. Tier D below accordingly names zero L5 declines; this is reported as a finding, not an omission.

## Scoring table

All 128 rows of `04-PROSPECTS.jsonl`, in file order, split into three tables for readability. Columns follow the phase brief: legal name, NIF, CNAE, legal form (parsed from the name), the layer estimate with its driving requirement, the Administrative score (0–10, the one CAGE dimension partly supported by evidence), the weighted **Total** (0–100, incorporating the fixed 22.0-point Cultural+Economic+Geographic floor described above), overall **Confidence**, the provisional **Tier**, **detected triggers** from `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` (almost uniformly "sector-default only," for the reason given in the next paragraph), and a one-line **rationale**. Cultural, Economic and Geographic distance are not repeated as columns because they are identical for every scorable row — 5/10, PROVISIONAL, no signal — as established above; repeating three constant columns 127 times would not add information, only length.

**On the triggers column.** `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md`'s non-regulatory triggers — a failed inspection, a margin loss, a generational handover, a gestoría change, headcount growth, a new general-contractor client, working-capital stress — are each detectable only from a field this extraction does not carry at any useful density: `directors[]` is empty in all 128 rows, `permits_last_24m` and `public_contracts_count` are empty in all 128, and `review_count` is populated in exactly one row of 128. What every S.L. and S.A. in this list does carry, by virtue of being a Spanish corporate income-tax payer, is the **regulatory** default: the Verifactu certified-billing deadline of 1 January 2027 (111 days away as of the trigger map's writing) and the quarterly Modelo 303/111 filing cycle apply to all of them without any firm-specific detection needed, because they are a function of tax status rather than an externally observable event. That is recorded as "sector-default only" throughout, and a handful of rows carry an additional flag where the extraction itself surfaced a specific fact worth a second look before outreach — a consumer webmail address, a mobile-shaped sole phone number, an email field that duplicates the phone field, or (once) a populated review count — each traceable to a specific finding in `04-EXTRACTION-QA.md`'s whole-file audit.

## Tier A — design partners

Seven firms, not thirty-one. The 31-firm top-scoring bucket (total 63.6, the ceiling this extraction supports) is the pool Tier A is drawn from, but the roadmap is explicit that Tier A is not simply the highest scores — it is the firms whose success is most citable to the rest of the segment. Selection inside the 63.6 bucket therefore ran on a second pass looking for the combination the top-score tie cannot itself distinguish: a populated, prefix-consistent NIF (registry-verifiable without a paid lookup); at least one working contact channel (phone, email or an owned domain — not a marketplace listing); and enough spread across the metropolitan ring that a reference in one of these seven is not simply a restatement of a reference in another. Only three of the thirty-one carry both a NIF and a contact channel; the other four below carry a NIF alone, chosen over same-scoring alternatives for a specific reason named in each write-up. None of the six hard gates in `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` §A.5 can be evaluated from this extraction, so none is claimed clear here — the confirming fact listed for each firm is the fact a first call or a registry pull needs to establish before that firm is treated as gate-clear, not a claim that it already is.

### 1. Gonzvilarsa S.L. — row 16, Sant Joan Despí — NIF B66865254 — CNAE 4399 — Total 63.6 (Medium)

**Why citable.** The best-evidenced row in the entire extraction: CNAE confirmed inside the 43.9x finishing family, S.L., a NIF whose B-prefix is consistent with that form, and — uniquely among the top scorers — an owned domain (`gonzvilarsa.com`) carrying a working contact email rather than a marketplace listing or a directory mirror. That is the one combination in this list that lets a first outreach start from the firm's own material rather than a registry stub.

**The fact that would confirm or demote.** A quotation sample or a first call establishing the operations/administration split (`02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.4.2), current job count, and quoting method. Demoted if the domain turns out to be a lead-generation or reseller front rather than an operating contractor with its own crews, or if a live incumbent accounting package surfaces (hard gate 4).

**Single most likely reason we lose them.** The contact email is generic (`contacto@…`), not addressed to a named person; a cold message to a shared inbox at a firm this size is the easiest kind of outreach to lose to silence.

### 2. A Barcelona Reformas SL — row 39, Barcelona — NIF B63839583 — CNAE 4399 — Total 63.6 (Medium)

**Why citable.** Reachable by both phone and an owned domain, CNAE-confirmed, and broad enough branding ("A Barcelona Reformas," not a founder's surname) to suggest an established multi-job operation rather than a single-owner outfit — a stronger reference story for the segment than a firm whose name is one person's.

**The fact that would confirm or demote.** `04-EXTRACTION-QA.md`'s whole-file audit flags this row's email (`barcelonareformas@yahoo.es`) as the grey-zone case the GDPR posture itself anticipates — plausibly the firm's own published contact, plausibly not. **Single most likely reason we lose them.** The webmail address is itself a soft signal against the sale: a firm that answers commercial enquiries from a free consumer account reads as informal at the exact moment it is being pitched a system that replaces informality with structure, and a first written approach to that address risks never being opened.

### 3. Edificaciones Y Reformas Mollet 2000 SL — row 114, Mollet del Vallès — NIF B62393517 — CNAE 4121 — Total 63.6 (Medium)

**Why citable.** Registry-verifiable, CNAE-confirmed renovation-led general construction, and a "2000" in the trading name consistent with two and a half decades of trading — an established-SME story, not a two-year-old shell, if the implied founding year holds up.

## Tier C — investment cases

Thirteen firms across three named packs, each with the capability it forces and the unlock counted honestly — including the two cases where the honest count argues against building the thing at all. The roadmap's instruction is to name the pack and quantify how many Tier A/B prospects it converts to L0; where that number is small, this document says so rather than inflating a case to justify the section's existence.

### Pack 1 — a recurring-contract and work-order capability for project-led installers (8 firms)

**The pack forced.** `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` §C.3 and the adjacency map (`01-MARKET-MAP.md` §5, rank 3) both name this gap identically: a firm in the CNAE 43.2x installer family quotes its project work in exactly the chapters the reference vertical already models, but a maintenance-revenue share — annual service rounds on systems it installed — needs a recurring-contract object and a work-order spine that do not exist in any capability today. Per `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` §A.6 type 2 ("the service-led climate installer with a maintenance book"), a firm earning the majority of its revenue this way is a hard-gate decline, not a configuration question; a firm earning a minority can, per Worked Example 3 (§R.7), be sold today with the maintenance share left outside the system for a first year, scored L1 and priced as bespoke work until the plugin loader described in §C.2 exists.

**The firms.** Five carry a CNAE code inside the 43.2x family: `FM&M Reformas E Instalaciones S.L.` (row 8, CNAE 4324), `Reformas Barcelona Low Cost SL` (row 40, CNAE 4329 — also the one row in the whole file with a populated `review_count`, 355, an unweighted volume signal consistent with a high-throughput, lower-ticket profile worth a revenue-mix question in its own right), `Instalaciones Y Reformas Serrano SL` (row 89, CNAE 4321), `Terramajor Reformas Y Construcciones SL` (row 97, CNAE 4329) and `Reformas El Masnou SL` (row 122, CNAE 4329). Three more carry no CNAE but an unambiguous "Instalaciones" in the trading name: `Izquierdo Instalaciones Y Reformas S.L.` (row 10), `Instalmar Castelldefels S.L.` (row 27) and `Instalaciones Y Reformas Megar SL` (row 99).

**The unlock, quantified.** Directly, eight named firms move from L1-priced-as-bespoke (or an outright hard-gate decline, if the maintenance share turns out to be a majority) to L0 once the recurring-contract capability exists and each firm's revenue mix is confirmed by a qualifying call. Indirectly, and this is the number that actually justifies the investment: `01-MARKET-MAP.md`'s adjacency map counts roughly **14,357 firms in Catalonia** across CNAE 43.21/43.22/43.29 — the largest retrievable adjacent segment Phase 1 identified, of which the eight found in this single 128-row extraction are a visible fraction, not the whole. A pack built for eight named firms is a project; a pack built for a segment of that size, with these eight as the first confirmable customers, is the investment character §C.3 asks for.

### Pack 2 — a civil-engineering and site-preparation bridge (2 firms; recommend against investment)

**The pack forced.** `Proyectos Y Excavaciones Rubi S.L.` (row 36, CNAE 4312, site preparation/demolition) and `Rubinense De Albañileria Y Construcciones S.L.` (row 38, CNAE 4299, civil engineering — a segment `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.1 places explicitly "Out") would each need an asset-and-plant register and a different measurement basis than the chaptered-quote spine the reference vertical is built around — the same L4 pattern `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` §A.6 type 1 names for "the scaffolding and plant-hire firm with a construction code": a plausible construction CNAE sitting on a business whose margin is asset utilisation, not chapter-level purchase decisions.

**The unlock, quantified, honestly.** Two firms out of 128 (1.6%) is below the population threshold `01-MARKET-MAP.md` §5 itself uses to decline landscaping as an adjacency ("too small to carry pack investment on its own"), and one of the two — Rubinense's masonry-sounding name against a civil-engineering CNAE — reads more plausibly as extraction or registry noise than as a genuine population signal; a single registry check could resolve it as an ordinary finishing-trade firm, at which point the true population behind this pack is one. **Recommendation: decline as an investment case.** It is named here, per the roadmap's instruction that any L2–L4 firm be routed to Tier C with the unlock counted, precisely so that count can be seen to argue against building it, rather than left unstated.

### Pack 3 — a promoción/real-estate capability (3 firms; recommend against investment)

**The pack forced.** `Reforma Integral Granollers Interiorisme SL` (row 103, CNAE 4755, retail trade in household furnishings — not construction at all), `Binder Meridian SL` (row 74) and `Group Arfil SL` (row 75) all sit closer to the promotor-constructor type `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` §A.6 type 4 names than to the reference case: a developer or furnishings retailer does not quote and accept in the reference sense, it appoints or sells units, and the capability that would actually fit — scheme-level capitalisation, a sales pipeline of units — is, in that same section's own words, "Tier C at the absolute best."

## CLV assumptions

Julián Villanueva's customer-equity framing treats lifetime value, not first-year revenue, as the unit of account, and the roadmap asks for exactly that: expected annual contract value, expected retention, gross margin after the tailoring cost, and CLV, shown as an explicit table because every number in it will be challenged. Every figure below is an **ASSUMPTION**, and every one is labelled **PROVISIONAL**, because the roadmap's own sequencing makes that unavoidable: Phase 7, not this phase, measures the tailoring cost per layer, which is the dominant unmeasured term in the whole calculation. What follows is built so that a single input — Table 3's tailoring-cost row — is the only thing Phase 7 needs to change, and the final CLV in Table 4 recomputes from that one change without touching anything else.

**Simplifying choice, stated rather than hidden.** No discount rate is applied below; CLV is shown as an undiscounted sum over the expected customer lifetime. A real customer-equity model would discount future years' margin to present value, and Phase 6's payback-period and sensitivity work is the more natural place to introduce that refinement, once a real discount rate assumption (the firm's cost of capital, or a standard SaaS hurdle rate) has a source. Flagged here so the omission is visible, not silent.
