# 10 — DESIGN-PARTNER PILOT SPECIFICATION

This document is Phase 10 of the Barcelona market roadmap (`docs/market/barcelona/ROADMAP.md`), written on **2026-09-13**, and it specifies the pilot offered to the seven Tier A design partners named in `05-TARGET-LIST.md`, sequenced per `08-GTM-PLAN.md` §2 with a renovation-led general building contractor from Segment 2 — Edificaciones Y Reformas Mollet 2000 SL, Delgado & Dilla Construccions I Reformes Integrals SL, or Reformas Lluca Les Corts SL, the choice being a qualifying-call outcome rather than a decision made here — as the first firm through it. Everything below is written as the standing instrument offered to any of the seven, with the concrete arithmetic worked for a Segment 2 partner because that is the pilot most likely to run first; a Segment 1 or Segment 3 partner's pilot substitutes that segment's own numbers from `06-VALUE-CASE.md` without changing the structure. Nothing here has been agreed with a named firm. This is the offer as it will be put to one, and the term sheet at the end is written so that a lawyer could turn it into a contract without a further drafting pass.

---

## What the partner gets and what they give

**What the partner gets.** A running, signed-in tenant of the product, branded to the firm, with its own series, clause blocks and catalogue, producing real quotations, contracts, progress valuations and invoices in Spanish, Catalan and English PDF, Word and Excel — the same artefact `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §4 measured on the probe tenant, not a demonstration shell. The partner gets direct input into what gets built next: every gap the pilot surfaces is logged and, where it changes a roadmap priority, the partner is told so by name in the write-up, which is a genuine form of leverage a paying customer of a mature product does not get. The partner gets a named point of contact for the duration of the pilot, a written baseline-and-outcome report at the end regardless of whether the pilot converts, and — this is stated plainly because it is easy to promise vaguely and hard to keep — the pilot price set out below, which is lower than list and locked in writing before any data moves.

**What the partner gives, and in what quantity.**

_Data access._ Before onboarding, and strictly for the purpose of measuring the pre-install baseline (see Success Metrics below): read-only access to the tax identifier, fiscal address, and bank export format the firm actually uses; the current price catalogue or a representative sample of it; a sample of completed job files sufficient to reconstruct, per job, the third-party spend, the quotations issued against it, and any extras raised — `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §2's own method requires this, since every euro figure in that document is built from a physical quantity, a price and a capture rate, and the physical quantity has to come from the firm's own files rather than from the segment average. During the pilot: the same categories of data the product needs to run — customer records, project and progress data, supplier and subcontractor records, banking data for reconciliation, and, if the firm's labour hours are tracked in the product, site-worker time entries and approvals under the labour module. This is real financial and employee data, not the anonymised or synthetic data used everywhere else in this roadmap, and the data-protection posture below governs it accordingly. Nothing is asked for beyond what running the tenant and measuring the three agreed metrics requires; in particular, no data about the firm's own customers' or subcontractors' individuals is requested beyond what already exists inside the firm's own quotations, contracts and invoices.

_Time._ Two commitments, kept separate because they behave differently. **Before onboarding, a one-off baseline session of four to eight hours**, split between a structured intake conversation (`07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §3 prices this step at 0.5–3.0 hours for the identity and configuration values alone) and pulling the job-file sample the baseline measurement needs — this is the step a pilot with no pre-measurement cannot skip, because a metric measured only after go-live has nothing to be compared against. **During the ninety-day pilot, an ongoing commitment modelled directly on `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §2.5's Segment 2 adoption-effort assumption of ninety administrative hours and twenty-five owner hours across a first quarter**, which spreads to approximately seven administrative hours and two owner hours a week, front-loaded in weeks one to four (configuration, data entry, first quotations) and tapering to two to three hours a week by week eight as the tenant becomes the system of record rather than a new thing being learned. This is stated as an assumption with no independent source for this specific firm, and the pilot's own weekly check-in (see Duration and exit criteria) is where it is corrected against what the partner actually experiences, rather than defended in the abstract.

_Reference rights._ The partner is asked, not required, to be named as a reference once the pilot has produced a result worth citing — and the two are kept structurally separate: agreeing to be a design partner obliges nothing about being quoted publicly, and the two decisions are made at different times by design. **Consent mechanism.** No quote, statistic, screenshot, or the partner's name is used in any outreach, case study or sales conversation with a third party without a specific, written, opt-in confirmation obtained after the fact and describing exactly what will be shown — this is the same discipline `docs/market/barcelona/ROADMAP.md`'s Phase 9 mandate already enforces with its `[REQUIRES CUSTOMER CONSENT]` placeholder convention, extended here from a hypothetical testimonial to a real one. Three separate consent tiers are offered, and a partner may grant one, several, or none: (1) anonymised aggregate use — "a Segment 2 renovation contractor in the Baix Vallès reduced its quarter-close time by X hours," no name; (2) named, non-quantified use — the firm's name and trade, no specific euro or hour figure; (3) named and quantified use — the firm's name and a specific, partner-approved number, which requires the partner to see and approve the exact sentence before it is used anywhere. Consent for any tier is revocable in writing at any time, including after material has already been used in a live conversation, in which case that material is withdrawn from future use (existing printed or emailed material already sent cannot be recalled, and this limitation is stated to the partner up front rather than glossed over). Reference rights are entirely independent of the kill criteria below: a pilot that is killed on the metrics can still, with consent, generate an honest reference about what was learned, and a pilot that succeeds generates no reference at all without a separate, explicit opt-in.

## Duration and exit criteria

**Duration: ninety days, aligned to one calendar quarter**, because the artefact that proves the product rather than merely running it is a completed quarterly tax-filing cycle assembled from the system rather than reconstructed from paper — `08-GTM-PLAN.md` §4 names exactly this as the sixth and final stage of the sales cycle, "the export package for that first quarter, which doubles as the artefact a reference conversation can be built around." A pilot shorter than one quarter cannot produce that artefact at all; a pilot longer than one quarter delays the go/no-go decision past the point where `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §3's own calendar estimate (three to ten working days to a signed-in tenant) says it is needed.

**Structure inside the ninety days.** Days 1–7: baseline capture (the pre-install session above) and the qualifying-call hard-gate checklist of `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` A.5 answered in writing, before any configuration begins. Days 8–17: onboarding — the measured `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §3 Segment 2 central estimate of sixteen hours, spread across roughly two weeks to allow for the customer's own tax identifier, bank export and catalogue to arrive, which the same document names as the actual bottleneck rather than engineering effort. Days 18–24: the smoke test named in `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §1 — a quotation, a contract and an invoice, each issued and downloaded — plus a written handover note. Days 25–83: live use, with a short check-in every two weeks to review the time-commitment assumption above against reality and to flag anything approaching a kill criterion early rather than at the ninety-day mark. Days 84–90: the first full quarterly close attempted from the product, the outcome report written, and the go/no-go decision made.

**Exit criteria — what "done" means, stated before the metrics that measure it.** The pilot exits successfully into a paid conversion when all four of the following hold: (a) the smoke test has passed and remains passing; (b) at least one of the three success metrics below shows a measurable, positive movement against its captured baseline; (c) the first quarterly filing cycle has been assembled, even partially, from the product rather than wholly reconstructed from paper; and (d) no kill criterion (below) has been triggered. The pilot exits into a documented no-convert outcome — not a failure to be hidden, but a recorded finding — when the ninety days elapse without (b) or (c), or when any kill criterion fires. Either exit produces the written report; only the successful exit produces a pricing conversation about continuing.

## Success metrics

Exactly three, and each is one of the three operational failures `06-VALUE-CASE.md` costs for the partner's own segment — the roadmap's own instruction that "a pilot with no pre-measurement can only produce opinion" is honoured by measuring each metric on the firm's own numbers, with the **baseline** captured before a single configuration change is made, not estimated from the segment averages that carry the rest of this roadmap. For a Segment 2 partner, the three failures and their pilot-metric form are:

**Metric 1 — the share of third-party purchase decisions made without a recorded comparison, tied to Failure 1 in `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §2.1 (modelled at €40,900 a year, of which €36,000 is the uncompared-and-overpaid share of €2.4m in subcontractor and material spend).** Baseline measurement method: during the pre-install baseline session, pull the last six to ten completed or in-progress job files and, for each, determine whether the largest three purchase decisions on that job (by euro value) were made against more than one priced alternative, and whether the chosen price and its date are recorded anywhere durable. The **baseline** figure is the percentage of those decisions with no recorded comparison, stated as a fraction (for example, "11 of 24 large purchase decisions across eight jobs had no recorded comparison, a 46% baseline uncompared rate") rather than as a euro figure, because the euro figure depends on the overpayment-rate assumption `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §2.1 itself flags as its least evidenced number — the pilot is the first chance to measure that number rather than assume it. At day 90, the same measurement is repeated on jobs run inside the pilot period using `@repo/capability-sourcing`'s comparison record, and the movement in that percentage is the metric.

**Metric 2 — the euro value of executed work sitting un-valued or extras never invoiced at any point in time, tied to Failure 2 in `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §2.2 (modelled at €61,500 a year, the largest single failure in the segment and, per §2.4, the one with the highest capture rate at 70% because it is arithmetic the system performs rather than a habit the owner must form).** This is deliberately the lead metric for the reference this pilot is meant to manufacture, per `08-GTM-PLAN.md` §2's own reasoning that a capability the product executes is a more citable outcome than one that depends on the owner changing how he behaves. Baseline measurement method: during the pre-install session, take one job currently in progress and reconcile, by hand, what has actually been executed on site against what has been invoiced to date, recording the gap in euros and the number of days it has stood uncorrected; do the same for the count and euro value of verbal or informally agreed extras on the same job that have not yet appeared on an invoice. That reconciliation, done once, by hand, on one real job, is the **baseline**. At day 90, the same job (or, if it has closed, the nearest equivalent job run inside the pilot) is reconciled using the product's `invoiceBases` progress-valuation engine and the `change-orders` variation record, and the reduction in both the euro gap and the days it stood uncorrected is the metric.

**Metric 3 — administrative hours spent reconstructing one quarterly VAT and withholding filing from paper, tied to Failure 3 in `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §2.3 (the "quarterly reconstruction" component, modelled at four days of administrative time and one day of owner time per quarter, part of a €21,400 annual total).** Baseline measurement method: at the pre-install session, ask the administrative person (or the owner, in a firm without one) to state, from memory and cross-checked against the gestoría's own invoice for that quarter if available, how many hours the most recent completed quarter's Modelo 303/111 preparation actually took, broken into gathering paper, re-typing, and the gestoría's own back-and-forth. That figure, however imprecise, is the **baseline** — `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md`'s own second-ranked trigger names this exact Tuesday, and asking the question in the same words as that trigger's opener is itself part of the qualification, per `08-GTM-PLAN.md` §3's "Identify Pain" adaptation. At day 90, the hours actually spent assembling the pilot quarter's filing from the product — timed, not recalled, because the pilot itself is the instrument that can time it — is compared against the baseline, and the reduction in hours is the metric.

A partner in Segment 1 or Segment 3 substitutes that segment's own three failures and euro figures from `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §1 or §3 in the same structure; the method — a physical quantity, measured by hand before anything is installed, and the same measurement repeated the same way at day 90 — does not change. No metric in this section is a system-reported statistic the product simply logs; each requires a real, if rough, before-measurement taken from the partner's own files, because a number the product only starts recording from day one is not a baseline, it is a starting point with nothing behind it.

## Pricing during and after

**During the ninety-day pilot: a flat one-time fee of €900, no monthly subscription.** This is deliberately not free. `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §2.7 and `08-GTM-PLAN.md` §3's Paper Process section both treat a signed commercial term, however small, as part of what qualifies a partner as serious rather than merely curious — a firm unwilling to pay anything is a firm that has not yet felt the pain the pilot exists to measure. The figure is set with reference to `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §3's measured Segment 2 onboarding cost of €1,120 at the central estimate (sixteen hours at €70 an hour): €900 recovers 80% of the measured delivery cost, a discount of €220 against actual cost that is the pilot's own genuine concession, rather than a headline discount off list price that costs nothing to grant. The remaining €220 plus the ongoing hosting cost of roughly €9 a month (`docs/HETZNER-SETUP.md`, and see Concurrency and Infrastructure below) is the factory's own investment in the reference it is trying to manufacture, and is accounted as such rather than hidden inside a "free pilot" framing that would misstate what a successful outcome is worth to either side.

**After a successful pilot: standard Segment 2 pricing at a locked-in design-partner discount for the first twelve months, then list.** `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §2.7 recommends per-site pricing for Segment 2 at €60 per active site per month, floored at €400 and capped at €900 a month (€6,480 a year at the modelled nine concurrent sites). The design-partner discount is 25% off both the floor and the cap for the first twelve months of the paid relationship — a €300 floor and a €675 cap, landing at roughly €4,860 a year at the modelled site count — reverting to full list price from month thirteen. The discount is offered in exchange for the reference rights above, not as a permanent price, and is stated as a fixed, written twelve-month term precisely so it cannot drift into an indefinite discount that a later, list-price customer could point to as evidence of an unstated real price. A second sibling entity under the same ownership, if one exists, is priced at 50% of whatever tier applies, per `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §1.7's general rule that this is "a two-tenant sale at nearly one tenant's delivery cost." If the pilot exits into a documented no-convert outcome, the €900 fee is not refunded — it purchased the ninety days of delivery and support already provided — but no further payment is due and the data-deletion clock in Data Protection below starts immediately.

**Why not free, stated once rather than argued twice.** A pilot priced at zero signals that the product's own economics do not need to be respected even by the firm choosing to trial it, which is a strange thing to signal to a prospect being pitched, in the very same conversation, a value case built entirely on the discipline of pricing things correctly. €900 is small enough to be a rounding error against the modelled €63,000 of annual captured value in `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §2.4, and large enough to filter out a firm that is merely curious rather than in genuine pain.

## What we learn, mapped to the layer model

Every pilot is also, and more durably than any single sale, a controlled test of the layer-distance claims `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` Part C and `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` make on the strength of one probe tenant and a codebase reading. This is what a real Tier A pilot tests at each layer.

**L0 — tenant configuration only.** The pilot is the first time the measured L0 onboarding hours (`07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §3: 16 hours central, 11–26 interval, for Segment 2) are spent against a real firm's real catalogue and real opening balances rather than the roadmap-probe tenant's placeholder data. Whichever of the interval's variance drivers actually dominates for this firm — catalogue size, opening-balance migration, or waiting on the customer's own paperwork — is now a measured fact about one real firm rather than an estimate ranked by plausibility, and it either confirms or corrects the central estimate the whole marginal-economics model in `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §5 is built on.

**L1 — additive plugin.** The pilot tests, on a live firm, whether anything the partner asks for in its first ninety days is genuinely additive and isolated rather than something that turns out to need a vertical or jurisdiction change once specified precisely — the gap `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` §C.2 names between what looks additive from a distance and what an unbuilt plugin loader would actually have to support. If the pilot never surfaces an L1 request at all, that absence is itself informative: it would suggest the L0 boundary is wider in practice than the rubric currently credits it for at least this segment.

**L2 — new or extended vertical pack.** Segment 2's own defining risk, named directly in `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §2.4's capture-rate table, is that the progress-valuation and change-order mechanics work as arithmetic on the reference vertical's eighteen-chapter taxonomy but have never been tested against a second firm's actual chapter structure. The pilot is that test: if the partner's own quotation taxonomy diverges from the reference tenant's in a way that breaks the baseline-versus-executed computation, that is a concrete, named L2 gap rather than a hypothetical one, and it is exactly the kind of fact `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §6 says the Tier C analysis could not see because it was built from sector adjacency rather than from a real tenant's data.

**L3 and the L3-bridge — jurisdiction pack and the Spain × construction bridge.** The pilot is the first real test of two things `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §2.4 and §2.9 both flag as claimed at high capture but conditional: the Verifactu-facing tax lines, which are correct in the tables but not yet connected to an official submission channel, and the Libro de Subcontratación, REA and coordination-of-activities objects, which `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §5 costs as the single highest-payback investment on the whole board (one converted tenant) precisely because no real firm has yet been run against it. If the pilot partner passes any subcontracted work to a third party during the ninety days — which a Segment 2 general contractor at 60% third-party spend is likely to do — the pilot will show, concretely, whether the generic dated-document workaround `06-VALUE-CASE.md` §2.4 already flags as "not the authorised book a coordinator asks for" is a survivable gap for ninety days or an immediate, disqualifying one.

**L4 — new capability.** The pilot is a direct test of `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §5's single largest named assumption without a source: that the supported bank statement importer covers at most one in five Spanish construction SMEs' primary banking relationship, blocking roughly thirty of the thirty-seven Tier A and B firms. Whichever bank the pilot partner actually uses answers, for one real case, whether that firm needs the N43 adapter `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §5 prices at fifty hours and a two-tenant payback — the single most cheaply falsifiable claim this pilot can test, because the answer is a fact about one firm's bank rather than a judgement call.

**L5 — kernel change.** No pilot is expected to surface an L5 requirement, and none is designed to test for one; if a partner demands something that would require overriding a tax decision, skipping invoice numbers, or adding a per-tenant custom field outside the resolver, `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md`'s own instruction applies without exception — that partner is declined, mid-pilot if necessary, and the decline is itself logged as a finding rather than quietly absorbed as scope creep.

## Concurrency and infrastructure

**At most two pilots run at the same time, each on its own dedicated stack.** `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §4 measured, rather than assumed, why: a tenant today is baked into the deployed container image rather than read as data at runtime, so bringing a new tenant live rides behind a CI build and a release; and the authorisation layer above the database — no tenant claim on the session token, environment accounts merged into every tenant's membership, no test yet asserting a cross-tenant request is refused — assumes one customer per deployment. `08-GTM-PLAN.md` §4 states the consequence for sales in one sentence worth repeating here in full because it is the honest core of this section: "two customers cannot yet safely share one stack." That is not a caveat to soften in front of a prospect; it is the fact objection 11 in `08-GTM-PLAN.md` §6 already answers this way, and this document answers it the same way rather than differently for an internal audience: a pilot partner is told, before signing anything, that their tenant runs alone.

**The infrastructure posture, concretely.** Each pilot tenant is provisioned on its own Hetzner Cloud server, in the Falkenstein or Nuremberg location per `docs/HETZNER-SETUP.md`'s own instruction to keep personal and tax data inside the EU, with its own backup schedule and its own Cloudflare R2 backup bucket, at the measured running cost of about €9 a month per stack (`docs/HETZNER-SETUP.md`; `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §5 confirms this is not the binding cost constraint — support hours are). No pilot tenant shares a database, a session store, or an access-control boundary with any other tenant, pilot or paying, which is the safest configuration available today precisely because it is the only one that does not depend on the isolation fixes below having landed.

**Why two, not the “two to three” `08-GTM-PLAN.md` §4 allows, and what changes the number.** Two is the conservative end of that range, chosen because a pilot — unlike a mature paying tenant — carries the additional load of the baseline-and-outcome measurement work above, which competes for the same limited engineering and support attention `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §5 already prices at 36 hours in year one for a Segment 2 tenant. Running a third pilot concurrently would mean either thinning that support hour budget across three firms at once — directly threatening the exit criteria of all three — or delaying the runtime-tenant and session-isolation fixes `07-TAILORING-ECONOMICS.md` §4 prices at two to four weeks and three to six weeks of engineering respectively, which are the fixes that would raise the safe concurrency ceiling in the first place. The number is reviewed, not fixed forever: once both fixes land and a cross-tenant-refusal test exists and passes, the concurrency limit is expected to rise, and that review is itself logged as a dated decision rather than left to drift upward informally.

## Kill criteria

The conditions under which this pilot stops are decided here, in writing, before the first pilot begins, precisely so that stopping is a decision already made rather than one argued about under the pressure of a specific relationship six weeks in. Each kill criterion below names who decides and by when.

1. **A cross-tenant data exposure, a security incident, or any unauthorised access to the pilot partner's data.** Decided by the operator, immediately on discovery, no later than 24 hours after detection. The pilot is suspended pending a written incident account and, if the incident touched personal data, the breach-notification clock in Data Protection below starts at the same moment, not after the kill decision.
2. **The smoke test — a quotation, a contract and an invoice, each issued and downloaded — fails to pass by day 24 (seven days past its scheduled slot in the Duration section above).** Decided by the engineering lead at the day 24 checkpoint. A single slip inside that window is not itself a kill; failing to pass by day 24 is, because it means the tenant cannot yet produce the artefacts every later stage of the pilot depends on.
3. **By day 75 (two weeks before the pilot's scheduled end), none of the three success metrics shows any measurable movement against its captured baseline, in either direction.** Decided by the operator at the day 75 checkpoint that the Duration section already schedules. This is deliberately a checkpoint before the final day, so that a pilot heading toward a flat outcome is called honestly with time left to understand why, rather than allowed to expire quietly at day 90.
4. **The partner cannot sustain the stated time commitment (roughly seven administrative and two owner hours a week) for two consecutive two-week check-in periods, and a revised, lower commitment cannot be agreed at the check-in.** Decided jointly by the operator and the partner's named contact at the second such check-in. This protects the partner as much as the pilot: a firm that cannot give the product the hours it needs to be judged fairly should not be judged on a result the missing hours produced.
5. **The partner reveals, during onboarding or the pilot itself, a hard-gate fact `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` §A.5 disqualifies** — a majority-maintenance revenue mix, an incumbent accounting ledger already in place, material public-sector contracting exposure requiring registers the product does not hold, or a legal form the product's permission model cannot honour without a fit that "has to be argued, not assumed" per `05-TARGET-LIST.md`. Decided by the operator on discovery, and the decision is to stop configuring further rather than to continue past a fact the ICP itself says should have excluded the firm.
6. **The partner withdraws consent to process pilot data under the terms of the processor agreement below, for any reason, at any time.** This is not treated as a negotiable event: the pilot stops within 24 hours of the withdrawal being received, decided by the operator with no discretion to persuade the partner otherwise, because continuing to process data without a valid legal basis is not a commercial risk to weigh, it is not permitted.

A pilot that meets none of the six conditions above and reaches day 90 proceeds to the exit-criteria evaluation in the Duration section, not automatically to a paid conversion — the two are related but distinct decisions, made at the same meeting but recorded separately.

## Data protection

This is now real financial and employee data belonging to an operating SME, not the public, company-level prospecting data `04a-SOURCE-PLAN.md` §5 governs, and the posture below is written as an extension of that section's method — a named legal basis, a named retention clock, a named right of the data subject — applied to a materially higher-stakes category of processing, under the **GDPR** (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) and the LOPDGDD (Ley Orgánica 3/2018).

**Roles.** The pilot partner is the **controller** of the personal data that flows through its tenant during the pilot: its customers' names and contact details on quotations and invoices, its employees' names, hours, approvals and corrections in the labour module if that module is used, and its suppliers' and subcontractors' contact and document data. The factory operating the product is the **processor**, acting solely on the partner's documented instructions, under art. 28 GDPR. This allocation is not a formality: it means the partner, not the factory, decides the purposes and legal basis for processing its own customers' and employees' data (for example, the employment-law basis on which it holds its site workers' hours), while the factory's obligations are the technical and organisational ones art. 28 lists — process only on instruction, ensure confidentiality, implement appropriate security, assist with the controller's own obligations, delete or return data at the end of provision, and permit audits.

**The processor agreement, and the clauses it must carry before any real data moves.** `08-GTM-PLAN.md` §3's Paper Process section already names this as the hard gate that must clear before a pilot's baseline can even be measured, and `08-GTM-PLAN.md` §4's stage 4 makes it a distinct exit criterion of its own, ahead of onboarding. The agreement is signed before the pre-install baseline session, not after, because the baseline session itself is the first real-data processing event. At minimum, per art. 28.3, it states: the subject matter, duration, nature and purpose of the processing, and that it is limited to the ninety-day pilot term stated above unless extended in writing; the type of personal data (customer, employee and subcontractor contact and transactional data as it appears in quotations, contracts, invoices, reconciliations and labour records) and the categories of data subjects; the processor's obligation to process only on the controller's documented instructions, including with regard to any transfer of personal data outside the EU, of which there is none contemplated here; confidentiality commitments binding anyone with access; the technical and organisational security measures required by art. 32, named specifically rather than generically — per-tenant schema isolation, dedicated infrastructure per the Concurrency section above, encrypted credential storage for any integration such as the draft-mailbox feature (see below), and the SHA-256 invoice chain the product already produces as an integrity control, not a confidentiality one, and stated as such; the sub-processor list below, with the controller's general authorisation to it and the right to object to a new one before it is engaged; assistance obligations for data-subject rights requests and for a data protection impact assessment should the controller's own assessment call for one; the deletion-or-return position at the end of the pilot, stated in the Retention paragraph below; breach-notification duties; and an audit right, exercisable on reasonable notice.

**Sub-processors.** Two are engaged for every pilot tenant and are named in the processor agreement rather than left implicit: **Hetzner Online GmbH**, for compute and storage, hosted at its Falkenstein or Nuremberg facility in Germany, chosen specifically per `docs/HETZNER-SETUP.md` to keep personal and tax data inside the EU; and **Cloudflare**, for the object-storage backup layer, provisioned at its EU region per the same setup guide. A third, conditional sub-processor arises only if the partner opts into the mailbox-draft feature described in `apps/web/lib/draft-mailbox.ts`: the partner's own mailbox provider (Gmail, Outlook, Hostinger or another, resolved from the domain the partner supplies), which receives IMAP-written drafts into a folder the partner already owns. This is disclosed for completeness rather than treated as a material sub-processing risk, because of what that module deliberately does not do — its own header comment states the constraint the mandate imposes across this whole project: "there is no SMTP here and no code path that transmits anything to a customer," and a draft written into a folder the operator already controls "has not been emailed to anybody." No sub-processor in this pilot ever transmits a document, a filing or a payment to a third party on the partner's behalf; the product produces documents and, where configured, places unsent drafts in the partner's own mailbox, and every "send," "file," or "pay" action remains a human action taken by the partner in their own tools. This is the direct extension, to a live pilot, of `CLAUDE.md`'s standing rule that the whole codebase is built against — "no real emails/filings/payments — fakes behind ports only" — and it is stated to the partner in exactly those terms during onboarding: nothing the pilot produces is submitted to a bank, a tax authority, or a customer without the partner's own separate, deliberate action.

**Retention and deletion at pilot end.** If the pilot converts to paid, the data continues to be processed under the ongoing commercial agreement and this pilot-specific retention clock does not apply. If the pilot exits into a no-convert outcome, or is killed under any criterion above: the tenant is taken offline within 7 days; a full export of the partner's own data, in the same document formats the product already produces, is offered to the partner within 14 days at no charge, as the art. 20 portability position this processor relationship should honour even though art. 20 speaks to controllers; and all copies of the partner's data are deleted from the dedicated stack and its backups within 30 days of the offline date, with deletion confirmed to the partner in writing. The one exception is data the factory is independently obliged to retain — for instance, the pilot's own baseline-and-outcome measurements, held in anonymised or firm-consented form only per the Reference Rights consent mechanism above, and never held as raw personal data past the 30-day window.

**Employee data in the labour module.** Where the pilot partner uses the labour-hours capability — site-worker time entries, approvals and corrections — that is employee data of the partner's own staff, and the partner remains its controller in exactly the sense described above: it is the partner's obligation, not the factory's, to have a lawful basis toward its own employees for tracking their hours (ordinarily performance of the employment contract or a legitimate interest in accurate payroll and site records) and to have informed those employees that the system exists. The factory's obligation as processor is unchanged by the data being about employees rather than customers — the same security measures, the same sub-processor list, the same retention clock — but it is called out separately here because `docs/market/barcelona/ROADMAP.md`'s own Phase 10 brief names it specifically, and because an owner-manager running a pilot personally is easy to miss as also being, separately, an employer with obligations toward the people the labour module tracks.

**Breach notification.** Any personal data breach affecting the pilot tenant is notified by the factory to the partner (the controller) without undue delay and in any event within 24 hours of the factory becoming aware of it, per the processor agreement's own tighter internal standard sitting inside the art. 33 GDPR framework, which separately requires the controller to notify the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos within 72 hours of the controller itself becoming aware, where the breach is likely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. The factory's notification to the partner states what is known, what is not yet known, and what is being done, and is updated as more is learned rather than delayed until the picture is complete — kill criterion 1 above already ties this obligation to an immediate pilot suspension rather than treating notification as a separate, later step.
