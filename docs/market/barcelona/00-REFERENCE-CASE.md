# 00 — THE REFERENCE CASE: CANEI SUBIRATS, S.L.

This document is Phase 0 of the Barcelona market roadmap (`docs/market/barcelona/ROADMAP.md`): the codification of tenant #1 as a reference case, written on **2026-09-12** and generated entirely from this repository — its code, its packages, its governance logs, the archived client intake under `intake/diorka/`, and the client identity capture under `docs/clients/canei-subirats/`. It answers three questions that the eleven later phases depend on and must not re-litigate: which of the delivered capabilities generalise beyond one company, one sector and one country; what the customer actually bought, reconstructed only from recorded evidence; and what a second customer would therefore be buying. Nothing here is sourced from outside the repository, no customer motive is inferred where the record is silent, and every judgement that rests on inference rather than on a file is marked as an assumption in bold.

---

## How the judgements in this document are made

Phase 0 asks for each capability to be placed in one of four buckets: (i) universal to any project-based SME, (ii) specific to construction and renovation work, (iii) specific to Spain or Catalonia, or (iv) specific to Canei Subirats alone. The instruction is to be strict, and strictness here has a precise meaning: a capability is only called universal if there is an artefact in the repository that demonstrates the knowledge is _absent_ from the reusable part — a package whose stated contract forbids sector or jurisdiction knowledge, a linter that fails the build when that knowledge leaks in, or a test that exercises the capability with no Spanish and no construction inputs at all.

That evidence exists and is unusually strong for a codebase of this age. `packages/boundary-lint` enforces a layer import matrix and a forbidden-literal scan over the kernel and every capability, including comments and doc-strings; `CLAUDE.md` lists the forbidden vocabulary explicitly (partida, medición, certificación, IVA, IRPF, facturas emitidas, paquete gestoría, AEAT, Modelo NNN, and hardcoded locales such as `es-ES` in `Intl.*` calls) and records that the linter has already caught a real locale leak in kernel documentation. So when the eighteen packages under `packages/capabilities/` each declare in their `package.json` description "No jurisdiction or sector knowledge", that is a claim a build gate defends rather than a comment somebody wrote.

There is, however, a second and much less comfortable fact that governs everything below, and it is better stated at the top than discovered in Phase 7. **The product the customer uses today and the factory that is supposed to reproduce it are not yet the same artefact.** The running ERP is `site/erp.html` (about 31,000 lines) over `site/erp-engine.js` (about 12,700 lines): a hand-written, Spanish-labelled, single-document engine. The factory layer — kernel, eighteen capabilities, two packs, CLI — is real, tested and composed per tenant, but `site/erp-ownership.json`, the repository's own machine-readable migration ledger, records **19 of 27 domain areas still owned by `engine` and only 8 owned by `factory`**. The eight already migrated are the graphic annex, bank reconciliation and its matching engine, project economics, scheduling and the Gantt, OCR extraction, the communications templates, and the task-status summary. Every classification in Part A therefore carries two readings: what the architecture _permits_ to transfer, and what is _already_ in a transferable shape. They are cited separately wherever they differ.

**Assumption:** I treat the fifteen capability families named in the Phase 0 prompt as an accurate inventory of what was delivered, because each one is traceable to code in this repository; where the prompt's wording and the repository's wording differ (for example "progress valuations" against the engine's `Certificación de obra`), **I take the repository as authoritative.**

### A.1 Quotations with chapters, line items, versions and a graphic annex

**Universal core, with a bespoke vocabulary and a sector-shaped default taxonomy.** The mechanism — a hierarchical document of groups and priced lines, frozen versions, revisions linked to their predecessor, optional lines kept out of the base total, and selective acceptance — is stated in `@repo/capability-quoting` as "quotes, sections, lines, totals, acceptance. No jurisdiction or sector knowledge", and `intake/diorka/REQUIREMENTS-MAP.md` traces the version and option behaviour to requirement ids QUO-07, QUO-12 and QUO-13. Nothing in that list is construction: an architecture practice, a translation agency or a boat yard quotes the same way.

Three things in this capability are _not_ universal and are the most instructive part of Part A. First, the 18-chapter default catalogue seeded into `@repo/pack-vertical-construction-reformas` comes from BRD Appendix A.2 and reads Demolition and preliminary works, Structure, Masonry, Finishes, Sanitary equipment, Interior joinery, Exterior joinery, Kitchen, Painting, Electrical installation, Air conditioning, Ventilation, Plumbing, Drainage, Telecommunications, Fire protection and Optional works. That is renovation work itemised, and it is correctly located in a vertical pack rather than in the capability. Second, the words the screens use are Canei's own: `ASSUMPTIONS.md` S23 records a client-mandated, operator-confirmed rename on 18 August 2026 in which chapter became **Partida** and line item became **Subpartida** across screens, toasts and printed documents in all three languages, while the code identifiers deliberately stayed `chapter*`. That is a label layer doing its job, but it means the customer's vocabulary is one layer of data away from the product and a second customer may well use the conventional reading. Third, the graphic annex is the one part of the quote already migrated into a capability: `site/erp-ownership.json` records `budget-graphic-annex` as factory-owned in `@repo/capability-docs` (`src/annex.ts`), deciding grouping by chapter and line number, correlative numbering when a line carries several pictures, pagination and reference marks, from a list of images it is handed — never touching bytes and never deciding eligibility. **Assumption:** the annex generalises to any quote that needs photographs, which is most on-site trades and very few desk trades.

### A.2 A priced catalogue

**Universal.** `@repo/capability-catalogue` describes itself as a register of work and material items with code, chapter, unit and cost or price, plus room templates that expand into quote lines; `@repo/capability-suppliers` holds effective-dated purchase prices per item with source and optional discount, resolving a net price at a date. The only sector-shaped detail is the unit vocabulary (square metre, linear metre, lump sum) and the room-template notion, both of which are data — `tenants/diorka/tenant.yaml` carries a "Standard bathroom" template and five catalogue items as configuration, not code. The dated, sourced, per-supplier price with a discount is a generic answer to BRD pain point P3 ("no controlled view of latest price, discount, supplier, validity date and prior purchase evidence") and applies to any business that buys on negotiated terms.

### A.3 Contracts with clause blocks, signature and instalments

**Universal mechanism; the clause content is jurisdictional and the instalment behaviour is the sharpest engineering in the area.** A contract of reusable clause blocks, a signature that freezes what was agreed, and a payment schedule is not a construction idea. Two recorded decisions show where the care went. `ASSUMPTIONS.md` 181 records that milestone invoices were charging tax twice because the contract stores a gross total and the milestone pane compared against it — a bug class that exists in any instalment contract. `ASSUMPTIONS.md` S34 records that the language a document speaks is resolved once, through one chain (explicit choice, then the job's accepted quote, then the customer's own preference, then the company default) and then **frozen with the signature**, on the stated reasoning that the customer signed the words they were shown. That rule is jurisdiction-free and is worth more to a second customer than the clause text, which is not: warranty periods, validity periods and exclusions are Spanish-law-shaped and, in Canei's case, carry their own commercial promise (`docs/clients/canei-subirats/BRAND.md` §1: two years on renovations, six months on repairs).

### A.4 Projects with Gantt scheduling, baseline, critical path and S-curve

**Universal, and already in transferable shape.** `site/erp-ownership.json` records `scheduling-gantt` and `project-economics` as factory-owned; `docs/worklog/WORKLOG.md` sessions 5, 6 and 10a record a scheduling capability with calendar and critical-path method and baselines, an SVG Gantt with drag, resize and linking, and Gantt-derived-from-budget. The baseline is the architecturally interesting part: `@repo/capability-projects` creates a project from an accepted quote as an **immutable baseline**, books committed and actual cost against it, and lets change orders adjust the current budget while the baseline stays queryable. That is the textbook answer to BRD pain point P5 and outcome BO-05, and it is sector-neutral — a consultancy running fixed-price engagements needs exactly the same object. The S-curve and critical path are more construction-flavoured in practice than in principle; `ASSUMPTIONS.md` 197 and 206 record that the curve could not move before the plan started and that the schedule on a phone became a list rather than a smaller chart, which are honest signs that this surface was tuned to one operator's device and habits.

### A.5 Variations and change orders

**Universal in mechanism, and carrying one strong sector-specific insistence.** The repository records that the customer did not accept a change order as a delta on a project: `ASSUMPTIONS.md` "A variation is a REAL budget, and acceptance is what joins it" (2026-08-18) attributes to client blocks 5 and 6 the requirement that "there must be a Variation Budget which, together with the originally approved budget, forms the basis for financial control". The consequence is visible in the navigation: `site/erp.html` places **Adicionales** under _Comercial_, not under _Proyectos_, with the comment that an extra is a change to what was sold, negotiated and approved before anyone builds it. **Assumption:** this is a renovation-trade reflex rather than a Canei idiosyncrasy — extras are where the margin of a reformas job is won or lost — but the repository does not contain the customer's own explanation, so it is an inference and is listed as a question in the final section.

### A.6 Progress valuations

**Construction-specific framing over a universal computation.** The computation is generic: `invoiceBases` proposes a valuation as executed minus already billed, and the engine labels the resulting line `Certificación de obra` (`site/erp-engine.js`). The word itself is on the forbidden-literal list in `CLAUDE.md` precisely because it is sector vocabulary, which is why it appears in the site layer and in `packages/packs/jurisdiction-es-es/src/manifest.ts` but never in a capability. For a second customer outside construction, the object to transfer is "bill for what has been executed and not yet billed", which is universal to time-and-progress work; the word and the chapter-level presentation are not.

### A.7 Customer invoicing, credit notes, receipts and receivables

**A universal mechanism sitting on the single most jurisdiction-bound asset in the repository, and the split is clean.** `@repo/capability-billing` holds immutable invoices, gapless numbering, the rectification path and a required `tax@1` port, and is proven against a fake adapter with no Spanish in it at all (`PROGRESS.md` item 5). Everything Spanish is in `@repo/pack-jurisdiction-es-es`: effective-dated tax tables, the dwelling-renovation reduced-rate decision engine whose inputs, rule id, legal basis and pack version are **persisted as a justification on the invoice**, withholding profiles, document labels and a SHA-256 invoice chain. `LEGAL_REVIEW.md` §2 records the three cumulative conditions behind that decision and the sources checked on 2026-07-16; §1 records that the certified-billing deadlines moved to 2027 and that the feature is hard-gated off at resolve time; §3 records that withholding on subcontractor invoices is profile-dependent and defaults to none. `packages/factory/src/negative.test.ts` proves the boundary from the other side: kernel plus billing with no jurisdiction pack fails loudly.

This is the part of the reference case that transfers furthest and is worth the most, and it is also the part where the honest caveat is largest: every rule carries `legally_verified: false` pending a human adviser, and `receipts` with partial allocation across invoices live in `@repo/capability-receivables`, which is generic. **Assumption:** for any second Spanish customer this pack is reused unchanged and the work is configuration — series, seller identity, withholding profiles — because that is what `tenants/azulejos-lopez/tenant.yaml` demonstrates at 0.02 seconds of machine time; a formal timed onboarding has not been run, and `PROGRESS.md` is explicit that it is still owed.

### A.8 Supplier invoices, purchases and subcontracts with document-expiry compliance

**Universal mechanisms; the reason anybody cares is construction.** `@repo/capability-payables` detects duplicate supplier-and-number bills, takes partial payments and produces due lists; `@repo/capability-procurement` runs purchase orders through draft, sent and received with committed-cost rollup by chapter and over-budget detection; `@repo/capability-sourcing` compares several bidders over quantified lines with per-group totals, variance against a baseline and explicit missing-price visibility. That last one is the direct digitisation of the customer's own evidence: BRD Appendix A describes a 245-row, 18-column workbook comparing four alternatives with absolute and percentage variance at chapter level, and Appendix A.1 insists that "missing supplier prices must be visible rather than silently treated as a valid zero" and that "the selected cost basis must be explicit, rather than inferred from spreadsheet colour or position". `ASSUMPTIONS.md` 21 records the decision to build it with kernel-only vocabulary — lines, groups, bidders — treating the customer's workbook as the motivating instance and not a special case. That is the factory thesis working exactly as intended.

Document expiry deserves a careful note, because it is where a Spanish construction reading could be over-claimed. The engine raises `SUB-DOC-EXPIRED` and `WORKER-DOC-EXPIRED` alerts, puts expiry dates on the calendar, and has a communication template for expired paperwork whose body says that without it site access is not possible. But a grep of `site/erp-engine.js` and `site/erp.html` finds **no REA registration, no Libro de Subcontratación, no coordination-of-business-activity model and no trade-card model by name**. The obligations are handled as generic dated documents attached to a subcontractor or a worker. **Assumption:** this is adequate for a company of Canei's size and shape today, and it is a deliberate generalisation rather than an oversight; **it is also a gap that Phase 3 must size, because a prospect that bids public work or sits deeper in a subcontracting chain may need the statutory registers as first-class objects, which is jurisdiction-pack work.**

### A.9 Bank statement import and reconciliation, petty cash and cards

**The sharpest split in the whole reference case: a universal matcher and a single-bank importer.** `@repo/capability-reconciliation` is factory-owned and describes itself as scoring candidate documents against a bank movement by amount, date and reference, proposing combinations for partial reconciliation and spotting internal transfers, knowing "nothing about invoices, projects, jurisdictions or sectors — only amounts, dates and text". That is as transferable as anything in the repository.

The importer is the opposite, and the repository says so plainly. `ASSUMPTIONS.md` "The bank statement importer reads ONE fixed layout" records an operator-chosen decision to parse the spreadsheet export one named bank produces today, found by its column names, with no column-mapping step and no second bank, and states the cost in advance: a renamed column fails loudly, a second bank means a second profile. The follow-up entry is the most valuable single paragraph in the repository for Phase 11's pre-mortem: the importer had been built, tested and deployed against an invented fixture, met the customer's two real exports for the first time on 18 August 2026 and failed three ways, each silent and each about money — 117 of 479 rows had wrong amounts because a numeric cell carried a float's binary expansion, a genuine duplicate payroll pair was suppressed as tidiness and a thousand euros vanished from the register, and the counterparty field was not in the header map at all, so the field that decides which invoice a transaction pays was discarded. All three were fixed and the chain now closes on all 478 steps against the bank's own arithmetic. **Classification: (iv) bespoke to this customer's bank, with a (iii) Spanish upgrade path already named — the `bank-statements@1` port in `INTEGRATIONS_PENDING.md` anticipates a standard Spanish statement format.** Petty cash and cards ride on the same import and allocation machinery and are universal.

### A.10 Labour hours with a site-worker role, approvals and corrections

**Universal, with a latent jurisdictional obligation and one genuinely reusable security decision.** `@repo/capability-time` books labour minutes against a project, chapter and person and rolls up hours and labour cost; `@repo/capability-access` supplies roles carrying permissions with wildcard checks. `tenants/diorka/tenant.yaml` encodes the customer's actual split as data: `administration` may manage customers, issue quotes and invoices, record receivables and payables and view reports; `operations` may record visits, log time, raise procurement, update projects and manage the schedule. That reflects BRD §2.1 — operations on site, back office in the office — and BRD §9.1's adoption requirement that the operational owner must not be forced to complete back-office fields while on site.

The reusable decision is in `docs/SITE-WORKER-SCOPE.md` and `PROGRESS.md` S13: a site worker sees their own hours and the jobs they are assigned to, and **no amount of money ever reaches a site worker's device — not their own rate, not a total, not a margin — removed from the data before it leaves the server, not hidden on screen**. The same entry records that the worker's write path had no door at all until a command endpoint was added, that the endpoint's receipt was briefly returning the whole company file, and that the clamp existed on two of three navigation doors. **Assumption:** server-side redaction by permission rather than by role name is a pattern that transfers to every tenant with crews, and it is the kind of thing a competitor ships late because nothing goes red when it is missing. The jurisdictional latency is working-time recording, which Phase 3 must map: the repository models hours, approvals and corrections, and `ASSUMPTIONS.md` references a day-record feature in the Spanish pack, but no statutory register is asserted here.

### A.11 An accountant hand-off package

**Spain-shaped presentation over a universal idea, and deliberately unfinished.** The navigation entry is `ADM-07 Reporte a gestoría`; the alert `GES-PACKAGE-DUE` is typed _fiscal_ with a tenant-configurable threshold in days after quarter-end, and the code comment states in terms that it is an advisory internal reminder and **not** a legal filing deadline, with no tax-authority date asserted anywhere in the engine. The export adapter `accounting-export@1` is listed in `INTEGRATIONS_PENDING.md` as unbound, pending the accountant's preferred format, and the same question appears in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` twice. One detail matters for pilot design: `ASSUMPTIONS.md` S20 records that the package refuses to build while any bank movement is unallocated and unexplained — 477 of them on a fresh import of a year's statements — and calls that the designed gate rather than a defect. **Assumption:** the universal object here is "a sealed period archive for an external bookkeeper, with a completeness gate"; the word, the quarterly rhythm and the eventual file format are Spanish.

### A.12 A 13-phase customer journey

**A universal mechanism wearing construction phase names.** The phases in `site/erp.html` are Oportunidad, Visita, Presupuesto, Envío al cliente, Aceptación, Contrato, Inicio de obra, Compras, Ejecución, Facturación, Cobros, Pagos a proveedores, Cierre y reseña. Four of those are unmistakably site work; the other nine describe any quoted-work business. The mechanism is the asset, and the repository is unusually explicit about why: the journey screen **owns no data**. Every status is derived from records that already exist and every action is the ERP's own form, after a prior version had kept its own database, its own price book, its own tax-identifier validator and its own invoice numbering, so a customer was typed twice and the journey's invoice was not the accountant's. About 5,400 lines were deleted to reach that state (`PROGRESS.md` PK14, `ASSUMPTIONS.md` S75). Later sessions added a thirteen-dot phase strip asserted equal to the computed phase states so the two drawings of one fact cannot drift, verbs that render disabled with the engine guard they mirror quoted beside them, and a record card listing every action performed and document produced. **Assumption:** this screen is the single most demonstrable thing in a sales meeting and the part a second customer will ask for first, which is why Phase 9's assets should be built around it.

### A.13 Every document in PDF, Word and Excel, in Spanish, Catalan and English

**Universal writers, a Catalan layer that is Catalonia-specific, and a document design that is Canei's.** The gate is real and measurable: `PROGRESS.md` S19 records a sweep over every document of every journey in both formats — **176 documents, 352 files** — each verified as a real PDF or a real Word file, broken down as 55 quotes, 46 contracts, 45 invoices, 24 job sheets and 6 receipts. The same entry records why it was needed: only the quote had a real PDF writer, and the other five kinds called the browser's print dialog, which does nothing inside a native web view, so the buttons were inert in the app while the test passed because it asserted the print mechanism. The trilingual layer is also measured: `ASSUMPTIONS.md` 182 records 3,264 dictionary entries complete in all three languages with the Catalan backlog at zero and a build that fails on a string added without Catalan, while stating plainly that the Catalan forms are machine-authored and marked as one reviewable block, that 72 remaining reported strings are company data that must never be translated, and that the setup guide stays English by the operator's decision. The honest transfer statement is: **the PDF, Word and Excel writers and the document-language resolution chain are universal; the Catalan dictionary is an asset only inside Catalonia and the Valencian and Balearic reading of it is untested; the document design is Canei's brand (`docs/clients/canei-subirats/BRAND.md`: brand green #48733C, Roboto Serif headings, Inter body) and is tenant data.**

### A.14 Templated communications filed as mailbox drafts

**Mixed, and the posture is bespoke by mandate rather than by customer choice.** `STANDARD_COMMS_TEMPLATES` in `site/erp-engine.js` ships six: quote sent, quote followed up, overdue invoice chased, works starting, subcontractor paperwork expired, and a post-completion check. Four of those are universal to any quoting business; works-starting and expired-paperwork are construction. The engine stores them as editable data and versions an edit rather than overwriting it, so a message already sent stays reproducible — that is a universal audit property. `@repo/capability-messaging` drafts from lifecycle events through an `email-out@1` port and ships a log-only adapter that never touches the network.

The distinctive part is the delivery posture: `INTEGRATIONS_PENDING.md` records that save-to-drafts is **built as an IMAP append** to the mailbox's Drafts folder and that there is **no SMTP anywhere in the codebase** — the system composes, files a draft, and a human presses send in their own mailbox. `ASSUMPTIONS.md` 28 and S35 record this as the chosen posture rather than a limitation. **Assumption:** this is a compliance and trust decision taken by the builder, not a requirement the customer stated, and a second customer may well ask for true automated sending; the port exists, so that is configuration plus credentials rather than redesign. `PROGRESS.md` S20 also records that assembling all eight composed emails into one pack found a live defect — two of the six messages had never had their tokens supplied, so a customer received an unfilled placeholder in the greeting of a works-starting message, visible only in the sent mail because every screen shows the template. That defect class is worth remembering in Phase 10.

### A.15 A native iOS app

**Universal packaging; the distribution route is specific to a single-company deployment.** `ios/` holds a SwiftUI shell over the web app with an animated splash, a floating tab bar, pull-to-refresh, offline recovery, native share and file export, so web changes reach the app without an App Store release and only shell changes need a rebuild (`PROGRESS.md`). The release path is written for a **Custom App distributed through Apple Business Manager** (`docs/RELEASE-IOS.md`), which is the right route for one named company and the wrong route for a fleet of a thousand tenants. Two recorded near-misses are instructive: the ten store screenshots were ignored by git and existed only in the container that made them, so a submission from a clean checkout would have gone to review with an empty gallery, and a review password was left in a tracked file after the lane ran. **Assumption:** for a multi-tenant go-to-market the shell generalises and the distribution model does not; Phase 7 should cost a single fleet-wide app with tenant selection rather than one Custom App per customer.

### A.16 What Part A means, stated strictly

Strictly applied, the classification gives: **universal or near-universal mechanisms** in quoting, catalogue, suppliers, sourcing comparison, projects and baselines, scheduling, change orders, billing, receivables, payables, procurement, time, access, documents, reconciliation matching, messaging and the journey projection — seventeen objects, all of which exist as named packages or as clearly separable engine areas. **Construction-specific:** the chapter taxonomy, measurement conventions, the progress-valuation framing, subcontractor and worker document expiry, four journey phase names, two communication templates. **Spain or Catalonia specific:** the entire tax, withholding, numbering, chaining and certified-billing posture, the accountant package's shape, the Catalan document layer. **Canei alone:** the Partida and Subpartida vocabulary, the single-bank statement importer, the brand and document design, the two-owner role split, the Business Manager distribution route, and the specific redaction rule that no money figure reaches a worker's phone.

The one thing I will not claim is that the universal seventeen are all _ready_ to transfer. Nineteen of twenty-seven areas are still in the hand-written engine, and the engine is a single-document state blob with a Spanish-labelled surface. **Assumption:** moving an area from engine to factory is cheaper than building it, because eight areas have already made that trip and the ledger records each move with its reasoning, but it is not free and Phase 7 must cost it rather than assume it.

## PART B — WHAT THE CUSTOMER ACTUALLY BOUGHT

### B.1 The pain before, which is documented in the customer's own commissioned words

This is the best-evidenced part of the reference case, because the customer's pain was written down before any software existed. `intake/diorka/brd-extracted.txt` is a Business Requirements Document prepared on 17 July 2026, marked "Draft for business validation", explicitly prepared from project conversations, prior workbook scope and a named comparison spreadsheet, and explicitly declining to choose a technology. Its §3.1 names eight pain points, and they are worth restating because the later phases should sell against these and not against invented pains: quotation and operational data captured more than once (P1); information split across files, portals and personal knowledge (P2); no controlled view of latest price, discount, supplier, validity and prior purchase evidence (P3); inability to prove which scope, price and options the customer accepted (P4); budgeted and real cost not continuously reconciled by item and chapter (P5); dependence on one owner for operational knowledge and the other for administrative continuity (P6); no single view of expected collections, supplier commitments and upcoming payments (P7); no consolidated multi-company view (P8).

Two details give the whole case its texture. **Quotations were prepared on paper by the operations owner**, and the back office then re-typed them — §1 and §3 both say so. And the comparison work that protects the margin was being done by hand in spreadsheets: one sheet, roughly 245 rows and 18 columns, comparing four named cost alternatives against an initial study, with absolute and percentage variance at chapter level and exceptions marked by highlighting. The BRD's own derived requirement is that the structure "should be preserved conceptually, even if the future interface is simpler".

### B.2 The trigger — and what the repository does not know

**I do not know what triggered the purchase, and the repository does not record it.** There is no dated account of a lost job, a failed audit, an adviser's recommendation, a software renewal, a grant application or a generational handover anywhere in `PROGRESS.md`, `ASSUMPTIONS.md`, the BRD or the client folder. What exists is a sequence: a BRD dated 17 July 2026 prepared from prior conversations, a functional specification dated 31 July 2026 (`intake/diorka/20260731_REQUERMIENTOS BÁSICO CANEI.docx`), a pinned baseline, and then continuous build sessions through to today. The regulatory forcing functions that Phase 3 will map are _visible in the repository as build constraints_ — `LEGAL_REVIEW.md` §1 records that the certified-billing regime was postponed to 2027 and is hard-gated off — but **nothing records the customer citing them as a reason to buy.** Treating a 2027 deadline as this customer's trigger would be inventing a motive, so I do not. It is the first question in the final list.

### B.3 Who decided

**Two owners, jointly, with no evidence of anyone else in the room.** The BRD's own instruction is to "review each requirement with the husband and wife together" and to resolve its §11 questions "with the husband and wife before defining a solution or estimating development effort". The roles are described functionally rather than by name: operations — site visits, scope, technical decisions, crews, subcontractors, materials, and the informal pricing knowledge; back office — customer communication, quotations, supplier relations, price lists, invoices, supplier bills, collections, payments and records. `tenants/_groups/diorka.yaml` encodes exactly those two roles as the owner group, and `tenants/diorka/tenant.yaml` turns them into permission grants. An external adviser appears in the BRD's stakeholder table only as "Accountant / tax / legal, to confirm" — **to confirm**, which is the BRD's own flag that the relationship was not yet mapped when it was written. **Assumption:** the accountant was not a buying-decision participant, only a downstream consumer of exports; this is inference from the word "confirm" and from the fact that the export format is still an open question nine weeks later, and it is listed as a question.

Throughout the build the decision channel is recorded as "the operator", who relays client mandates (for example `ASSUMPTIONS.md` S23: "Client-mandated, operator-confirmed 18/08"), reviews screens on a phone, and sends real bank exports. **Assumption:** the operator is an intermediary between this build and the two owners rather than one of the owners; the repository never says which, and it matters for Phase 8's buying-centre map.

### B.4 What they would have bought instead

**Not recorded, and one repository claim about it is stale and should not be carried forward.** `tenants/_groups/diorka.yaml` still lists under `currentTools` a hosted management instance at the customer's web domain with "export feasibility to assess". `ASSUMPTIONS.md` 24 **strikes that out**: the domain is Canei Subirats' own public marketing website on a common content-management platform, the earlier refusal was transient rather than an authentication wall, and the domain belongs to their web agency. `INTEGRATIONS_PENDING.md` repeats the correction. So the incumbent system was **paper quotations and spreadsheets**, with supplier prices spread across portals, past invoices and discounted price lists — exactly as the BRD describes — and the repository contains no evidence that any commercial package was evaluated, priced or rejected. Naming the alternatives they would have considered is Phase 1 and Phase 6 work against market sources; asserting it here would be invention. **One action for the repository itself: the stale `currentTools` line should be corrected, because a later phase reading that file in isolation would conclude the customer had a system to migrate from.**

### B.5 What they refused to change about how they work

This is the richest seam in the record, because every refusal left a trace in the code.

1. **Their vocabulary.** The chapter-and-line rename to Partida and Subpartida was client-mandated and applied to screens, messages and printed documents in three languages, with the code identifiers deliberately left alone. A company that insists on its own word for the most-used object in the system is a company that will not be trained out of its habits.
2. **Their chapter structure and their comparison sheet.** The BRD asks that the observed workbook structure be preserved conceptually; the 18-chapter taxonomy came straight from their evidence into the vertical pack as default data.
3. **The division of labour between the two owners.** BRD §9.1 states that the operational owner must not be forced to complete back-office fields while on site, and that the back office must be able to complete, correct and validate operational entries. The permission grants encode that literally.
4. **Extras are commercial, not technical.** The variation had to be a real budget joined to the original by acceptance, and the screen for it sits under Comercial.
5. **One job can have more than one payer.** `ASSUMPTIONS.md` 180 records that a project records who owes for it, that scope is attributed per chapter to a payer, and that invoicing follows the attribution — built because the same job may be invoiced partly to a general contractor and partly to the end customer. `LEGAL_REVIEW.md` §5 records why this is an adviser's determination and not a rule in code, and that each payer carries a tax treatment and a free-text justification set by the operator. **Assumption:** this is a structural fact about how they win work — sometimes as a direct supplier to households, sometimes inside a contractor's chain — rather than a one-off; it is listed as a question because the repository does not say how often.
6. **Catalan.** Their own site is Catalan-first with a Spanish toggle, and the slogan is kept verbatim in Catalan in the brand lockup. Trilingual documents were not a nice-to-have.
7. **The phone.** A striking number of defects in `PROGRESS.md` were found by the operator using the product on a phone — the phase rail opening off the right edge of the screen, the schedule needing to be a list rather than a small chart, a page loaded before a deploy never noticing the new build. The product had to work on a phone on a site, not on a desk.
8. **Their bank's file, as it comes out of their bank.** They sent two genuine exports and the importer was bent to those layouts by explicit choice.
9. **Work happens in one place.** `ASSUMPTIONS.md` S77 records the removal of "open the full screen" from every journey phase, and the customer's real document drawn inside the phase from the same sheet the PDF is printed from.

### B.6 What I do not know, and will not fill in

Beyond the trigger and the alternatives: I do not know the price paid, the contract shape, the expected payback, what the owners consider success, how many projects run at once, how many suppliers are active, whether they hold stock, how extras are agreed with customers today, or which of the BRD's own §11 questions have since been answered in conversation. The BRD's §11 is forty-odd questions long and `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` shows at least nine of them still open on the repository's side nine weeks later, including the legal names and tax identifiers of the group entities — `tenants/diorka/tenant.yaml` still carries a placeholder tax identifier marked pending validation. **Assumption:** the validation workshop described in BRD §11.1 has not taken place, because if it had, the placeholder would have been replaced by a configuration edit and the repository records config edits meticulously.

## PART C — THE TRANSFERABLE ASSET

### C.1 One page: what a second customer is actually buying

A second customer is not buying an ERP. Anyone can buy an ERP; there are dozens for Spanish construction, and Phase 6 will have to argue against them by name. What is on sale here is **an accumulated fit between four things that are individually available and jointly rare: a project-based trade's real operating sequence, Spanish invoicing law as an effective-dated and self-justifying decision rather than a hardcoded rate, documents in three languages that a customer and an accountant will both accept, and the ability to re-emit all of it for a new company as a data change instead of a project.**

Concretely, the buyer receives a system in which the quote they already write on paper becomes the baseline of the job, and the baseline is immutable, so the question "are we making money on this one" has an answer that cannot be edited after the fact. They receive the comparison sheet their margin depends on as a first-class object in which a missing supplier price is visible as missing rather than silently worth zero, and in which the chosen cost basis is recorded rather than implied by a cell's colour. They receive an invoice whose tax decision carries, persisted on the document itself, the rule identifier, the inputs, the legal basis and the version of the rule pack that decided it — so when an adviser or an inspector asks why this invoice was at this rate, the document answers. They receive every document in the format their customer expects and the language their customer reads, with the language frozen at signature because the customer signed the words they were shown. They receive a crew-facing surface on which no money figure exists at all, because it is removed before the data leaves the server. And they receive a thirteen-phase view of their own job that owns no data, so it can never disagree with the records.

What they are buying that is invisible is the **negative knowledge**: a build log of roughly two hundred recorded decisions in which a plausible-looking implementation was found to be silently wrong about money — 117 of 479 bank rows mis-parsed by a float's binary expansion, a thousand euros of genuine duplicate payroll suppressed as tidiness, tax charged twice on milestone invoices, an invoice cap that refused a legitimate final invoice by one cent, five document kinds whose download button was inert inside the phone app while the test passed, two email templates that reached customers with an unfilled greeting. Each of those is now a gate. A competitor starting today would ship every one of them, and would not know it, because in each case nothing went red.

### C.2 The three capabilities that took the most effort and would take a competitor longest to copy

**First: the effective-dated, self-justifying tax and invoicing decision, with the boundary proven from both sides.** This is not a tax table. It is a rule engine in a jurisdiction pack that refuses to guess when it has no rule for a date; a decision whose inputs, rule identifier, legal basis and pack version are persisted on the artefact; a billing capability that is provably free of jurisdiction knowledge because a build-time linter fails on the vocabulary and a negative test proves that kernel plus billing with no jurisdiction pack fails loudly; an invoice chain sealed with a per-tenant hash sequence; a resolve-time hard gate that refuses to enable the certified-billing mode before certification; and a legal register that cites its sources with dates and stamps every finding as unverified pending a human adviser. The reason a competitor cannot copy this quickly is not the rules — the rules are public. It is that almost every product in this market embeds them, and the work here is the _separation_, which cannot be retrofitted to a codebase that has already hardcoded a rate into a quote screen. `LEGAL_REVIEW.md`, `packages/packs/jurisdiction-es-es`, `packages/capabilities/billing`, `packages/boundary-lint` and `packages/factory/src/negative.test.ts` are the evidence.

**Second: one record of truth across the whole money chain, with immutability where it matters and no second copy anywhere.** The quote freezes on acceptance; the accepted quote becomes an immutable project baseline; change orders move the current budget and leave the baseline queryable; scope is attributed per chapter to a payer and invoicing is capped by that attribution, with a stated cent-level tolerance because the round trip through a tax-inclusive total costs a cent; invoices are immutable and gapless with a rectification path; receipts allocate partially across invoices; supplier bills detect duplicates; bank movements reconcile by a generic matcher that can propose combinations and spot internal transfers; and the accountant package refuses to build while movements are unexplained. The hardest-won part is the deletions: about 5,400 lines removed when the journey stopped keeping its own database, price book, tax-identifier validator and invoice numbering, and the explicit rule that two pages holding two engine instances over one store mint colliding identifiers and erase each other. A competitor copies features quickly and arrives at exactly the architecture that was deleted here — a second model of the same nouns that agrees with the first until a customer notices it does not.

**Third: trilingual, multi-format documents as a measured gate rather than a claim.** Three complete dictionaries with a build that fails on a string added without Catalan; 3,264 entries; a residue of 72 reported strings dumped and explained as company data rather than pattern-matched away; an identity-mapping rule found masquerading as a translation and fixed; a document-language chain resolved in one place and frozen at signature; twenty document kinds written as real PDF and real Word files through one descriptor, gated by a sweep over 176 documents and 352 files; and a spreadsheet export that looks like the company. The effort here is not translation, which is cheap. It is that **the same document, in the same design, has to be correct in three languages across four frozen legal states and two file formats, and the only way to keep it correct is to make the build fail when it is not.** A competitor can translate an interface in a month and will not discover until a customer's Catalan contract that a rule mapped a string to itself and reported success.

### C.3 What is explicitly not for sale

The brand and document design are tenant data and belong to Canei. The single-bank importer is one company's file format. The Business Manager app route is one company's distribution. The Partida and Subpartida vocabulary is one company's language. The nineteen engine-owned areas are a real product but not yet a reproducible one, and the Catalan dictionary is machine-authored and awaiting a native reviewer, which is a disclosure to make before a Catalan-speaking prospect reads a contract, not after.

### C.4 The honest ledger, for the phases that follow

Phase 7 will be asked for a cost-per-layer model. The repository already supplies the numbers it has and is candid about the ones it lacks: a second tenant was created config-only in 0.02 seconds of machine time, the definition-of-done target is a formal timed onboarding under fifteen minutes, and `PROGRESS.md` states plainly that the timed run with a real intake has not happened. **Assumption, to be tested in Phase 7 and not before: the fifteen-minute claim holds for a Spanish reformas company whose requirements stay at the configuration layer, and collapses the moment a prospect brings a different bank, a different sector's chapter taxonomy, statutory subcontracting registers, or public-sector invoicing — those are pack and capability work, which is reusable investment but is not fifteen minutes.**

## Open questions for the customer

These are the questions the repository cannot answer and nobody should guess. They are ordered by how much the later phases depend on them. Items marked (BRD) were already asked in the customer's own requirements document and are recorded as unanswered in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`.

**About the decision to buy**

1. What actually triggered this project in mid-2026, and what was the alternative to doing nothing? Was there a specific incident — a job whose margin was lost, a dispute about what had been accepted, an adviser's request, an audit, a grant, a succession plan?
2. Who else, besides the two owners, had to agree? Did the accountant, a bank, or a family member outside the business have a view?
3. What was evaluated and rejected before this, if anything — commercial construction software, a general business package, a bespoke spreadsheet rebuild? If nothing was evaluated, why not?
4. What will make this a success in twelve months, in the owners' own words and numbers?
5. What is the acceptable monthly cost and implementation effort (BRD §11.7)?

**About how they work, where the build has had to assume**

6. Is invoicing one job to two payers — an end customer and a general contractor — routine or occasional, and what share of revenue arrives through a contractor? (Drives the tax-treatment model and the margin split in `ASSUMPTIONS.md` 180.)
7. How are extras agreed with a customer today, and is the insistence that a variation be a full budget a trade norm or this company's own rule?
8. How are customer acceptances evidenced today — signature, email, message, verbal? (BRD §11.2)
9. Is the selling price set from cost plus margin, from market price, or by judgement, and who decides? (BRD §11.3)
10. Are labour and machinery inside unit prices or shown separately? (BRD §11.3)
11. How many projects run at once, what is a typical duration, and what are the usual causes of overrun? (BRD §11.5)
12. Are the crews employees, self-employed, subcontractors, or a mixture? (BRD §11.5)
13. Which billing pattern is normal — deposit, monthly progress, milestones, final payment — and what are the usual supplier terms? (BRD §11.6)
14. Does the business hold stock, or is everything bought to a project? (BRD §11.4)

**About compliance and the external advisers**

15. What are the legal names, tax identifiers, fiscal addresses, banking details and numbering habits of every group entity? The tenant configuration still carries a placeholder tax identifier. (BRD §11.1)
16. What export does the accountant actually want, and in which format? This has been open since July and blocks the hand-off adapter. (BRD §11.6)
17. Which withholding profile applies to each real subcontractor and self-employed supplier? The interface currently badges the field as needing review, and the rule is profile-dependent. (`LEGAL_REVIEW.md` §3)
18. Does the work mix include structural rehabilitation, where the reduced-rate path differs and the system currently falls back conservatively? (`OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` 3)
19. Do they bid for public work, now or within two years? This decides whether public-sector electronic invoicing is in scope at all.
20. Which subcontracting-compliance documents do their clients and coordinators actually demand, and should the statutory registers be modelled as named objects rather than as dated documents?
21. Who is responsible for working-time records today, and what does the collective agreement require of the record?

**About data, tools and adoption**

22. Which banks and which accounts, and will the current single-layout importer cover them all?
23. Which suppliers publish downloadable price lists, in what format, and which portals show negotiated net prices? Can access be shared contractually, or must a person log in? (BRD §11.4)
24. Is there a standard priced-database import the business expects to use, and from which source?
25. Which spreadsheets must keep working during the transition, and which must be migrated? (BRD §11.7, §7.3)
26. Are quotes required in Spanish, Catalan, or both, and will a native speaker review the Catalan document set before it goes to a customer? (BRD §11.3)
27. Which devices do both owners use daily, and is connectivity reliable on site? (BRD §11.7)
28. Should the system ever send email itself, or is filing a draft in the owner's mailbox the permanent arrangement?
29. Who, other than the two owners, needs access within two years, and what should crews and subcontractors be allowed to see or enter? (BRD §11.1)
30. Will they act as a reference for a second customer in the Barcelona area, and under what conditions?

Questions 1 to 5 are the ones Phase 3, Phase 6 and Phase 8 cannot be written honestly without. Until they are answered, every statement about why a Spanish reformas SME buys this system is an inference from a build log, and should be labelled as one.
