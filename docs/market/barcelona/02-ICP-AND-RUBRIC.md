# 02 — IDEAL CUSTOMER PROFILE, THE DISTANCE MODEL AND THE SCORING RUBRIC

This document is Phase 2 of the Barcelona market roadmap (`docs/market/barcelona/ROADMAP.md`), written on **2026-09-12**, and it is the targeting instrument the later phases operate rather than an essay about targeting. It takes the strict generalises-versus-bespoke classification of `00-REFERENCE-CASE.md` and the market definition, CNAE segmentation and 8-to-40-employee sweet spot of `01-MARKET-MAP.md`, and converts them into four things that can be applied to a named firm by someone who has never read either document: an Ideal Customer Profile with disqualifiers of equal standing to the qualifiers; an adaptation of Pankaj Ghemawat's CAGE distance framework in which the origin is not a country but this repository's reference implementation; a layer-distance overlay whose L0 boundary is enumerated from the configuration surface the code actually exposes rather than from an assumption about it; and a weighted 0–100 rubric with anchored score bands, a layer-decision tree, hard gates, and three worked examples — the reference tenant itself, a deliberately poor fit, and one borderline case included because calibration happens at the margin and not at the extremes. Every judgement that rests on inference rather than on a file or a sourced figure is flagged in bold as an assumption, and the two places where the architecture's own promises are not yet implemented are stated in the body rather than in a footnote, because the rubric's unit economics depend on them.

---

## 0. How this instrument is meant to be used, and what it deliberately does not score

The rubric scores **fit and cost-to-serve**. It does not score urgency, reachability or reference value. That separation is deliberate and it is the most important procedural decision in this document, because the standard failure of an SME scoring model is that it collapses "this firm would work well on our system" into "we should call this firm on Monday", and then cannot explain why a high-scoring prospect never closed. Urgency is Phase 3's output — the regulatory and non-regulatory forcing functions and, crucially, their detectability from outside the company. Reference value and channel reachability are Phase 8's. Phase 5 combines all three into tiers, and the roadmap is explicit that Tier A is "not just the highest scores" but the firms whose success is most citable. So a firm can score 88 here and sit in Tier B because nothing is forcing it to act; and a firm can score 71 and sit in Tier A because a gremi will introduce it and it will go on the record afterwards.

Three procedural rules make the instrument repeatable. **First, score from observable evidence, never from a sales impression**: every dimension below names the artefact the score is read from, and Phase 4's extraction schema was written to supply most of them. **Second, an unknown scores 5 and carries a flag, never a guess**, with one documented exception for the reference tenant, which is the calibration point and is scored from its full intake record. **Third, no dimension may be scored above 8 without a named artefact** — a permit record, a filed account, a public register entry, a website page, a stated answer from the firm. An 8 is "strong evidence"; a 10 is "proven". That ceiling is what stops enthusiasm from entering the arithmetic.

## PART A — ICP

### A.0 The profile in one box

> **A Spanish limited company of 8 to 40 employees and roughly €1.0m to €8.0m of revenue, registered in CNAE-2009 43.3, 43.9 or the renovation end of 41.2, within about 60 minutes' travel of Sant Just Desvern, that wins work by issuing a chaptered priced quotation which a private customer or a general contractor accepts; runs between four and twenty simultaneous jobs of €20k to €400k; passes at least a third of its cost through subcontractors and material suppliers whose prices it compares by hand before committing; has a separate operations decision-maker and administrative decision-maker, both owners or family; files through an external gestoría on a quarterly rhythm; works in Spanish and Catalan; and today holds its margin question in a spreadsheet, a paper quotation pad and one person's head.**

Everything in Part A is a decomposition of that sentence into indicators an analyst can observe before making contact.

Two notes on the bands. The 8-to-40 employee range is Phase 1's sweet spot and its bounds are judgements, consistent with the sourced size distribution but not derived from it; Phase 1 also noted that the band is thin — roughly 2,291 firms in the 10–49 band and 5,410 in the in-scope 3–9 band in the province — and that thirty firms scored either side of each bound would settle the question cheaply. **Assumption: the employee count is a proxy for the variable that actually matters, which is the number of simultaneous jobs that exceed what one person can hold in their head.** Where job count is observable from permit records, it should override the employee band, and the rubric's Economic dimension is written so that it can.

On group structure: a second sibling entity is a positive signal rather than a neutral one, because the reference tenant is itself a family group with a consolidated-view pain point (BRD P8) and the repository already models a group as data over tenants in `tenants/_groups/diorka.yaml`, with each legal entity its own tenant carrying its own numbering and branding. A prospect with two S.L.s under the same two owners is therefore a two-tenant sale at nearly one tenant's delivery cost. An external shareholder, by contrast, introduces a buying centre the reference purchase did not have.

### A.2 Operating characteristics

These are the qualifiers that actually predict whether the product works, and they are ordered by how much of the asset they activate.

1. **The quote is the contract.** The firm wins work by issuing a structured document of chapters and priced line items, with optional items kept out of the base total, revised into versions, and accepted in whole or in part. This is the spine of the reference case and the thing Phase 1 put first on the IN list. Observable: a downloadable sample quotation, a website page describing "presupuesto sin compromiso", an aparellador or architect naming them as a firm that quotes against a measured schedule.
2. **Margin is decided at purchase time.** The firm compares several suppliers or subcontractors per chapter before committing, and does it by hand today. The reference customer's own evidence was a 245-row, 18-column workbook comparing four alternatives against an initial study with chapter-level variance. Observable only by asking, which makes it the single best qualifying question: _"When you price a job, how many supplier prices do you put side by side, and where do they live?"_
3. **Between four and twenty simultaneous jobs.** Below four, one person holds the state and the product's central promise — an answer to "are we making money on this one" that cannot be edited after the fact — has no audience. Above twenty, the firm usually has a controller and an incumbent system. Observable from permit records, which name the contractor and the declared budget.
4. **Jobs of €20k to €400k lasting three weeks to nine months.** This is where chapters, progress valuations, change orders and retentions all exist simultaneously. Andimac's figure of roughly €32,000 for an integral residential reform sits at the lower end; a €400k fit-out or rehabilitation sits at the upper.
5. **At least a third of cost through third parties.** Subcontractors and material suppliers are what make the catalogue, the comparison, the payables duplicate-detection and the document-expiry machinery worth opening. A firm with direct labour and no subcontractors is buying four features it will never use.
6. **Instalment or progress billing rather than a single invoice on completion.** Deposit, milestones, monthly progress against executed work — any of these activate the contract instalment model and the executed-minus-billed valuation. A firm that invoices once at the end uses perhaps half the billing asset.
7. **Change orders are commercially negotiated before work proceeds.** The reference customer insisted a variation is a real budget joined to the original by acceptance, which is why the screen sits under the commercial area and not under projects. A firm that absorbs extras informally to keep the client happy is a firm whose margin problem the product will diagnose and whose behaviour it will not change.
8. **Crews on sites, and an owner who reads on a phone.** The crew-facing surface exists, has no money figures in it at all because they are removed server-side, and a striking share of the build log's defects were found by an operator using the product on a phone on a site.
9. **An external gestoría on a quarterly rhythm, and a hand-off that is currently a manual assembly.** The accountant package is Spain-shaped and deliberately unfinished — the export adapter is unbound pending the accountant's preferred format — so a prospect whose gestoría has a stated, conventional format is cheaper than one whose gestoría wants something idiosyncratic.
10. **Both Spanish and Catalan in customer-facing documents.** Where Catalan is required rather than appreciated, the trilingual document asset is worth real money; where only Spanish is needed, it is worth nothing and should not be priced.

**Assumption: an abandoned previous attempt at a management package is a positive signal rather than a negative one**, because it evidences both willingness to spend and a diagnosis the owner has already made about what went wrong; the repository contains no evidence either way, and this is an inference from the reference case's own history of having no incumbent at all.

### A.4 Organisational signals

1. **Two decision-makers, both inside the family or the ownership.** The reference purchase was two owners deciding jointly with no evidence of anyone else in the room, and the roles were functional: operations owns site, scope, crews, subcontractors, materials and the informal pricing knowledge; administration owns customer communication, quotations, supplier relations, invoices, collections and records. The permission grants in `tenants/diorka/tenant.yaml` encode exactly that split as data.
2. **A named administrative person who is not the operations owner.** This is the single most reliable organisational qualifier, because the product's defining configuration decision — operations must not be forced to complete back-office fields while on site, administration must be able to complete and correct operational entries — is inert without two people.
3. **A generational or succession question in the air.** Phase 3 will assess detectability; the signal here is an officer change in BORME adding a family member in their thirties to a board otherwise in its sixties.
4. **Appetite for being measured.** The reference customer commissioned a business requirements document, sent real bank exports, reviewed screens on a phone and mandated a vocabulary change. That is a firm that wants to be measured. Its opposite — a firm whose owner says the numbers are in his head and that is where they work best — is a cultural decline and §B.1 scores it.
5. **Membership of something.** The Gremi de Constructors d'Obres de Barcelona i Comarques reports 1,332 affiliated companies across itself and its linked comarcal guilds, which against a provincial SAM of roughly 7,000 firms is a fifth of the market by count, and its members self-select for being the firms that join things. Membership is evidence of both process appetite and channel reachability.
6. **An aparellador or architect in the loop on most jobs.** They sit between owner and contractor on most reforms, and their presence correlates with measured schedules, which correlates with chaptered quotes.
7. **No controller, no finance director, no IT employee.** The presence of any of the three moves the buying centre from a kitchen table to a committee and moves the project from configuration to integration.

### A.5 Disqualifiers

Disqualifiers are not the inverse of qualifiers; they are conditions under which the sale is possible and should still not be made. Six are hard gates that override any score, and five are soft gates that cap a score.

**Hard gates — decline regardless of total score.**

1. **Any requirement that lands at L5.** A kernel change is forbidden by the architecture. The practical forms this takes are a demand for an override-the-rate control on tax, a demand for per-tenant custom fields on core objects, a demand to skip invoice numbers, or a demand to edit an issued invoice in place. The governing decision names money math and rounding, invoice immutability and the rectification path, tax decisions, numbering gaplessness, the audit trail, per-tenant schema shape, and effective-dating semantics as never configurable for any customer, and accepts explicitly that some prospects will walk because they were buying a fork.
2. **Outside the Spanish tax perimeter within the planning horizon.** A firm invoicing from France, Andorra or Portugal, or about to, needs a jurisdiction pack that does not exist. The architecture makes that cheap _later_; doing it before the second Spanish tenant converts an asset into a project.
3. **Public-sector work as a material share of revenue today.** Public tendering brings tender files, classification registers and public-sector electronic invoicing, none of which the jurisdiction pack holds. This is the largest named unlock in the adjacency map and the natural Tier C investment case, which is precisely why it must not be sold as a Tier A onboarding.
4. **A live incumbent ERP or accounting ledger that must be integrated rather than replaced.** The product's strongest claim is one record with no second copy anywhere; next to an incumbent ledger that claim is unhonourable, and the reference case records what happens when two models of the same nouns coexist — they agree until a customer notices they do not.
5. **A revenue base that is recurring maintenance contracts with service levels.** There is no recurring-contract object and no service-level clock. This is the most commonly blurred boundary in the whole map, because installers routinely do both and will describe themselves as project firms while earning from maintenance rounds. Test it with a revenue-mix question, never with an activity description.
6. **Refusal of the data posture.** Real financial and employee data under GDPR and LOPDGDD requires a processor agreement and a retention position, and the pilot design depends on a pre-installation baseline measurement. A firm that will not permit either cannot be a design partner, and a pilot with no pre-measurement can only produce opinion.

**Soft gates — score is capped, firm may still be worth a Tier B or C slot.**

7. **Single decision-maker doing both roles.** Cap the total at 70; the permission model's central distinction has no one to serve.
8. **Fewer than three simultaneous jobs.** Cap at 65. Excel plus a gestoría remains the rational choice and should be conceded rather than argued around.
9. **A second or third bank with a different statement layout.** Cap at 75 until the statement-import port is real, because today this is not the additive change the architecture intends it to be.
10. **Catalan required for legally binding customer documents, with no native reviewer available.** Cap at 80 and disclose before a contract is signed, not after: the Catalan dictionary is machine-authored and awaiting a native reviewer, and that disclosure is a condition of selling into a Catalan-first firm honestly.
11. **No willingness to be referenced, in a Tier A slot.** Does not affect the fit score at all, but disqualifies the firm from the design-partner cohort, whose entire purpose is a citable outcome.

### A.6 Eight firm types we would actively decline, and why

These are described as types rather than named companies; no real firm is named, and each description is drawn from a segment Phase 1 either excluded or flagged as half-fit. **Assumption: these eight types are individually recognisable in a Phase 4 list from CNAE, filed accounts and a website, which is what makes them useful as a screening filter rather than as a cautionary tale.**

**1. "The scaffolding and plant-hire firm with a construction code."** Twenty-five employees, CNAE 43.99, a yard of scaffolding, formwork and two telehandlers, revenue from day-rate hire plus erection crews. It has a construction classification, a site presence and a plausible employee band, and it will appear at the top of any unfiltered 43.9 extraction. **Decline because it is an asset-rental business wearing a construction CNAE**: its margin is utilisation of owned plant over time, not chapter-level purchase decisions; it needs an asset register, availability calendar and hire-contract object, none of which exist as ports. Layer L4 for a business whose quote is a rate card, which is the worst combination in the model — expensive and not reusable into the core segment.

**2. "The service-led climate installer with a maintenance book."** Thirty employees, CNAE 43.22, two thirds of revenue from annual maintenance contracts on air-conditioning and boiler installations across several hundred customer sites, one third from new installation projects. This is the firm the adjacency map warns about by name, and it is genuinely tempting: it is in the largest and best-measured segment, it quotes its project work in chapters, and it buys materials competitively. **Decline because the two thirds of revenue that actually pays the wages needs a contract-and-work-order spine with a recurring-contract object, an asset register per installed unit and a penalty clock** — and because a firm will not buy a system that runs the minority of its business. Layer L2 at best and realistically L4. Route to Tier C only if Phase 5 can quantify how many project-led installers the same pack would convert.

**3. "The 150-employee general contractor that bids public work."** CNAE 41.2, revenue around €30m, forty per cent public tenders for school refurbishment and municipal rehabilitation, a financial controller, a payroll bureau, an incumbent accounting package and a two-person administration department. Every operating characteristic in §A.2 is present and the firm would genuinely benefit. **Decline because four of the six hard gates fire at once**: public-sector invoicing and classification registers (L3 plus L4), near-continuous invoice-level reporting to the tax authority above the filing threshold, an incumbent ledger to integrate, and a committee purchase with a written specification and a comparison against named commercial products. Phase 1 is blunt about the competitive reality: five per cent of Spanish construction firms account for sixty-two per cent of turnover, and those firms are already sold to by vendors with implementation partners. Competing there on the strength of one reference is a losing position.

**4. "The promotor-constructor."** Twelve employees, CNAE 41.1 — re-cut to 68.12 under CNAE-2025, which moves it out of construction and into real estate — buying plots, financing through a bank, building six-to-twelve-dwelling blocks and selling the units. Small, local, apparently ideal on headcount. **Decline because the quote-to-acceptance spine does not exist: a developer does not quote, it appoints.** The part of the asset that survives is payer attribution and the invoicing chain, perhaps a fifth of the whole, and the part that would have to be built — scheme-level capitalisation, a sales pipeline of units, long-horizon cost capitalisation — is a new capability for a segment whose buying process is a committee of bank, architect and partners. Tier C at the absolute best.

**5. "The two-man multi-trade with a van."** The owner plus one employee plus his brother-in-law invoicing as an autónomo, CNAE 43.39, revenue €180k, three or four small bathroom and kitchen jobs at a time, quotations written by hand on a pad. Emotionally the nearest thing to the reference customer's own origin story. **Decline because every lower-bound condition in Phase 1 fires**: no second administrative person, so the permission split is inert; the firm is more often the subcontractor than the one collecting others' documents, which inverts the compliance flow; the number of simultaneous jobs is inside what one head holds; and willingness to pay sits below the floor at which any human onboarding can be served profitably. The honest statement to such a firm is that a spreadsheet and a gestoría remain the right answer.

**6. "The integrated facility-management contractor."** Eighty employees, CNAE 81.10, cleaning, maintenance, minor works and concierge services across twenty commercial buildings on multi-year contracts with service levels and penalties. It has crews, sites, subcontractors, document expiry, a Spanish tax position and a real administrative function. **Decline because the quote-and-baseline spine does not fit a contract-and-service-level business at all**: it needs a recurring-contract capability, an asset register and penalty clocks — three ports that do not exist — while the assets that do transfer are the jurisdiction pack, documents, payables and time recording, which is the low-value half. The adjacency map scores this at 44 out of 100 and says decline unless a Tier C case names the unlock.

**7. "The industrial pipework and mechanical maintenance contractor."** Forty-five employees, CNAE 33.12, framework agreements with two chemical plants and a food factory, work arriving as work orders against an annual contract, crews mobilised to shutdowns, a different collective agreement, and customers who insist on their own supplier portals for documents and invoices. **Decline because it is asset-centric and work-order-centric rather than project-centric**, because the document-expiry regime is the customer's portal rather than ours, and because each industrial client's portal is an integration. It is the furthest of the seven adjacencies from the reference case, scoring 38. The jurisdiction pack and time recording survive; the spine does not.

**8. "The cross-border fit-out contractor."** Eighteen employees, CNAE 43.32, registered in Girona, half its revenue from retail fit-out in Perpignan and Andorra la Vella, invoicing from a French sister entity and an Andorran one. On trade, size, operating shape and culture it is close to ideal, and it will look like a strong prospect in every extraction field. **Decline for now on the jurisdiction gate**: two additional jurisdiction packs with their own tax, numbering and filing regimes, for a single tenant, before the second Spanish tenant has been proven. The architecture makes this the designed extension point and therefore the _right_ L3 investment eventually; the commercial error would be to make it the first one. Park in Tier C with the unlock quantified, which for France in particular is a large number of Catalan firms working across the border.

Two further types are worth naming without a full entry, because they will both appear and both waste time: the firm whose owner opens with "we need it to work exactly like our spreadsheet, with our own fields" — an L5 demand wearing an L0 costume, and the documented death of these systems; and the firm in a UTE, a temporary joint venture, where the contracting entity is the joint venture rather than either partner, so the tenant boundary, the numbering series and the payer attribution all belong to a vehicle that dissolves at the end of the job.

## PART B — THE DISTANCE MODEL

Ghemawat's CAGE framework measures the distance between two _countries_ along cultural, administrative, geographic and economic axes, and its central insight is that distance is multidimensional and that the dimensions are not substitutes — a firm cannot compensate for administrative distance with geographic proximity. The adaptation here keeps the insight and moves the origin. **The origin is not Spain and not Barcelona; it is the reference implementation itself**: the tenant configuration at `tenants/diorka/tenant.yaml`, the packs it selects, the capabilities it composes, and the operating reality of the firm that configuration was shaped around. Distance is therefore measured from an artefact, which has one large advantage over measuring it from a country — the artefact can be read, and disagreements about where the origin is can be settled by opening a file.

Each dimension below is scored 0 to 10 against written anchors at 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10, with intermediate odd values permitted only when two named indicators point to adjacent anchors. The anchors are absolute, not relative to the prospect pool, so scores remain comparable across Phase 5's batches and across time.

### Cultural

**What it measures:** the distance between how the reference firm works and thinks and how the prospect does — the language of the business, whether it is owner-managed or professionalised, whether its self-image is craft or process, and above all its appetite for being measured.

**Indicators, in descending order of predictive value.** (i) _Appetite for measurement_: does the firm already keep a job-cost workbook, however crude, and does the owner volunteer a number when asked about margin? The reference customer commissioned a requirements document and sent real bank exports — that is the top of this scale. (ii) _Owner-managed with an operations/administration split_ versus either a single proprietor or a professionalised management layer; the reference configuration encodes the split literally as permission grants. (iii) _Working language_: Spanish, Catalan, or both, and specifically whether customer-facing legal documents must be Catalan. (iv) _Craft versus process self-image_: a website that leads with craftsmanship and photographs of finished bathrooms is not the same firm as one that leads with methodology, certifications and guarantees — both can be excellent, but the second adopts a system in weeks and the first in quarters. (v) _Phone-first working_: the owner who answers from a site is the owner the crew surface was designed for. (vi) _Vocabulary insistence_: the reference customer mandated its own words for the most-used objects in the system, which the label layer absorbed as data; a prospect with equally strong vocabulary preferences is not a problem, and a prospect with none is mildly cheaper.

| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 10    | Owner-managed with a distinct administration owner; keeps a job-cost or comparison workbook today and will show it; volunteers margin numbers; works in Spanish and Catalan; owner operates from a phone on site; has asked for measurement before being sold it |
| 8     | Owner-managed with a distinct administrative employee; keeps partial job costing; answers the margin question with a range rather than a shrug; bilingual in practice                                                                                            |
| 6     | Owner-managed, administration shared or part-time; margin known at year-end from the gestoría; willing to be measured but has never tried                                                                                                                        |

| 0     | Hostile to transparency, or a working language the document layer does not cover for binding documents                                                                                                                                                           |

**Cap rule:** Catalan required for binding customer documents with no native reviewer available caps the total score at 80, because the Catalan document set is machine-authored pending review and that must be disclosed before a contract rather than after.

### Administrative

**What it measures:** the distance between the reference firm's administrative and legal envelope and the prospect's — legal form, tax regime, collective agreement, subcontracting compliance load, public-work exposure, and the gestoría relationship. This is the dimension that most often converts a promising firm into pack work, and it is where Phase 3's verification feeds directly in.

**Indicators.** (i) _Legal form and entity count_: S.L. with one or two sibling entities is the origin; a UTE, a cooperative with dispersed governance, or a branch of a foreign parent is distance. (ii) _Tax and filing regime_: quarterly periodic filings through a gestoría is the origin; near-continuous invoice-level reporting above the filing threshold is real distance, because the pack's job changes from producing documents to sustaining a channel; the certified-billing regime is hard-gated off in the pack until certification and its deadline moved to 2027, so a prospect who wants it switched on today is a prospect being sold something that does not exist. (iii) _Collective agreement and working-time obligations_: every construction firm with employees has carried the sector pension-plan obligation since February 2024 under the general sector agreement, which at ten employees is a payroll line and at a hundred and fifty is a function; the repository models hours, approvals and corrections but asserts no statutory register. (iv) _Subcontracting compliance load_: the engine handles subcontractor and worker documents as generic dated documents with expiry alerts, and a deliberate grep of the codebase finds no named model for the statutory subcontracting register, the sector employer register, the coordination-of-activities duty or the trade card. A prospect whose clients and coordinators demand those as first-class named objects is asking for jurisdiction-and-vertical bridge work — which is precisely the mechanism the architecture provides for law that is genuinely the product of one jurisdiction and one sector, costed as one isolated bridge module rather than a new pack. (v) _Public-work exposure_: any material share is a hard gate today. (vi) _Gestoría relationship_: a conventional, cooperative gestoría with a stated export format is near; an idiosyncratic one, or one that resists the client holding its own records, is far — and the export adapter is still unbound pending exactly that answer.

| Score | Anchor                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 10    | S.L., one or two sibling entities under the same owners, quarterly filings through a cooperative gestoría with a conventional export format, sector collective agreement, subcontractor documents handled as dated paperwork, no public work, no certified-billing demand before its deadline |
| 8     | As above, with one complication: a second entity with separate numbering habits, or a gestoría whose format must be confirmed, or occasional work inside a general contractor's chain requiring payer attribution                                                                             |
| 6     | Subcontracting compliance demanded as named statutory registers by clients or coordinators — bridge-module work, additive and isolated, but not configuration                                                                                                                                 |
| 4     | Above the near-continuous reporting threshold, or an unusual withholding position across many self-employed suppliers, or a cooperative governance structure with no single administrative owner                                                                                              |
| 2     | Material public-sector revenue requiring tender files, classification registers and public-sector electronic invoicing                                                                                                                                                                        |
| 0     | Outside the Spanish tax perimeter, or a UTE as the contracting vehicle, or a demand for a control that overrides a tax decision                                                                                                                                                               |

**Hard gates on this dimension:** scores of 0 and 2 are declines in Phase 5 irrespective of the weighted total, and must be recorded as such with the instrument named.

### Geographic

**What it measures:** the cost and friction of deploying, training and supporting on site — travel time from the reference implementation's own base, how many simultaneous sites the firm runs, and how mobile its crews are. This is the lightest-weighted dimension and deliberately so: the product is a web application with a native shell, the crew surface is designed for a phone on a site, and remote onboarding is plausible. It is not weightless, because the first five to eight design partners need in-person time, a pilot needs a baseline captured before anything is installed, and a reference that can be visited is worth more than one that can only be telephoned.

**Indicators.** (i) _Travel time from Sant Just Desvern by road or commuter rail_ — not kilometres, because the Barcelona metropolitan geography makes thirty kilometres either twenty-five minutes or seventy. (ii) _Number of simultaneous sites and their spread_: four to twenty jobs inside the metropolitan area is the origin; two jobs four hundred kilometres apart is distance. (iii) _Crew mobility_: do crews return to a yard, or go direct to site? Direct-to-site crews make the mobile surface valuable and make training harder to schedule. (iv) _Connectivity on site_: the repository records offline recovery in the native shell, and basement and structural-work sites are where that is tested. (v) _Office reality_: is there a physical office with an administrative desk, which is where the back-office training happens?

| Score | Anchor                                                                                                                                                              |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 10    | Within 30 minutes: Barcelonès, Baix Llobregat, and the near Vallès; an office with an administrative desk; 4–20 simultaneous sites all inside the metropolitan area |
| 8     | 30–60 minutes: rest of Vallès Occidental and Oriental, Maresme, Garraf, Alt Penedès                                                                                 |
| 6     | 60–120 minutes: Girona, Tarragona, Central Catalonia; one visit per quarter is realistic                                                                            |
| 4     | Rest of Catalonia, or a firm with sites spread across more than two provinces; no fixed administrative desk                                                         |
| 2     | Outside Catalonia inside Spain; onboarding and pilot measurement fully remote                                                                                       |
| 0     | Sites outside Spain, or an itinerant operation with no office and no stable crew base                                                                               |

### Economic

**What it measures:** the distance between the reference firm's economics and the prospect's — revenue band, project-size distribution, margin structure, payment terms and working-capital stress. This dimension carries the willingness-and-ability to pay, and it is also where the product's value is most directly proportional to the pain.

**Indicators.** (i) _Revenue band_ against the €1.0m–€8.0m target, derived in Phase 1 from sector turnover per employed person of about €134,000 and widened at the top because reformas contractors subcontract heavily. (ii) _Project-size distribution_: the ideal is a portfolio of €20k–€400k jobs with a tail of small ones; a single job representing more than forty per cent of annual revenue is concentration risk that changes the buying conversation from process to survival. (iii) _Margin structure and whether it is known_: the firm that cannot answer the margin question until the gestoría closes the year is the market; the firm that already produces weekly job-cost variance is not. (iv) _Payment terms and working-capital stress_: retentions, long customer terms against shorter supplier terms, and a receivables aging that nobody owns. The receivables capability ships configurable aging buckets, and the absence of a single view of expected collections against supplier commitments was pain point seven in the reference customer's own document. (v) _Ability to pay, anchored publicly_: the Kit Digital ceilings are the one public number that says what a Spanish SME of each size has been given permission to spend on software — €6,000 for three-to-nine employees, €12,000 for ten-to-forty-nine — and Phase 1's sizing assumed €3,600 and €6,000 of annual recurring value inside those ceilings. (vi) _Filed accounts present and recent_: a firm that files is a firm that can be scored; a firm whose last filed accounts are four years old is a credit question before it is a fit question.

| Score | Anchor                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 10    | €1.5m–€6m revenue, 6–20 simultaneous jobs of €20k–€400k, no job above 25% of revenue, margin unknown in-flight and acknowledged as a problem, retentions and mismatched payment terms causing visible working-capital stress, accounts filed within 18 months |
| 8     | €1.0m–€8.0m, 4–20 jobs, margin known only at period end, terms manageable but unmonitored                                                                                                                                                                     |
| 6     | €0.6m–€1.0m or €8m–€12m, 3–6 jobs, or a project mix with one dominant job between 25% and 40% of revenue                                                                                                                                                      |
| 4     | Under €0.6m or over €12m; or a margin discipline already in place with weekly variance reporting, which removes the product's central promise                                                                                                                 |
| 2     | Loss-making two years running, or a single job above 40% of revenue, or payment behaviour suggesting distress: the firm needs cash, not a system                                                                                                              |
| 0     | Insolvency proceedings, no filed accounts for three or more years, or a revenue base below the floor at which any human onboarding can be served                                                                                                              |

**Note on direction of error.** High economic distance can mean _too small_ or _too large and too well run_, and the two have opposite sales consequences. Phase 5 must record which, because Phase 11 is already scheduled to attack the ICP on precisely this point: that the firms which fit best may be the ones least willing to pay, because they are well run and already coping. The anchor at 4 encodes that attack explicitly — a firm with weekly job-cost variance scores _low_ on fit, not high.

## PART C — THE LAYER-DISTANCE OVERLAY

This is the dimension that governs unit economics, and it is the one where the instrument must be mechanical rather than judgemental. The layer a prospect forces us into is not an opinion about how unusual the firm is; it is a property of the composed configuration schema, and it can be decided by reading the schema. So the first task is to state what the schema actually exposes.

### C.0 What L0 is, enumerated from the code rather than assumed

The resolver composes a validator from three sources — the kernel's own fragment, one fragment per selected capability mounted at `config.<capability-id>`, and one fragment per selected pack mounted at `config.jurisdiction` and `config.vertical` — and then parses the tenant file against it with a **strict** object schema. Strictness is what makes the boundary mechanical: a key that no fragment declares is not ignored, it fails resolution. **Therefore L0 is exactly the set of requirements expressible in the keys below, and the test for "is this prospect L0?" is "can the requirement be written as a value of one of these keys without a code change?"**

**Spec-level keys** (`tenants/<id>/tenant.yaml`, validated by the kernel's tenant-spec schema): `tenant` (lowercase id), `kernel` (range, packs pin a caret-major), `capabilities` (non-empty array of installed capability ids), `jurisdiction` (pack reference with an optional effective date after an `@`), `vertical` (pack reference), `plugins` (array of strings), `config` (record).

**Kernel config keys** (present for every tenant regardless of packs): `config.locale`, `config.currency`, and `config.branding` with `legalName`, optional `tradeName`, optional `slogan`, optional `contact` (`address`, `phone`, `email`, `web`), optional `palette` as a token-to-value map, optional `typography` (`display`, `body`) and optional `logo` (`wordmark`, `white`, `mark`).

**Capability config fragments actually declared** — sixteen of the eighteen installed capabilities declare one, and two do not:

**Pack config fragments:** `config.jurisdiction.verifactu.enabled` for the Spanish pack — a boolean that is hard-gated to fail resolution when true, because the certified mode is not implemented or legally verified; and for the construction-renovation pack, `config.vertical` with `terminology` (`quote`, `line`, `measurement`), `terminologyByLang` (the same three words per UI language, shipping English, Spanish and Catalan), `chapterLabels` (per-language display labels keyed by the canonical chapter value), `defaultUnits`, `materialsShareDefaultBp`, and `chapters` — the eighteen-entry default renovation chapter catalogue, overridable per tenant as an ordinary array.

**Group-level keys**, one file per owner group (`tenants/_groups/<id>.yaml`): `group`, `displayName`, `owners` (role entries), `members` (tenant ids), plus the bookkeeping fields the reference group carries for pending validations and current tools. A group is data over tenants; each legal entity remains its own tenant with its own numbering, branding and isolation partition.

What the template itself exposes is narrower than what the schema permits, and the gap is worth noting because the template is what a new tenant starts from: the template file carries the six-capability baseline (`quoting`, `billing`, `scheduling`, `time`, `procurement`, `docs`), the Spanish jurisdiction pack at an effective date, the renovation vertical, an empty `plugins` array, locale and currency, a branding block with a legal-name placeholder token, a billing seller with a placeholder tax identifier and the two standard series, the certified-billing gate set to false, and an empty `config.vertical`. The reference tenant's own file is the same shape with sixteen capabilities, a full branding and palette block, four message templates, three roles with explicit grant lists, five catalogue items and one room template.

**Assumption: this enumeration is the L0 boundary as of today's commit, and it will widen**, because every capability that currently declares an empty config fragment is a place where a future tenant difference becomes data rather than code. Phase 7 is asked to inventory exactly this surface and state it precisely; this section is that inventory's first draft, and the two documents must be kept in agreement.

### C.1 L0 — tenant config only. **This is the target.**

**Definition.** Every requirement the prospect brings can be expressed as a value of a key enumerated in §C.0, using the capability set and the two existing packs. Marginal cost approaches the cost of the intake conversation itself. **Test:** write the prospect's differences as a diff against `tenants/_template/tenant.yaml`; if the diff is entirely values and the resolver accepts it, the prospect is L0.

**What this covers in practice, and it is more than it sounds:** company identity, brand colours, typography and logos; invoice series and numbering padding; the full price catalogue and room templates; role names and their permission grants; pipeline stages; aging buckets; margin-floor alerting; default hourly rate; document-extraction review thresholds; bank-matching tolerances; the message template set with its delays and channels; the chapter catalogue and its per-language labels; the three core domain words in three languages; default units; and the contractor-materials share. A renovation contractor in the province of Barcelona with a different chapter order, different trade words, its own colours, its own catalogue and a different role split is **L0** — the second and third tenant files in the repository are the evidence, and one of them was created configuration-only in hundredths of a second of machine time.

**What looks L0 and is not** — the list that matters most, because each of these is where an analyst will over-score:

- A **second bank's statement layout**. The importer reads one fixed layout found by its column names, with no column-mapping step, by explicit operator decision, and the cost was stated in advance: a second bank means a second profile. There is no configuration key for this. See §C.2.
- **Custom fields** on any core object. Forbidden, and the forbiddance is the product decision, not an omission.
- A **different accountant export format**. The hand-off adapter is unbound pending the answer, so the first prospect whose gestoría wants a specific format pays for an adapter.
- **Statutory subcontracting registers as named objects**. Today they are generic dated documents; named objects are bridge work.
- **True outbound email**. There is no SMTP anywhere in the codebase; messages are filed as drafts by IMAP append and a human presses send. The port exists, so this is credentials plus an adapter — not a key.
- A **sector whose chapter list differs but whose measurement and valuation semantics do not**. This one is the happy inverse: it looks like pack work and it is genuinely L0, because `chapters` is an array in the tenant file.

### C.2 L1 — plugin (additive, isolated)

**Definition.** The requirement is additive, sits outside every existing capability's contract, and touches nothing else: an extra document type, an extra report, an integration with a third-party service behind a port, a screen that reads existing data and writes nothing.

**And here is the honest finding this phase is obliged to report.** The spec schema accepts a `plugins` array of strings, and a repository-wide search for that key outside the schema returns nothing: no loader, no registry, no sandbox, no contract test, no expiry mechanism. The architectural decision that names the versioned plugin API as the escape hatch for real needs describes an intended mechanism, not a shipped one. **Assumption, and it is load-bearing for the rubric's weights: until a plugin loader exists, any requirement classified L1 must be priced and scheduled as if it were L2 — bespoke, additive code in a package, with its own tests, reviewed and released like any other package.** The consequence for scoring is that the L1 anchor sits at 6 rather than at 8, and the consequence for the roadmap is that building the loader is a candidate Tier C investment in its own right, because it converts a class of prospects from "cheap in principle" to "cheap in fact". Phase 7 should cost it.

### C.3 L2 — new or extended vertical pack

**Definition.** The prospect's _sector_ is not modelled: its work is not chaptered in a way the renovation taxonomy can express, or its measurement and valuation semantics differ, or it needs domain objects the vertical layer does not have. Examples from the adjacency map: a recurring-contract and work-order object for service-led installers; plant and availability for a hire business; per-installation asset records and commissioning files for solar retrofit; plant material and seasonal cycles for landscaping.

**Cost character.** Reusable across every future tenant in that sector, which is what makes it an investment rather than a project — but only if the sector has enough firms to amortise it. The adjacency map is explicit that landscaping fails this test on population size alone. Phase 5's Tier C rule is the right one: quantify the unlock by counting how many Tier A and B prospects the pack converts to L0.

### C.4 L3 — new or extended jurisdiction pack

**Definition.** A tax, invoicing, filing or registry regime we do not hold. Three concrete forms: a second country; public-sector electronic invoicing, tender files and classification registers; and near-continuous invoice-level reporting to the tax authority above the filing threshold. A fourth form is the cross-cutting case — law that is genuinely the product of one jurisdiction _and_ one sector, such as the Spanish construction subcontracting and employer registers — and the architecture's answer is neither a combined pack nor a fork but a bridge: the vertical pack declares a port with a published contract type, the jurisdiction pack ships an optional sub-module implementing it, and the resolver activates it only when both packs are selected. That keeps the cost of a genuine jurisdiction-by-sector law at one isolated bridge module, which is irreducible because the law exists.

**Cost character.** The most valuable investment layer, because the asset that makes a second jurisdiction cheap is the separation already proven from both sides: a linter that fails on jurisdiction vocabulary inside capability code and comments, and a negative test proving that the kernel plus billing with no jurisdiction pack fails loudly. It is also the layer most easily mistimed — selling a second jurisdiction before the second Spanish tenant converts an asset into a project.

### C.5 L4 — new capability

**Definition.** A port that does not exist: recurring contracts with service levels, an asset register, a hire calendar, a scheme-capitalisation model, a sales pipeline of units, a statement-import abstraction covering arbitrary bank layouts. **Price it as investment, not as project cost** — the roadmap's instruction — and therefore require of every L4 case a named count of future tenants it serves, exactly as Tier C demands.

**One L4 hides inside the current product and should be named here rather than discovered in Phase 5**: a real `bank-statements` port with per-bank profiles. It is anticipated in the pending-integrations register, it is the difference between "a second bank is a configuration change" and "a second bank is an engineering week", and every prospect in the ICP has a bank. **Assumption: this is the highest-value L4 on the board, because unlike every other candidate it is forced by the core segment rather than by an adjacency.**

### C.6 L5 — kernel change. **A prospect requiring this is a prospect we decline, and we say so.**

**Definition.** The requirement can only be met by changing money math or rounding, breaking invoice immutability or bypassing the rectification path, overriding a tax decision, skipping invoice numbers, making the audit trail optional, giving a tenant its own schema shape, or changing effective-dating semantics. These are named as never configurable for any customer, with the stated consequence that some prospects will walk because they were buying a fork.

**The decline must be explicit and recorded.** Phase 5's Tier D is instructed to say why, plainly, and to include any L5. The commercial reason to hold this line is not purity: it is that the asset being sold is the separation, and one fork destroys the claim that a tenant is a data change. The sales sentence is short — _the rates, the series, the catalogue, the words and the roles are yours to set; the arithmetic, the numbering and the audit trail are not, and that is why your accountant can trust the output._

### C.7 Deciding the layer from outside the firm: the decision tree

Applied in order; the first rule that fires sets the layer. Each rule names the evidence that triggers it, so two analysts reading the same Phase 4 row reach the same layer.

1. Does any requirement touch money math, invoice immutability, tax-decision override, numbering gaplessness, the audit trail, per-tenant schema shape, or effective-dating? → **L5, decline.**
2. Does the firm invoice from outside Spain, or need public-sector invoicing, tender files, classification registers, or above-threshold continuous reporting? → **L3.**
3. Does the firm need a port that does not exist — recurring contracts with service levels, an asset or plant register, a hire calendar, scheme capitalisation, a unit sales pipeline, or a bank layout other than the one supported? → **L4.**
4. Is the firm's sector not expressible in the renovation chapter and measurement model, or does it need domain objects the vertical layer lacks? → **L2.**
5. Does the firm need statutory subcontracting or employer registers as named first-class objects, demanded by its clients or coordinators? → **L3 bridge module** (scored as L3).
6. Is the remaining gap additive and isolated — one extra document, one extra report, one third-party integration behind an existing port? → **L1**, priced as L2 until a plugin loader exists.
7. Otherwise → **L0.** Confirm by writing the diff against the template and checking that every line is a value, not a key.

**Reconciliation rule.** If two analysts reach different layers for the same firm, the firm is routed to reconciliation _before_ its total is computed, and the lower layer is not adopted by default — the disagreement is resolved by naming the requirement that drives it, which is the field Phase 5 is already required to output. This rule is what keeps two analysts inside ten points, and §"Agreement budget" below shows the arithmetic.

### R.4 The agreement budget — how two analysts stay within ten points

The divergence cost of one anchor step (two score points) is 8.0 points on layer distance, 3.2 on each of administrative, economic and cultural, and 2.4 on geographic. Two analysts therefore stay inside ten points if and only if they agree on the layer and diverge by at most one anchor step on no more than three of the four CAGE dimensions (maximum 9.6). Three mechanisms enforce that:

1. **The layer is not scored, it is derived.** The §C.7 decision tree is ordered and evidence-triggered, and a disagreement on layer routes the firm to reconciliation before the total is computed rather than being averaged away. This removes the only divergence large enough to breach the bound on its own.
2. **Unknowns score 5 and carry a flag.** Two analysts who both lack the evidence land on the same number, and the flag — not the score — is what gets worked.
3. **No score above 8 without a named artefact.** The top anchor is reserved for proven facts, which removes the most common source of a two-step spread: one analyst's optimism against another's caution on the same thin evidence.

A fourth, cheap practice: score in pairs for the first thirty firms, measure the dispersion, and if it exceeds ten points on more than one firm in ten, the anchors are wrong and should be rewritten before the list grows. That is the same discipline Phase 1 proposed for testing the sweet spot's bounds, applied to the instrument instead of the market.
