# 04a — SOURCE PLAN FOR THE BARCELONA TARGET LIST

This document is the sourcing plan for Phase 4 of the Barcelona market roadmap, written on 2026-09-12. It names every source from which the prospect list for tenant #2 onwards can be built, states for each one what it yields, how it is accessed, what it costs, how often it changes, the legal basis on which we may process what it contains, and which field of the Phase 4b extraction schema or which trigger from `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` it feeds; it records, source by source, the result of an actual reachability probe run from the execution environment on the date above, because the extraction agent that follows can only work what is reachable from here and the operator needs to know what the gated and paid sources would add; and it fixes the data-protection posture and the extraction order that 4b must follow. It does not contain any firm names: naming firms is 4b's job, auditing them is 4c's, and scoring them is Phase 5's.

---

## 1. What the reachability probe found, and why it dominates this plan

Two probes were run against every source named below. First a direct transport probe (`curl` over the session's egress proxy, and `WebFetch` where a URL was known). Second a search probe, with domain restriction where the host was known, to establish whether the source's content is at least indexed and therefore partially legible through search result titles, URLs and snippets.

The transport result is uniform and it is the single most important finding in this document: **every external host tested answered `403` to the proxy's `CONNECT`, including hosts that are plainly innocuous.** The egress policy for this session is an allowlist containing, as far as could be determined, package registries and code hosting only. `registry.npmjs.org` returned `200`; `github.com` completed a tunnel; `example.com`, `es.wikipedia.org` and `docs.anthropic.com` were all refused. The proxy's own status endpoint records the refusals as `connect_rejected · gateway answered 403 to CONNECT (policy denial or upstream failure)` and the README is explicit that such denials must be reported rather than routed around. The two hosts already known to be blocked — `boe.es` and `sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es` — are blocked for the same reason as everything else, not as a special case.

The operative consequence, which the Extraction order section below is built around, is this: **from this environment there is exactly one working channel to the outside world, the web search tool, and no ability to fetch, parse, download, or call an API.** No CKAN query, no ATOM feed, no CSV download, no Places API call, no scraping of a directory page. Phase 4b can therefore produce a list whose evidential backbone is search-surfaced content — result titles, canonical URLs, and the search tool's own reading of pages it can reach on our behalf — with `source_urls[]` recording the page each field came from even where that page could not be opened locally. That is weaker than a CSV, and the honest position is that several schema fields will be `null` for most rows, which is what the schema's "never guess" instruction is for and what 4c's error-rate measurement will quantify. Where a field is structurally unobtainable through search, this plan says so rather than leaving 4b to discover it one firm at a time.

A note on the Barcelona permit dataset specifically, because the roadmap singles it out as "the single best signal of who is actively building, right now, at what value". The Open Data BCN catalogue was searched with domain restriction for `llicències d'obres`, `obres majors`, and for works datasets in CSV. **No dataset of granted works licences naming the contractor was found in the Open Data BCN catalogue.** What exists instead, and what was found, is three different things: `https://opendata-ajuntament.barcelona.cat/data/ca/dataset` (the 475-to-555 dataset catalogue, CC-BY, searchable but not fetchable from here); a related works dataset, `Obres a l'espai públic de la ciutat de Barcelona`, catalogued at `https://datos.gob.es/en/catalogo/l01080193-obras-en-el-espacio-publico-de-la-ciudad-de-barcelona`, which is georeferenced road and public-space works rather than private building permits; and aggregate statistics on major-works licences on the statistics portal, for example `https://portaldades.ajuntament.barcelona.cat/statistics/9m1bjouhcq` (new-build surface area in major-works licences by use) and `https://portaldades.ajuntament.barcelona.cat/estadístiques/hteaouewjv` (number of works files applied for by licence type), which are counts and square metres with no firm names in them at all. Per-address permit detail in Barcelona is served as a paid, identity-authenticated administrative product, the `Informe d'obres majors`, at `https://seuelectronica.ajuntament.barcelona.cat/oficinavirtual/ca/tramit/20050000116`, which requires a digital identification method. So the best available answer to "was the Barcelona permit open dataset found" is: **a catalogue and aggregate permit statistics were found; a per-permit open dataset naming the contractor was not.** The strongest open-permit lead found anywhere in the probe is not Barcelona at all but the Consorci AOC municipal open-data platform, where individual municipalities publish `llicències urbanístiques` datasets with a uniform CKAN identifier pattern — the worked example surfaced was `https://dadesobertes.seu-e.cat/dataset/matadepera-llicencies-urbanistiques`, described as a list of applied-for private works licences that generated a licence file, CSV, created 2020-02-20 and last updated 2020-05-21, with an API at `http://dadesobertes.seu-e.cat/api/aoc/action/package_show?id=matadepera-llicencies-urbanistiques`. That pattern is the thing to exploit when transport is available, and the 2020 staleness of the example is the warning that accompanies it.

## 3. Trigger-signal coverage check

Every detectable signal in the "Detectable from outside by" column of `03-TRIGGER-MAP.md` maps to at least one row above. Stated explicitly, trigger by trigger, with the governing source and the honest reachability caveat:

1. **Failed inspection or sanction** — sanction publications under RD 597/2007, labour-authority and DOGC publications, local press, and a prevention-service logo appearing on the firm's own site. All search-only from here.
2. **Margin lost on a large job** — filed accounts via the Registro Mercantil, eInforma, Empresite and (paid) SABI; one unusually large permit in an otherwise small portfolio via the municipal permit layer; supplier-side payment delay via Axesor or Informa reports. Accounts are the reachable part; payment behaviour is paid.
3. **Generational handover** — BORME officer and power-of-attorney changes, corroborated only by the firm's own website. LinkedIn may be read as a signal that a handover is happening but supplies no field.
4. **Incumbent software renewal date** — job adverts naming a product, vendor or partner badges on the firm's site, and the generator fingerprint of publicly posted quotation or invoice documents. Feeds `known_software[]`.
5. **Change of gestoría** — the deposit presenter or filing agent on the most recent accounts deposit (Registro Mercantil, paid per filing), plus a new advisory logo on the site. The cheapest reliable version of this is a qualifying question, as Phase 3 says.
6. **Rapid headcount growth** — employee bands over time from eInforma, Informa or SABI; volume and recency of job adverts; count and value of municipal permits; yard and fleet visible in street-level imagery.
7. **A new large general-contractor client** — the firm's own portfolio and posts, contractor and development press, and permit records naming the principal undertaking and its subcontractors.
8. **Working-capital stress** — average payment period and late-payment disclosure in the filed accounts, commercial credit reports and payment-behaviour scores, default-file appearances, and late or abbreviated filings. Partly free, mostly paid.
9. **Loss of the key administrative person** — job adverts for administrative, accounting or site-administration roles; a changed contact name on the firm's site.
10. **New premises, yard or second entity** — BORME incorporations and registered-office changes, a second address in the firm's own listings, and municipal activity-licence registers published through the AOC platform.
11. **Acquisition or absorption of a competitor** — BORME merger and absorption notices, a discontinuity in filed revenue and headcount, press and guild announcements.
12. **Certification or membership requiring documented process** — certification-body registries, certification marks on the site, and the membership listings of the guilds and of CAATEEB.

The firmographic filters of `02-ICP-AND-RUBRIC.md` §A.1 are covered as follows. Legal form, age, group structure and officers come from BORME and the free company pages. Employees and revenue come from the free company pages and Empresite rankings, with SABI as the paid upgrade and the caveat that free pages often publish a band or a stale year rather than a figure. CNAE comes from the company pages and from the registered object in BORME. Geography comes from the registered office plus the municipal and marketplace listings. Job count — the variable the employee band is only a proxy for — comes from permits where the permit layer is usable and otherwise remains `null` until the qualifying call. Of the firmographic filters, the one that is genuinely weak from here is **revenue band with a current year attached**; that weakness is the strongest single argument for the paid tier in section 6.

## 4. What this plan deliberately does not do

It does not propose to contact any firm: Phase 4 builds a list, Phase 8 and 9 design the approach, Phase 10 gates the pilot. It does not propose to collect anything about site workers, site managers or family members as individuals. It does not propose to buy a marketing list from a list broker, because a bought list arrives without provenance and the whole point of `source_urls[]` is that every field can be re-derived by 4c. It does not propose to scrape marketplaces or Google in volume: both restrict bulk re-use in their terms, and in any case the transport to do it does not exist from here.

## 5. GDPR and LOPDGDD posture

This is a standing constraint set by the operator. It is not a recommendation and 4b may not relax it.

**Company-level data only.** The unit of record is the firm. Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR) does not apply to information about legal persons, so the great majority of the extraction schema — legal name, NIF, CNAE, municipality, founded year, employee band, revenue band, contract counts, permit counts, review counts — is outside the material scope of data-protection law altogether. The schema nevertheless contains fields that can become personal data about identifiable natural persons, and those fields, not the rest, are what this posture governs.

**`directors[]` only from public registry sources.** Officer names may be taken from BORME and from registry-derived company pages and from nowhere else. They may not be taken from LinkedIn, from any other social profile, from press interviews, or from a firm's "about us" page where it names family members in a non-registry capacity. The reason is provenance, not squeamishness: a name published in the Boletín Oficial del Registro Mercantil is published there by legal mandate in a defined role with a defined date, which is exactly the "public registry" character that makes processing it for B2B prospecting proportionate and verifiable by 4c; a name harvested from a social profile has no such character, and pulling it in would turn a company record into a people database.

**No personal emails or mobile numbers.** `email` and `phone` are the firm's published contact points — `info@`, `administracio@`, the switchboard, the published site number. A named individual's direct mobile number or personal mailbox is out of scope even where it is technically findable. Where the only published contact point is plainly personal, such as the sole director's own mobile on a one-person site, 4b sets the field to `null` rather than recording it.

**Legal basis: legitimate interest, art. 6.1.f GDPR,** for B2B prospecting on published business data, supported in Spanish law by LOPDGDD (Ley Orgánica 3/2018) art. 19, which presumes a legitimate interest in the processing of the contact data of individual entrepreneurs and of staff acting for a legal person where the processing relates solely to that professional role. Commercial electronic communication is separately governed by Ley 34/2002 (LSSI) art. 21, and the advertising-exclusion system of LOPDGDD art. 22 is to be honoured; outreach design belongs to Phase 9 but the constraint is recorded here because the list is what makes outreach possible.

**The balancing test, written out.** _Purpose:_ to identify firms whose observable characteristics match a defined ideal-customer profile, so that a small number of them can be approached once with a relevant commercial proposition. _Necessity:_ the identification cannot be done without processing firm-level data, and the officer-name field is necessary only to know who holds legal authority to engage — it is the difference between a letter to a company and a letter to the person entitled to answer it. _Effect on the individual:_ the data processed is already published by legal mandate, relates exclusively to a professional role, and is used for a single low-frequency business communication; there is no profiling of the individual, no automated decision about them, no enrichment from their private life, no inference about health, politics, beliefs or anything else in art. 9, and no sharing with third parties. _Reasonable expectations:_ a director whose appointment is published in an official register, whose firm publishes a business number and a portfolio, and whose trade is won by responding to enquiries, can reasonably expect business approaches addressed to the firm. _Safeguards:_ registry-only provenance for names, no personal contact points, company-level storage, a short retention clock, a documented objection route, and 4c's audit of every field against its cited source. _Conclusion:_ the interest is legitimate, the processing is necessary and proportionate to it, and the impact on the individual is slight and expected. The balance favours processing. If any individual objects, the balance is treated as settled against processing for that record, immediately and without argument, because art. 21.2 gives an unconditional right to object to processing for direct-marketing purposes.

**Right to object, and how it is exercised.** A single named mailbox — `privacitat@` at the operator's own domain, to be created before the first outreach of Phase 9 and named in the privacy notice — receives objections, access, rectification and erasure requests. The first written communication to any prospect carries the art. 14 information notice (identity of the controller, purposes, legitimate-interest basis, the source of the data, the retention period, and the rights including the right to object and to complain to the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos). An objection is actioned within 30 days and always within the one-month art. 12.3 limit: the record is removed from the active list and reduced to a minimal suppression entry so that the same firm is not re-sourced by a later run.

**Retention position for the prospect list.** Four clocks, all measured from the date a row was last verified or last touched: (a) a candidate row never contacted — 12 months, then delete or re-derive; a row whose sources are older than that is not a prospect record, it is stale data pretending to be one. (b) A contacted but unconverted prospect — 24 months from the last contact, then delete. (c) A row rejected as out of the ICP — retained as a minimal suppression entry (legal name, NIF, municipality, decline reason, date) for 24 months so that later runs do not rediscover it, with all other fields deleted at the point of decline. (d) An objection or erasure request — all fields deleted within 30 days, leaving only the minimal suppression entry needed to honour the objection itself, which is retained on the art. 21 basis for as long as the list programme runs. `directors[]` is additionally subject to a shorter clock: it is deleted as soon as a row leaves the active pipeline for any reason, because its only purpose is to address a live approach. The list is held in the repository as `04-PROSPECTS.jsonl` with no copy outside it; the processing activity is recorded in the operator's register of processing activities under art. 30; and if a commercial data vendor is engaged under section 6, a processor contract under art. 28 is signed before the first query, not after.

## 6. Extraction order for 4b

4b works **only** the channel that the probe found open — the web search tool — and it works these sources in this order. Target for the whole pass: **80 to 150 firms** inside the ICP's band across the province of Barcelona, processed in batches of 50 as the roadmap requires, one JSON object per firm, `null` wherever a field cannot be sourced, `source_urls[]` always populated.

**Step 1 — Marketplace discovery, municipality by municipality (aim 55–75 firms).** Domain-restricted searches against `habitissimo.es`, `houzz.es` and `cronoshare.com`. Query patterns: `empresas de reformas <municipi>`, `empresa reformas integrales <municipi> opiniones`, `constructora <municipi> reformas`, and the Catalan equivalents `empresa de reformes <municipi>`. Municipalities, with per-municipality targets: Barcelona city 25–35; L'Hospitalet de Llobregat 8–12; Badalona 8–10; Sabadell 8–10; Terrassa 8–10; Mataró 6–8; Sant Cugat del Vallès 5–8; the Baix Llobregat belt nearest the reference tenant — Sant Just Desvern, Esplugues, Cornellà, Sant Boi, Sant Feliu, Molins de Rei, Viladecans, Gavà, Castelldefels, Martorell — 15–20 between them; Granollers and Mollet del Vallès 6–8; the Maresme beyond Mataró — Premià de Mar, Vilassar, Arenys de Mar — 4–6. Fields harvested here: `trade_name`, `municipality`, `province`, `website`, `review_count`, `review_avg`, `review_recency_months`.

**Step 2 — Membership lists (aim 20–30 firms, overlapping step 1).** Domain-restricted searches against `gremi-obres.org`, `gremibcn.cat`, `gremimet.cat` and the comarcal guilds linked from the first. Query patterns: `empreses agremiades <comarca>`, `associats constructors <municipi>`, `membre gremi constructors <municipi>`. Also search `ccoc.cat` — **but treat every CCOC member as a candidate for exclusion, not inclusion**, since its members average 80% by volume of Catalan public works and ICP hard gate 3 declines material public-sector revenue. Fields: membership flag for Phase 5, plus `legal_name` where the listing gives it.

**Step 3 — Identity resolution and firmographics (every row).** For each candidate, domain-restricted searches against `einforma.com`, `infocif.es`, `empresite.eleconomista.es`, `ranking-empresas.eleconomista.es` and `axesor.es` using the trade name plus municipality, then the legal name once found. Query patterns: `"<trade name>" S.L. <municipi> CNAE`, `"<legal name>" empleados facturación`, `ranking empresas <CNAE> Barcelona`. Fields: `legal_name`, `nif`, `cnae`, `founded_year`, `employees_band`, `revenue_band_eur`, `revenue_year`. Drop any row whose CNAE lands outside the ICP's in and acceptable sets, and any row that is an autónomo, a comunidad de bienes or a branch of a foreign parent.

**Step 4 — Registry layer for `directors[]` and corporate events.** Domain-restricted searches against `boe.es/diario_borme` and `openmercantil.es/borme/barcelona`. Query patterns: `BORME "<legal name>" nombramientos`, `BORME "<legal name>" constitución`, `BORME Barcelona "<legal name>" cambio domicilio`. This is the **only** permitted source for `directors[]`. Capture officer appointments and removals, incorporations, registered-office changes and any merger or absorption, because three of the twelve non-regulatory triggers read directly off them.

**Step 5 — Public-contract screen (exclusion).** Domain-restricted searches against `contrataciondelestado.es`, `contractaciopublica.gencat.cat` and `contractes.cat`. Query pattern: `"<legal name>" adjudicación` / `"<legal name>" adjudicatari licitació`. Set `public_contracts_count`; flag any firm with a visible pattern of public awards for the hard-gate review rather than silently dropping it.

**Step 6 — Permit layer (attempt, and record the failure honestly).** Domain-restricted searches against `dadesobertes.seu-e.cat`, `dadesobertes.diba.cat`, `opendata.terrassa.cat`, `opendata.l-h.cat` and `opendata-ajuntament.barcelona.cat`, using the CKAN identifier pattern `<municipi>-llicencies-urbanistiques` and the queries `llicències urbanístiques <municipi> dades obertes csv` and `llicències d'obres majors <municipi> dataset`. Expect this step to yield datasets, not rows: without transport there is no way to read a CSV, and the one worked example found was last updated in 2020. Unless a municipality's search-visible data names contractors, `permits_last_24m` and `permit_value_total_eur` stay `null` for that row. Do not infer a permit count from anything else.

**Step 7 — Contact points and estate.** Searches for the firm's own site: `"<legal name>" contacto presupuesto`, `"<trade name>" <municipi> telèfon`, plus `"<legal name>" empleo administrativo` and `"<trade name>" oferta trabajo obra` against `infojobs.net` and `indeed.com` for the headcount and software signals. Fields: `email` and `phone` as **published firm contact points only**, `known_software[]` from any product named in a job advert or badge.

**Step 8 — Close the batch.** Before emitting each batch of 50: confirm every row has at least one `source_urls[]` entry per non-null field; confirm `directors[]` is either `null` or registry-sourced; confirm no personal mobile or personal mailbox has entered `email` or `phone`; confirm the municipality is inside the province. Then emit JSONL only, no commentary, and leave the error-rate measurement to 4c.

If the pass lands below 80 firms, the first place to widen is step 1 with a second vocabulary — `rehabilitació d'edificis`, `reformes integrals`, `interiorisme i obra`, `constructora` — across the same municipality list, and the second is step 2 with the comarcal guilds individually. Widening by lowering the CNAE filter is not permitted: an unfiltered 43.9 extraction is exactly how the scaffolding-and-plant-hire type described in §A.6 arrives at the top of the list.

## 7. What paid sources would add

**SABI (Moody's, formerly Bureau van Dijk), from roughly €250 per month for the Iberian coverage.** SABI is the one purchase that changes the shape of the work rather than topping it up. It covers about 2.9 million Spanish firms with normalised accounts, ratios, employee series, officers, group links and — the part that matters most here — segmentation and export. With SABI, steps 1 to 3 above invert: instead of discovering firms on marketplaces and then trying to resolve them to a legal entity, the analyst writes the ICP's §A.1 filter directly as a query — CNAE 43.31/43.32/43.33/43.34/43.39/43.99 plus renovation-led 41.2, employees 8 to 40, revenue €1.0m to €8.0m, S.L. or S.L.U., registered office in Barcelonès, Baix Llobregat, Vallès Occidental, Vallès Oriental or Maresme, age 6 to 30 years — and exports the population. That is a complete frame rather than a sample, it carries `revenue_year` honestly, and its employee series turns the "rapid headcount growth" trigger from an inference into a measurement. It also makes the Phase 1 sweet-spot band testable: the thirty firms either side of each bound that §A.1 says would settle the question cheaply are a filter change, not a new research project. Note the licensing reality: institutional SABI licences are procured through public tenders and public bodies in Spain do so routinely, so list price is a starting point and the relevant question for a one-analyst programme is whether a single-seat commercial licence can be had at the low end.

**Informa D&B, subscription.** Informa is SABI's data partner for Spain and the natural alternative if the financial-analysis surface is not wanted. It adds payment behaviour and default-file exposure, which is the only reliable read on the "working-capital stress" trigger, and employee bands over time. For a programme that has already bought SABI, Informa's marginal value is mostly the payment-behaviour layer.

**eInforma, reports from roughly €10 to €15 each with package discounts advertised at above 60% off list.** eInforma is the cheapest way to buy certainty about a specific row rather than a population. The sensible use is not a subscription but a budget: a hundred reports at the low end is roughly €1,000 to €1,500 and would put a verified NIF, CNAE, incorporation date, employee band and last filed revenue behind every firm on a 100-row list — which is precisely the set of fields 4c is most likely to find unreliable when they are sourced from free pages. If only one paid item is bought before 4c runs, buy this one.

**Axesor, per-report with documented 50% and 100% surcharges for urgent and same-session delivery.** Axesor's distinguishing content is default and payment data — coverage of above 95% of Spanish defaulters, public-administration debt, and the main national default files. That is the "working-capital stress" trigger and, read the other way, a credit screen before any pilot with deferred payment terms. Credit-information processing carries its own regime under LOPDGDD art. 20 and should be used for our own credit decision, never quoted back to the prospect.

**LinkedIn Sales Navigator, Core at roughly €120.99 per month or about €1,089 per year, Advanced at roughly €148.21 per month.** Sales Navigator buys role-level search, employee-count trend and change alerts. It is useful for Phase 8 and 9 — knowing who holds the operations role and who holds the administrative role before the first call is worth real time — and it is useful for the "generational handover" and "loss of the key administrative person" triggers. It adds nothing to this list's structured fields and, under the posture in section 5, it may not be used to populate `directors[]` at all. Of the five paid items it is the most easily deferred until there is a list worth working.

A rough order of purchase, if the budget is sequential rather than simultaneous: eInforma report budget first (cheap, directly fixes the fields most at risk), SABI second (changes the method), Sales Navigator third (helps the approach, not the list), Axesor fourth (pre-pilot credit screen), Informa D&B only if SABI was declined.

## 8. Known gaps

Three gaps are worth naming so that Phase 5 does not read silence as absence of risk. First, the permit layer — the roadmap's best-signal source — is the weakest thing in this plan from this environment, and if `permits_last_24m` comes back overwhelmingly `null`, Phase 5 must score without it rather than score it as zero. Second, the legal references in section 5 to LOPDGDD articles 19, 20 and 22, to LSSI art. 21 and to GDPR arts. 6.1.f, 12, 14, 21, 28 and 30 are asserted from established knowledge of those instruments and were **not** re-verified against `boe.es` in this session, because that host is blocked; they should be re-read against the consolidated texts before the first outreach, in the same way Phase 3 verified its own instruments. Third, no source in this plan distinguishes a project-led installer from one with a maintenance book, which is the decline type §A.6 calls the most commonly blurred boundary in the whole map; that distinction is a revenue-mix question on the qualifying call, and no amount of sourcing will substitute for asking it.
