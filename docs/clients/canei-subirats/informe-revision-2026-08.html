1 · The contract on a phone fixed · 9223f62

What you saw. Text cut off, the document unreadable on the screen you actually carry to a site.

What was wrong. Every generated document is drawn as a 210 mm sheet — a sheet of A4, at A4’s width, whatever the screen. Our first reading of your screenshots was that the sheet was centred and its left edge unreachable. Measurement said otherwise: the left edge was exactly where it belonged. One cause — a width fixed in millimetres — failing two different ways depending on which panel the document opened in.

What changed. Below 700 px the sheet becomes fluid: one column, reduced margins, signature blocks stacked. Above it, nothing moves — the desktop keeps the approved design at its approved size. Printing and the downloaded PDF cannot be affected: print already forces its own width, and the file your customer receives is written by a different component that shares no styling with the screen at all.

✓ mobile: the whole contrato is on screen — 364px in a 390px viewport
✓ mobile: the whole adenda is on screen — 331px in a 390px viewport
✓ COM-04: the sheet keeps its A4 width on a desktop (794px)

2 · The Gantt on a phone fixed · 56e98d0

What you saw. The planning chart unusable on a phone.

What was wrong. Not the touch handling — the drag gestures were written for touch from the start. The chart spends 190 px on task names and leaves roughly 200 px for the timeline: about eight days of a job that runs twenty to sixty. There was nothing legible to aim a finger at.

What changed. Below 700 px the chart is replaced by a schedule read as a list — one row per task in date order with its dates, duration, progress, whether it is critical, and whether it is late or running today; the contract’s payment milestones sit in the same list, on their dates, because money falling due is one of the things that happens on a day. Tapping a row opens the same task panel the desktop opens, so the phone loses the dragging, not the editing. The desktop chart is untouched.

✓ mobile: the schedule reads as a list — 5 rows for 5 tasks, 360px in 390px
✓ mobile: every schedule row carries the schedule’s own dates — 5 rows checked
✓ gantt: the desktop keeps the chart, its name column and its tools

3 · Payment milestones fixed · 70efb17

What you said. “You can establish a milestone at 50 % but the payment can be 20 %” — and the old trigger, «Al llegar a una fase», never said which phase.

What changed. A milestone now names a percentage of the work: at 10 %…90 % of progress, in nine steps, alongside on signature, at works start, on completion and a fixed date. Trigger and amount stay independent — that was always true and is now visible — so a milestone at 50 % of progress releasing 20 % of the money is expressible and prints correctly in all three languages. The date is derived from the plan’s planned curve, never from recorded progress, so a payment date cannot move because somebody logged work.

✓ COM-04: the milestone trigger offers 10…90 % of progress, nine steps
✓ COM-04: a milestone at 50 % of progress can release 20 % of the money

4 · “8 % per year — what is this?” explained

The answer. It is a contract term, not a calculation. Every contract is drawn up carrying a late-payment interest rate — 8 % per year by default, from your company configuration — and prints it beside the clause saying interest accrues from the due date. Nothing in the system computes or charges it. No invoice, no ageing report and no reminder uses the figure.

Why we have not simply left it. A rate printed at a customer is a legal assertion. Ley 3/2004 sets the statutory rate for commercial transactions by reference to the ECB rate, published twice a year — so a fixed default is right on the day it is chosen and quietly wrong afterwards, and a consumer contract is a different regime again. It is now written up for your asesor with the open questions named, and marked unverified until they answer. No product change was made, because you did not ask for one.

5 · Exclusiones and Supuestos fixed · 120724d

What you saw. Both blocks visible on screen and missing from the downloaded PDF.

What was wrong. The quote is drawn twice by two independent components — one for the screen, one for the file. The screen renderer was taught about the two blocks; the PDF writer never was, and no check compared them.

What changed. The PDF writer prints both. More importantly, a check now feeds one document to both renderers and fails if any block reaches one and not the other — the two drifting apart is what happened, so that is what is pinned.

✓ «Supuestos» reaches both renderers — both
✓ «Exclusiones» reaches both renderers — both

6 · The progress chart did not move fixed · 120724d

What you saw. Progress recorded, the chart unchanged.

What was wrong, measured. The curve sampled forward from the plan’s start date and discarded every reading after today. Your plan started on 31 August; the day you looked was 29 August. So every sample sat in the future of your “today”, every actual value was discarded, and the chart drew zero of twenty points while the header above it correctly said 62.5 %. Confirmed independently from your screenshot: no green and no amber pixels in the chart, only the dashed planned line.

What changed. When today falls before the plan starts, the curve samples from today instead, and there is always a point at today. The header and the line are now required by test to agree — they may not disagree again without a check going red.

✓ tracking: S curve draws planned, actual and projected

7 · Adicionales — annex or menu? your call · 2e9d26d

What you asked. Whether the contract annex could be managed inside the contract rather than as its own menu entry. We designed the move, explained it in detail at your request — and you cancelled it to de-risk, asking instead that the screen stop leading with a project selector and five money counters, and look like Clientes and Proveedores.

What changed. Adicionales keeps its menu row and is now a register: one row per modification across every job, searchable by job, filterable by stage, with the whole lifecycle — price, send, approve, see the annex, apply to the plan — inside the row’s panel. Two things were protected deliberately: the amber marking on unapproved work, which exists to be readable from across a site office, and the fact that the unapproved total comes from the engine rather than a sum in the screen, so it cannot disagree with the rest of the system. The contract’s Anexos tab stays a read-only mirror for now — see page 5.

✓ PRY-03: opens as a register over every obra — no counters, no project bar (8 rows)
✓ PRY-03: the amber rule and the pill agree with the engine on every row
✓ modificaciones: the «sin aprobar» total rises when an extra is priced and falls when it is approved (0 → 50000 → 0 cents)

Two things worth saying plainly

The re-walk was run against the same files the site publishes, not against the live address.
The build machine used for this work cannot reach the published web address — the network it sits behind refuses it. So the seven items were re-checked against the site files exactly as they stand in the repository, which is byte-for-byte what the publishing job copies to the web. That is a strong check, but it is not the same as somebody opening the live URL, and we would rather say so than let the distinction pass.
Two of the seven had a plausible wrong diagnosis, caught by measuring.
The contract-on-a-phone fault looked, from the screenshots, like a centring problem that put the left edge of the page out of reach. It was not; measuring showed the left edge exactly where it belonged and a different cause underneath. The progress chart looked like a drawing bug and turned out to be a date-window bug that only appears when today falls before the plan starts. In both cases the fix that shipped is the one the measurement justified, not the one the screenshot suggested.
